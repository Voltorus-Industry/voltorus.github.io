Overview

Electricity generation in Israel is principally regulated by the Electricity Sector Law, 1996, together with regulations, standards, government decisions and decisions of the Electricity Authority.

The regulatory framework provides for licensing of electricity-sector activities, including electricity production.

Private electricity producers may therefore be required to obtain an electricity production licence depending on the nature and regulatory classification of the installation.

The Electricity Authority administers production licensing and establishes technical and regulatory conditions applicable to electricity-generation facilities.

The treatment of a purely self-consumption installation must be determined from the applicable regulations, standards, technology, capacity and whether the facility connects to or interacts with the public electricity system.

Maximum Electrical Power
General generation threshold:

No single MW threshold applicable to every generation project has been identified in the Electricity Sector Law.

Israel uses a licensing and regulatory classification system rather than a single national MW ceiling applicable to every electricity-generation installation.

Specific regulatory procedures can depend on technology, capacity, grid connection and the type of electricity activity.

The Electricity Authority has also adopted specific regulatory arrangements for certain smaller renewable facilities and technologies.

The applicable production licence should therefore be determined from the project's exact technology, capacity, connection configuration and intended electricity use.
