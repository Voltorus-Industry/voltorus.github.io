Overview

Electricity generation in Nepal is principally governed by the Electricity Act, 2049 (1992), the Electricity Regulation, 2050 (1993), and subsequent electricity-sector legislation and regulations.

Section 4 of the Electricity Act establishes the licensing framework for electricity production, transmission and distribution.

Rule 12 of the Electricity Regulation, 2050 provides the application procedure for a licence for production of electricity.

The regulatory framework also provides specific administrative arrangements for smaller electricity projects, including projects below 1 MW under the applicable local-government framework.

The exact licensing authority therefore depends on project capacity, technology, location and the applicable allocation of governmental powers.

Administrative Requirements

Rule 12 requires an applicant for a production licence to provide information concerning the proposed electricity project.

  • Applicant's name and address.
  • Electricity project name.
  • Method of electricity production.
  • Water-resource information where water is used.
  • Location and boundaries of the project.
  • Main structures and their locations.
  • Construction commencement and completion dates.
  • Environmental assessment and related documentation where required.

The licence itself identifies the generating project, generation method, relevant water resources, structures, location, validity period and other conditions.

Maximum Electrical Power
Nepal — no single national maximum MW figure applicable to every generation technology

The Electricity Act establishes a general licensing requirement rather than a universal maximum generating capacity.

Separate rules and governmental arrangements apply to smaller projects. The Nepal Renewable Energy Programme identifies a specific licensing framework for projects below 1 MW under the Local Government Operation Act framework.

Accordingly, capacity thresholds should be treated as thresholds determining the competent authority or licensing procedure rather than as universal generation ceilings.
