Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Bahrain and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the external electricity grid.

Electricity generation in Bahrain is principally regulated by Decree-Law No. (1) of 1996 concerning Electricity and Water.

Article 2 establishes the general rule governing electricity production and distribution by persons or entities outside the competent governmental authority.

The law provides an exception concerning personal use, subject to the conditions established by the competent authority.

Consequently, a genuinely private installation used exclusively for the operator's own electricity requirements is legally distinguishable from a private electricity-generation facility intended to distribute or commercially supply electricity.

No specific MW capacity threshold for gravity-powered electricity generation has been identified in the cited Electricity and Water Law. The principal legal issue for a purely self-consumption facility is therefore whether the installation qualifies for the personal-use exception and complies with the applicable technical and safety requirements.

Administrative Requirements

Article 2 of Decree-Law No. (1) of 1996 establishes the general prohibition on private electricity production or distribution without the required written authorization, while recognizing an exception concerning personal use.

Personal-Use Exception

The personal-use exception is the principal provision relevant to a genuinely isolated self-consumption facility.

The installation should remain dedicated to the operator's own use and should not become a private electricity-distribution or commercial supply system.

The exact conditions applicable to the personal-use exception should be confirmed in writing with the competent Bahraini authority before construction.

Other Potential Requirements
  • Written authorization where the personal-use exception does not apply.
  • Compliance with Electricity and Water Authority technical requirements.
  • Compliance with electrical installation regulations.
  • Electrical safety and inspection requirements.
  • Building and construction approvals.
  • Environmental requirements where applicable.
  • Fire and occupational safety requirements.
  • Any conditions imposed by the competent authority.
Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Keep the generating installation completely separated from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not distribute electricity to third parties.
  • Maintain documentary evidence that the electricity is used exclusively for internal purposes.
  • Obtain written confirmation that the installation qualifies for the personal-use exception.
  • Document the nominal and maximum electrical capacity.
  • Comply with EWA electrical installation and safety requirements.
  • Obtain all applicable building, environmental and safety approvals before construction.

Article 2 regulates electricity production primarily through the licensing requirement and the personal-use exception rather than through a specific generation capacity threshold.

The key legal distinction is therefore between electricity produced for personal/internal use and electricity produced for distribution or commercial purposes.

A purely isolated self-consumption installation should be documented so that its operation clearly falls within the personal-use framework.

The exact treatment of a gravity-powered installation, particularly at substantial generating capacity, should be confirmed in writing by the competent Bahraini authority before construction.
