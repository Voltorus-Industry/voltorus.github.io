Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Brunei Darussalam and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external public electricity grid.

Electricity generation in Brunei Darussalam is regulated principally by the Electricity Act (Chapter 223).

The Electricity Act regulates and controls the generation, transmission, distribution and use of electricity.

Section 5 establishes the licensing requirement for electricity-generation activities, subject to statutory exemptions.

The Electricity (Electricity Generation Licence) (Exemption) Order, 2023 provides a specific exemption from the generation-licence requirement for generating units having a name-plate rating of less than 1 MW.

The same Order also provides an exemption for qualifying standby generating units used solely as backup during interruptions and not connected to or operated in parallel with the transmission or distribution system.

A gravity-powered generating unit below 1 MW therefore falls within the express capacity-based generation licence exemption, subject to compliance with the other provisions of the Electricity Act and applicable technical and safety requirements.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Act establishes licensing requirements for generation, transmission and distribution activities.

Section 5 provides that a person must not engage in electricity generation unless authorized by an electricity licence or exempted under the applicable legislation.

Generation Licence Exemption — 2023

The Electricity (Electricity Generation Licence) (Exemption) Order, 2023 provides that the generation-licence requirement does not apply to a person generating electricity by means of a generating unit having a name-plate rating of less than 1 MW.

This exemption is capacity-based and does not depend on the generating technology being solar, wind, hydro or another particular source.

A gravity-powered generating unit below 1 MW can therefore rely on this express generation licensing exemption, subject to the remaining requirements of the Electricity Act.

Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Keep the facility physically isolated from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Document the name-plate generating capacity.
  • If the unit is below 1 MW, document its qualification under the Electricity (Electricity Generation Licence) (Exemption) Order, 2023.
  • If the unit is 1 MW or greater, obtain the required electricity-generation licence before operation.
  • Comply with applicable electrical installation and safety requirements.
  • Obtain all required construction and environmental approvals.
Maximum Electrical Power

Below 1 MW name-plate rating

The Electricity (Electricity Generation Licence) (Exemption) Order, 2023 expressly exempts generating units with a name-plate rating of less than 1 MW from the generation-licence requirement.

The 1 MW figure is a licensing exemption threshold.

It does not constitute a prohibition on generation at or above 1 MW.

A generating unit at or above 1 MW must be assessed under the general electricity-generation licensing provisions.

The exemption applies to the generating unit's name-plate rating, so the engineering documentation should clearly identify the rated electrical capacity.
