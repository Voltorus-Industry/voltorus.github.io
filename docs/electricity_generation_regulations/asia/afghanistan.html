Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Afghanistan and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate as an independent electrical installation without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external public electricity network.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, research, commercial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the operator.

Electricity-generation activities in Afghanistan are principally regulated through the Afghan Electricity Law and the regulatory framework administered by the Ministry responsible for energy and electricity.

The Electricity Law establishes a licensing framework covering electricity production, transfer, distribution, import and export.

Article 10 of the Electricity Law provides for an electricity production licence for a maximum period of 25 years. The law also provides for licensing fees and financial guarantees associated with the licensing process.

No specific statutory electricity-generation capacity threshold applicable specifically to gravity-powered generation has been identified in the cited Electricity Law provisions. The applicable licensing and authorization requirements therefore depend on the project, its capacity, its operation, its location and the requirements imposed by the competent Afghan authorities.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Law establishes a licensing system for electricity-sector activities.

Article 10 identifies electricity production, electricity transfer, electricity distribution, electricity import and electricity export as activities subject to the licensing framework.

Electricity Production Licence

Article 10 provides for an electricity production licence with a maximum duration of 25 years.

The licence is granted by the competent department in accordance with the applicable procedure, fees and financial-guarantee requirements.

The identified provisions do not establish a gravity-specific MW threshold below which a production activity is automatically exempt from the production licensing framework.

Other Potential Administrative Requirements
  • Electricity production authorization or licence where required.
  • Compliance with the applicable electricity technical requirements.
  • Payment of applicable licensing fees.
  • Financial guarantee requirements associated with the production licence.
  • Construction authorization for the generating installation where required.
  • Environmental requirements applicable to the project.
  • Land-use and construction requirements.
  • Electrical safety requirements.
  • Occupational health and safety requirements.
  • Any additional conditions imposed by the competent energy authority.
Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not operate as an electricity supplier or distributor.
  • Document the installation as an internal self-consumption facility.
  • Document the nominal and maximum electrical generating capacity precisely.
  • Obtain a written determination from the competent electricity authority concerning the applicable production licence before construction.
  • Verify environmental, construction and land-use requirements before commencing the project.
  • Ensure that the electrical system cannot unintentionally energize an external network.

No gravity-specific statutory MW threshold identified

The cited Electricity Law provisions establish a licensing framework for electricity production but do not identify a specific electrical-generation capacity threshold dedicated to gravity-powered generation.

Article 10 establishes the electricity production licence and provides for a maximum licence period of 25 years.

The absence of a specific MW threshold in the cited provisions should not be interpreted as an exemption from the licensing framework.

For a proposed gravity-powered facility, the applicable authorization should therefore be determined directly with the competent Afghan electricity authority based on the technology, capacity, location and operating configuration.
