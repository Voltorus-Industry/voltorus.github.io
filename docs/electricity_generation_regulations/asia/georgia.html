Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Georgia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate as a completely isolated electrical system without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external transmission or distribution network.

Electricity generation in Georgia is regulated principally by the Law of Georgia on Energy and Water Supply and the licensing framework administered by the Georgian National Energy and Water Supply Regulatory Commission.

Article 11 establishes mandatory authorization requirements for electricity production and other energy activities.

Article 11(3)(a) expressly provides that electricity production does not require licensing where an individual generates electricity for his or her own household and/or non-household consumption and the electricity-generation and electricity-consumption facilities are not connected to an electricity transmission or distribution network.

The provision is directly applicable to a genuinely off-grid self-consumption facility.

No gravity-specific generation capacity threshold is required by this exemption. The key statutory conditions are the nature of the self-consumption and the absence of connection to the transmission or distribution network.

Administrative Requirements

Article 11 establishes licensing requirements for electricity production and other regulated energy activities.

Article 11(2) prohibits carrying out the activities listed in Article 11(1) without the relevant licence, except for the exemptions expressly provided by law.

Off-Grid Own-Consumption Exemption

Article 11(3)(a) provides that the following electricity production does not require a licence:

Electricity produced by an individual for his or her own household and/or non-household consumption where both the electricity-generation facility and electricity-consumption facility are not connected to an electricity transmission or distribution network.

This provision directly corresponds to the proposed operating configuration of a completely isolated self-consumption facility.

The exemption is therefore based on the operational and connection conditions rather than on a gravity-specific electrical capacity.

Measures to Preserve the Licence Exemption
  • Keep the electricity-generation installation completely disconnected from transmission networks.
  • Keep the installation completely disconnected from distribution networks.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use the electricity exclusively for the owner's household and/or non-household consumption.
  • Maintain physical and electrical separation from the public electricity system.
  • Document the installation's off-grid configuration.
  • Document the ownership and internal consumption arrangement.
  • Verify environmental, construction and electrical safety requirements independently from the generation licence exemption.

Georgia — own-consumption licence exemption:

No specific MW threshold stated in Article 11(3)(a)

The statutory exemption is based on own consumption and the absence of connection to an electricity transmission or distribution network.

Article 11(3)(a) does not establish a specific MW ceiling for the exempted own-consumption activity.

The principal conditions are:

  • Generation by an individual.
  • Own household and/or non-household consumption.
  • No connection of the generation facility to a transmission or distribution network.
  • No connection of the consumption facility to a transmission or distribution network for the purposes of the statutory exemption.

A gravity-powered facility should therefore be assessed under Article 11(3)(a) based on its ownership, consumption arrangement and physical network configuration rather than by applying an unsupported MW limit.

Georgian energy legislation establishes a licensing framework for electricity production but expressly excludes from licensing, under Article 11(3)(a), electricity production by an individual for his or her own household and/or non-household consumption where the generation and consumption facilities are not connected to an electricity transmission or distribution network.
