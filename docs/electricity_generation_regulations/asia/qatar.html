Overview

Qatar regulates the electricity sector through its national electricity legislation and the regulatory framework administered principally through the Qatar General Electricity and Water Corporation (KAHRAMAA) and the competent governmental authorities.

The principal electricity legislation includes Law No. 10 of 2000 concerning the regulation and maintenance of the supply of electricity and water to customers.

Electricity generation, connection, transmission, distribution and supply are subject to the applicable electricity laws, regulations, technical codes and governmental approvals.

The legal treatment of a private electricity generation facility depends on whether the facility is connected to the public electricity system, supplies electricity to other persons, or operates exclusively for the owner's internal consumption.

No single nationwide MW threshold specifically applicable to every private off-grid electricity generation technology has been identified in the principal publicly available electricity legislation. The applicable authorization requirements must therefore be determined from the project configuration, location, connection status, technology and intended use.

Administrative Requirements
  • Verification of the applicable electricity authorization requirements before construction.
  • Verification of whether the project requires approval from KAHRAMAA.
  • Verification of grid-connection requirements if the installation is connected to the electricity network.
  • Compliance with applicable electrical technical standards and safety requirements.
  • Compliance with construction and building requirements.
  • Compliance with environmental requirements where applicable.
  • Compliance with fire and occupational safety requirements.
  • Additional approvals may apply depending on the project location, technology and capacity.
Maximum Electrical Power
Qatar — statutory capacity threshold:

No single nationwide MW threshold applicable to every private electricity-generation installation has been identified in the principal electricity legislation reviewed.

The absence of a single MW threshold does not remove the requirement to verify the applicable approvals.

The regulatory treatment depends on the nature of the electricity activity, the electrical connection, the purpose of the installation and the applicable technical and governmental requirements.

The project capacity should therefore be established from the engineering design and submitted for confirmation to the competent Qatari authorities before construction.
