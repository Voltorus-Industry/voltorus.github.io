Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in the People's Republic of China and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate as an independent electrical installation without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external public electricity network.

Electricity generation in China is principally regulated by the Electric Power Law of the People's Republic of China and the power-business licensing framework administered by the National Energy Administration.

The Electric Power Law applies to electricity construction, generation, supply and consumption within the territory of China.

Article 13 of the Electric Power Law provides that where a self-owned power station is not connected to the grid, the electricity investor independently controls and uses the electricity generated.

The National Energy Administration's current licensing policy expressly exempts self-owned generating stations whose generated electricity is entirely used by the owner and is not traded through the grid from the electricity business licensing requirement.

Therefore, for a completely off-grid gravity-powered self-consumption facility, the principal licensing issue is not a gravity-specific MW threshold but whether the installation qualifies as a self-owned station whose electricity is entirely self-consumed and not sold or traded through the public grid.

Administrative Requirements

The Electric Power Law establishes the general legal framework for electricity generation, supply and consumption.

Electricity business licensing is governed by the electricity business licensing regulations and National Energy Administration policies.

Self-Owned Station Exemption

The National Energy Administration's licensing policy provides that self-owned power stations whose generated electricity is entirely used by the owner and is not traded through the grid do not fall within the power-business licensing requirement.

This exemption is directly relevant to a completely off-grid self-consumption facility.

The exemption depends on the actual operating configuration. If electricity is sold, traded, supplied to external users or otherwise enters the public electricity market, a different regulatory treatment may apply.

Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Ensure that all electricity generated is consumed by the owner.
  • Do not sell surplus electricity through the public electricity market.
  • Do not supply electricity to external customers.
  • Document the project as a self-owned self-consumption power station.
  • Determine the project's investment filing or approval requirements before construction.
  • Obtain confirmation from the competent local energy authority concerning the applicable licensing status.
  • Comply with environmental, construction, electrical safety and fire-safety requirements.

No gravity-specific MW threshold identified

The current licensing exemption for self-owned stations whose electricity is entirely self-consumed and not traded through the grid is not expressed as a gravity-specific MW threshold.

China also has separate licence-exemption rules for certain categories of distributed generation and renewable-energy projects, including specified projects below 6 MW.

Those technology-specific 6 MW provisions should not be automatically applied to gravity generation.

The more directly relevant rule for a completely isolated gravity installation is the exemption for a self-owned station whose electricity is entirely self-consumed and not traded through the grid.

The project capacity should nevertheless be precisely documented and the applicable project approval, filing, construction and local energy-authority requirements verified before construction.

This information is provided for general public information purposes only. China's Electric Power Law regulates electricity generation, supply and consumption. The National Energy Administration's licensing policy provides an exemption from electricity-business licensing for self-owned generating stations whose electricity is entirely used by the owner and is not traded through the grid. A gravity-powered project should therefore be structured and documented as a genuinely self-owned, self-consumption installation if that exemption is to be relied upon, while all applicable project, environmental, construction and safety requirements remain applicable.
