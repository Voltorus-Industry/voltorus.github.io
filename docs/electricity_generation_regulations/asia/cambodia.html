Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Cambodia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate as an isolated electrical system without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the national public electricity grid.

Electricity-sector activities in Cambodia are principally regulated by the Electricity Law of the Kingdom of Cambodia, promulgated on 2 February 2001 and subsequently amended.

The Ministry of Mines and Energy is responsible for government policies, strategies and planning in the power sector, while the Electricity Authority of Cambodia regulates electric power services.

Article 29 identifies the Generation License as one of the electricity-sector licences administered by the Electricity Authority of Cambodia.

Article 30 expressly defines the Generation License as the right to own, operate and manage or control generation facilities for generating electricity for sale and not solely for own consumption.

Consequently, a facility genuinely producing electricity solely for internal self-consumption is legally distinct from a licensed generation facility intended to sell electricity.

The cited Electricity Law does not establish a specific MW capacity threshold for gravity-powered generation. The principal regulatory distinction is instead the purpose of the generation and whether the facility constitutes a regulated electric power service.

Administrative Requirements

Article 29 establishes eight categories of licences, including the Generation License.

Article 30 provides that a Generation License grants the right to own, operate and manage or control identified generation facilities for generating electricity for sale and not solely for own consumption.

Pure Self-Consumption

The wording of Article 30 is directly relevant to a purely off-grid self-consumption installation.

The Generation License is structured around generation for sale rather than generation solely for own consumption.

Accordingly, a facility that produces electricity exclusively for its own controlled consumption and does not sell or distribute electricity to customers is distinguishable from a commercial generation licensee.

Because Article 30 does not create a general technology-neutral exemption statement for every type of self-generation, the legal status of a large gravity-powered facility should nevertheless be confirmed with the Electricity Authority of Cambodia.

Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation from the national grid.
  • Do not import electricity from the national grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the national grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not distribute electricity to customers.
  • Document the project as an internal self-consumption installation.
  • Document the complete electrical capacity and operating configuration.
  • Obtain written confirmation from EAC concerning the project's licensing status before construction.
  • Comply with technical, environmental and construction requirements.

The Electricity Law regulates generation primarily by licensing category and purpose rather than by a gravity-specific installed-capacity threshold.

Article 30 defines the Generation License as the right to generate electricity for sale and not solely for own consumption.

The law therefore provides a strong distinction between commercial generation and generation solely for internal consumption.

A separate EAC regulation concerning solar PV expressly allows an off-grid solar system to be installed for own consumption when it is not connected or synchronized with the national grid.

That solar-specific rule should not automatically be treated as a technology-neutral exemption for gravity generation. The precise legal treatment of the gravity facility should be confirmed with EAC.
