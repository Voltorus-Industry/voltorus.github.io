Overview

Electricity generation in the United Arab Emirates is regulated through a combination of federal legislation and emirate-level electricity-sector legislation.

The competent authority depends on the emirate and the nature of the electricity activity.

Dubai, Abu Dhabi and the other Emirates have distinct electricity-sector regulatory arrangements.

A project must therefore be assessed against the legislation of the specific Emirate in which the generation facility will be located.

Dubai — Generation Licensing

Law No. 6 of 2011 regulates private-sector participation in electricity and water production in the Emirate of Dubai.

Article 5 provides that no public or private entity may conduct the regulated activities defined by the Law without first obtaining a licence from the Bureau.

The Bureau is the entity assigned to issue licences and exemptions under the Law.

The Dubai legislation specifically concerns regulated electricity-production activities for supplying the transmission system. A purely isolated installation must therefore be assessed against the applicable Dubai electricity legislation and regulatory decisions.

Self-Consumption / Isolated Operation
  • No intended connection to a public electricity network.
  • No intended electricity export.
  • No intended sale to third parties.
  • Verify the electricity authority of the relevant Emirate.
  • Verify construction, environmental and electrical safety approvals.
Maximum Electrical Power
UAE — no single nationwide generation threshold applicable to every project.

Electricity generation regulation is decentralized between federal and Emirate-level authorities.

Consequently, a single UAE-wide MW threshold should not be assigned to every private electricity-generation project.

The applicable capacity and licensing requirements must be determined for the specific Emirate and project configuration.
