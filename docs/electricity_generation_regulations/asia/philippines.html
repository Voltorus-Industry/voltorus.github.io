Overview

Electricity generation in the Philippines is principally governed by Republic Act No. 9136, otherwise known as the Electric Power Industry Reform Act of 2001 (EPIRA), together with implementing rules and regulations issued by the Energy Regulatory Commission (ERC).

Section 6 of EPIRA provides that electricity generation is a business affected with public interest and shall be competitive and open.

A new generation company must, before operating, secure a Certificate of Compliance from the Energy Regulatory Commission and obtain the applicable health, safety and environmental clearances.

Philippine regulation also recognizes Self-Generating Facilities (SGFs), distributed energy resources, embedded generators and off-grid generators.

A facility designed exclusively for internal consumption therefore remains subject to the applicable ERC self-generation regulatory framework.

Maximum Electrical Power
Philippines — no universal MW generation ceiling identified in EPIRA

Section 6 of EPIRA establishes the regulatory requirement for generation companies but does not establish one universal maximum MW generating capacity.

The ERC instead regulates different generation configurations through Certificates of Compliance, including Self-Generating Facilities, distributed energy resources and off-grid generators.

The applicable requirements must therefore be determined according to the facility's capacity, ownership, technology, connection status and use.
