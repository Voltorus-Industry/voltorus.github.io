Overview

Electricity generation in Singapore is principally regulated by the Electricity Act 2001.

The Energy Market Authority (EMA) administers the electricity regulatory framework.

The Electricity Act regulates the generation, transmission, supply and use of electricity and establishes an electricity licensing framework.

The official Singapore Government licensing portal states that an Electricity Generation Licence is required for electricity generation of 10 MW or more and for participation in the wholesale electricity market.

Specific exemptions exist for certain categories of generation, including standby generating units that satisfy the applicable conditions.

The exact legal treatment of a private self-consumption installation therefore depends on its capacity, operating configuration and whether it is connected to or operates in parallel with the transmission system.

Administrative Requirements
  • Determine the installed generation capacity.
  • Determine whether the facility is connected to or operated in parallel with the transmission system.
  • Determine whether an Electricity Generation Licence is required.
  • Determine whether a statutory exemption applies.
  • Comply with EMA electricity codes and technical requirements.
  • Comply with electrical installation and safety requirements.
  • Obtain applicable construction and environmental approvals.
Maximum Electrical Power
Singapore — generation licensing threshold:

10 MW

The official licensing portal identifies electricity generation of 10 MW or more as requiring an Electricity Generation Licence.

Installations below 10 MW must not automatically be considered exempt from regulation. The applicable exemption orders and technical requirements must be checked for the specific installation.
