Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Bhutan and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate as an independent electrical installation without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external public electricity network.

Electricity generation in Bhutan is principally regulated by the Electricity Act of Bhutan 2001 and by regulations, guidelines, codes and licensing instruments issued by the Electricity Regulatory Authority.

The Electricity Regulatory Authority is the sector regulator responsible for licensing and regulating electricity-sector activities.

The Electricity Act establishes a licensing framework for electricity generation and related electricity activities.

The Electricity Regulatory Authority issued the "Guideline for Exemption of License for Generation of Electricity from Distributed Energy Resources below 500kW, 2022".

That guideline applies to distributed energy resources defined as small-scale generation from solar PV systems, wind power plants or hydropower plants below 500 kW.

A gravity-powered generation system is not expressly identified as one of those three DER categories in the cited definition.

Accordingly, the 500 kW DER licence exemption should not automatically be applied to a gravity-powered facility. The project should instead obtain a determination from the Electricity Regulatory Authority concerning the applicable generation licence or exemption.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Act of Bhutan 2001 provides the legal framework for licensing and regulation of electricity activities.

The Electricity Regulatory Authority is responsible for issuing and administering electricity licences.

500 kW DER Exemption

The 2022 ERA guideline establishes a licence exemption framework for Distributed Energy Resources below 500 kW.

The guideline defines Distributed Energy Resources as small-scale electricity generation from:

  • Solar PV systems.
  • Wind power plants.
  • Hydropower plants.

The guideline requires the installation to be used for self-consumptive electricity generation and to satisfy safety, technical and environmental requirements.

Because gravity-powered generation is not expressly included in the cited DER definition, the exemption cannot safely be extended to gravity technology without an ERA determination.

Other Potential Administrative Requirements
  • Electricity-generation licence where required by the Electricity Act.
  • Written determination from the ERA concerning classification of the gravity technology.
  • Environmental clearances where applicable.
  • Electrical safety requirements.
  • Technical and performance standards.
  • Construction and land-use approvals.
  • Requirements imposed by the Bhutan Power Corporation where an electrical connection exists.
  • Applicable requirements of the National Environment Commission.
Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Keep the installation physically isolated from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use the electricity exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Document the technology and installed generating capacity precisely.
  • Obtain a written ERA determination before relying on any generation licence exemption.
  • Verify environmental and construction clearances before construction.
  • Ensure that the electrical installation cannot accidentally energise an external network.

No gravity-specific statutory capacity threshold identified

The ERA's 2022 licence-exemption guideline establishes a below-500 kW exemption for qualifying Distributed Energy Resources.

The guideline's definition of Distributed Energy Resources expressly refers to solar PV, wind power and hydropower below 500 kW.

Gravity-powered generation is not expressly listed in that definition.

Therefore, 500 kW should not be presented as a general statutory exemption for gravity-powered generation.

The maximum project capacity and applicable licence category should be determined by the Electricity Regulatory Authority based on the actual technology, installed capacity, operating configuration and intended use.
