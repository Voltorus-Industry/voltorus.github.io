Overview

Electricity generation in Pakistan is principally governed by the Regulation of Generation, Transmission and Distribution of Electric Power Act, 1997 and regulations issued by the National Electric Power Regulatory Authority (NEPRA).

Section 15 establishes the generation-licensing requirement.

Subject to the statutory framework, a person may not construct, own or operate a generation facility without the authority of a generation licence issued by NEPRA.

Pakistan also has specific distributed-generation and net-metering rules for smaller renewable installations.

The licensing treatment therefore depends on the type of generation facility, its capacity, connection arrangement and whether a specific regulatory exemption or distributed-generation framework applies.

Maximum Electrical Power
Pakistan — no general national maximum MW generation ceiling identified in the NEPRA Act

Section 15 regulates generation facilities through licensing rather than establishing a universal maximum generation capacity.

A separate government guidance document states that a distributed-generation facility above 25 kW requires a generation licence from NEPRA for the relevant net-metering framework.

The 25 kW threshold therefore concerns licensing within that distributed-generation framework and should not be treated as a general national generation limit.

Public Information Notice

Pakistan regulates electricity generation principally through the NEPRA Act and generation licensing regulations. Section 15 establishes a general generation-licensing requirement, subject to the applicable statutory framework. Separate rules apply to distributed generation and net metering, including a 25 kW licensing threshold identified in government guidance. This threshold should not be interpreted as a general maximum generation capacity.
