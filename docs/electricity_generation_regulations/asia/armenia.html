Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Armenia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate as an isolated electrical installation without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external public electricity network.

Armenia regulates electricity generation through the Law of the Republic of Armenia on Energy and the electricity-sector licensing rules administered by the Public Services Regulatory Commission.

Article 35 of the Energy Law regulates electricity production licences and provides the legal framework for construction, reconstruction and operation of generating stations.

The law expressly recognizes electricity production carried out exclusively for the producer's own needs.

The law does not establish a general gravity-specific generation threshold. However, Article 35 distinguishes electricity-production licences for generating stations with installed capacity below 30 MW from licences for stations with installed capacity of 30 MW or more.

The 30 MW figure is an additional governmental-consent threshold for licensing and should not be interpreted as a general exemption from the electricity-production licensing framework.

Administrative Requirements

Article 23 of the Energy Law provides that electricity and heat generation activities are subject to the applicable activity licensing framework.

Article 35 specifically regulates electricity-production licences.

Own-Consumption Generation

Armenian legislation expressly recognizes electricity production exclusively for the producer's own needs.

The licensing framework contains specific rules applicable to persons holding an electricity production licence for their own needs.

Consequently, the fact that a facility is completely off-grid and intended solely for internal consumption does not by itself justify assuming that the project falls outside the regulatory framework. The exact licence category must be determined under the current Commission rules.

Capacity Thresholds in Article 35

Article 35 provides a specific distinction at 30 MW installed capacity.

  • Up to 30 MW: electricity-production licensing is considered under the applicable regulatory framework and, for renewable resources, relevant energy-development considerations apply.
  • 30 MW and above: the electricity-production licence additionally requires the consent of the competent governmental authority in the energy field.

The 30 MW value is therefore an additional authorization threshold, not a general production exemption.

Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Keep the installation physically isolated from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Document the installation as an internal self-consumption facility.
  • Determine whether the technology qualifies as a renewable-energy resource under Armenian law before relying on renewable-energy provisions.
  • Determine the precise installed capacity before applying for authorization.
  • Obtain the applicable electricity-production licence or written regulatory determination.
  • If the installed capacity reaches 30 MW or more, obtain the additional governmental consent required by Article 35.
  • Verify construction, environmental, land-use and electrical-safety requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
Armenia — principal statutory capacity threshold:

30 MW installed capacity

Article 35 establishes an additional governmental consent requirement for electricity-production licences involving generating stations with installed capacity of 30 MW or more.

The 30 MW threshold does not mean that generation below 30 MW is exempt from all licensing requirements.

The Energy Law separately establishes the electricity production licensing framework and specifically recognizes production for the producer's own needs.

For a gravity-powered facility, the applicable capacity and authorization should therefore be determined from the installed generating capacity and the legal classification of the technology.
