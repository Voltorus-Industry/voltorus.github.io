Overview

Electricity generation in the Democratic People's Republic of Korea is governed principally by the Electricity Law of the DPRK.

The available English translation of the revised Electricity Law states that the law regulates the construction of power facilities, power production, power supply, power utilization, dispatching and the national integrated power-management system.

The law follows a centralized state-management model for the electricity sector.

Article 21 requires institutions, enterprises and organizations intending to construct, expand or relocate power-generation facilities to obtain approval from the central power-industry guidance institution.

The accessible text does not establish a general electricity-generation MW licensing threshold. Regulatory approval is instead based principally on the project, institutional authority and national power-system framework.

Administrative Requirements
  • Approval for construction, expansion or relocation of power-generation facilities.
  • Compliance with approved technical design.
  • Completion inspection before operation.
  • Compliance with national power-management requirements.
  • Approval for connection to or separation from the national power system.
  • Installation and registration of power-management technical and communication equipment where required.
  • Compliance with technical regulations and operating procedures.
Maximum Electrical Power
North Korea — no general MW generation threshold identified in the accessible current Electricity Law text

The Electricity Law recognizes large-scale and medium-small-scale power plants but does not establish a general maximum electrical capacity applicable to private or self-consumption electricity generation.

The principal regulatory requirement is governmental approval of power-facility construction and operation within the centralized national electricity-management system.

Public Information Notice

Electricity generation in the DPRK is governed by a centralized state electricity-management framework. The current accessible Electricity Law requires governmental approval for construction, expansion and relocation of power-generation facilities and prohibits operation of facilities that have not passed completion inspection. No general MW generation threshold was identified in the accessible current text.
