Overview

Electricity generation in India is principally governed by the Electricity Act, 2003, together with regulations, technical standards, environmental legislation and applicable State-level requirements.

The principal statutory provisions concerning generation are contained in Sections 7, 8, 9 and 10 of the Electricity Act, 2003.

Section 7 provides that a generating company may establish, operate and maintain a generating station without obtaining a licence under the Electricity Act, subject to compliance with the applicable technical standards relating to connectivity with the grid.

Section 9 specifically recognizes captive generation and permits a person to construct, maintain or operate a captive generating plant and dedicated transmission lines, subject to the statutory conditions.

Therefore, the fact that a generating installation is privately owned or intended principally for self-consumption does not by itself create a general electricity-generation licence requirement under Section 7.

Other approvals may nevertheless apply depending on the technology, location, environmental impact, fuel, land, construction, electrical installation and whether the installation is connected to a public grid.

Administrative Requirements
Section 7 — Generation

Section 7 of the Electricity Act, 2003 provides that a generating company may establish, operate and maintain a generating station without obtaining a licence under the Act if it complies with the applicable technical standards relating to connectivity with the grid.

Section 9 — Captive Generation

Section 9 expressly permits a person to construct, maintain or operate a captive generating plant and dedicated transmission lines.

Electricity supplied from a captive generating plant through the grid is subject to the applicable regulatory provisions.

The statutory framework also recognizes open access for carrying electricity from the captive generating plant to the destination of its use, subject to the availability and regulation of transmission facilities.

  • Grid-connection requirements if the installation is connected to the electricity system.
Maximum Electrical Power
Statutory generation threshold under Section 7:

No general MW generation-capacity threshold is established by Section 7 as a condition for the generation licence.

The Electricity Act, 2003 does not establish a general MW ceiling in Section 7 above which electricity generation automatically requires a generation licence.

The legal treatment depends instead on the nature of the activity, the technology, the electricity supplied, grid connection and other applicable statutory requirements.

Hydroelectric projects are subject to the separate requirements of Section 8.

Consequently, the project capacity should be determined from the engineering design and then checked against the applicable Central and State regulatory requirements.
