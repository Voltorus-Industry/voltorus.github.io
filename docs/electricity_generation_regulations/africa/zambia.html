Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Zambia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in Zambia is principally regulated by the Electricity Act and the Energy Regulation Act.

The Energy Regulation Board (ERB) specifically addresses electricity generation intended exclusively for own use.

Where the capacity of the generation unit is up to 100 kW , an exemption from the generation licence may be granted by the ERB.

At 100 kW or higher , a "generation for own use" licence must be obtained before commissioning and generation.

Administrative Requirements

The Zambian regulatory framework distinguishes electricity generation for own use from electricity generation involving supply to other persons.

The ERB states that where a generation unit has a capacity of up to 100 kW , an exemption from a generation licence may be granted.

Where the generation unit has a capacity of 100 kW or higher , a generation for own use licence must be obtained before commissioning.

Application to an Isolated Gravity Installation

Zambian legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically for gravity-powered electricity generation.

The relevant classification is therefore based on the generating unit and its intended use.

For a purely off-grid self-consumption installation, the relevant maximum capacity below the ordinary own-use licensing threshold is 100 kW.

  • No electricity export.
  • No electricity sale to third parties.
  • No electricity purchase from the public grid.
  • Electricity used exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Independent island-mode operation.
  • Electrical protection preventing accidental grid energisation.
  • Precise documentation of the nominal generating capacity.
  • ERB verification before construction and commissioning.
