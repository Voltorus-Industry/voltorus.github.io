Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in South Africa and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in South Africa is principally regulated by the Electricity Regulation Act 4 of 2006 (ERA), as amended.

The regulatory framework is administered by the National Energy Regulator of South Africa (NERSA).

South African legislation distinguishes between electricity-generation activities requiring a licence, activities requiring registration and activities exempt from both licensing and registration.

The current framework specifically excludes from the application of the Electricity Regulation Act the operation of a generation facility that has no point of connection to the electricity network.

The regulatory treatment must nevertheless be assessed according to the facility's electrical configuration, capacity, intended use, connection status, technology, location and applicable environmental, construction and safety requirements.

Administrative Requirements

South African electricity legislation generally requires an appropriate licence for the operation of electricity-generation facilities unless the activity falls within an applicable statutory exemption.

The Electricity Regulation Act, as amended, expressly provides that the Act does not apply to the operation of a generation facility, irrespective of capacity, where the facility does not have a point of connection to the electricity network.

The legislation also contains specific exemptions and registration requirements for other categories of generation facilities.

Purely Off-grid Self-Consumption Generation

For the proposed project, the installation is intended to operate as a private isolated electricity-generation facility.

The facility would have no physical point of connection to the South African electricity network.

Electricity would be generated solely for internal consumption by the owner or operator.

The current Electricity Regulation Act specifically provides an exemption for a generation facility without a point of connection.

The 100 MW figure is the principal regulatory benchmark used for the South African generation exemption/registration framework and should be distinguished from the separate legal treatment of a genuinely isolated facility with no point of connection.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical point of connection to the South African electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public electricity network.
  • No electricity is exported to the public electricity network.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public electricity network.
  • No electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No wheeling of electricity through the public electricity network is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public electricity network.
  • The installation is documented as a private self-consumption facility.
  • The nominal electrical capacity is documented precisely.
  • The applicable NERSA requirements are verified before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, structural and safety requirements are independently verified.
Maximum Electrical Power
South Africa — regulatory capacity benchmark:

100 MW

South Africa's electricity regulatory framework uses 100 MW as the principal threshold for the modern embedded generation licensing-exemption and registration framework.

Generation projects from 1 MW to 100 MW fall within the regulatory registration framework established under Schedule 2 of the Electricity Regulation Act.

The South African Government confirmed the increase of the embedded-generation licensing threshold from 1 MW to 100 MW in 2021.

The current legislation also contains a specific provision for generation facilities without a point of connection to the electricity network.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

South African electricity legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The applicable regulatory treatment is therefore technology-neutral and depends on the legal classification and electrical configuration of the generation facility.

For a facility with no point of connection to the electricity network, the Electricity Regulation Act provides a specific exemption from its application.

For website classification and project screening, use 100 MW as the principal South African regulatory capacity benchmark.

Website classification for South Africa:

100 MW

Use 100 MW as the principal South African regulatory capacity benchmark for the gravity-powered electricity- generation facility.

A genuinely isolated installation with no point of connection must nevertheless be assessed separately under the applicable provisions of the Electricity Regulation Act and other South African legislation.

The 100 MW figure is a regulatory benchmark and should not be interpreted as a technology-specific mechanical or electrical performance limit for gravity generation.
