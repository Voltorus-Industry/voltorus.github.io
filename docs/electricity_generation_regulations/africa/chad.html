Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Chad and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

The electricity sector in Chad is principally regulated by Law No. 036/PR/2019 of 26 August 2019 relating to the electricity sector .

The regulatory framework is supplemented by implementing legislation, including Decree No. 1841/PR/MPME/2019 of 8 November 2019 establishing the conditions and procedures for granting electricity-production licences.

The Chadian electricity framework distinguishes electricity-production activities from self-consumption and establishes specific regulatory requirements according to the characteristics and capacity of the installation.

Current information published by the Electricity Sector Regulatory Authority (ARSE) indicates that electricity-production activities are subject to licensing requirements according to the applicable capacity and activity.

For self-consumption installations, the 2019 regulatory framework provides that installations with a capacity below 1 MW are exempt from a production licence but remain subject to a prior declaration to the regulator.

A completely isolated installation must therefore be assessed according to its nominal generating capacity, electrical configuration, technology, location and applicable construction, environmental, safety and planning requirements.

Administrative Requirements

Chadian electricity legislation establishes a regulatory framework for electricity production and self-consumption installations.

Under the framework applicable to self-consumption, installations below 1 MW are exempt from a production licence but remain subject to a prior declaration to the competent regulatory authority.

The regulatory framework therefore distinguishes the self-consumption regime from the general electricity-production licensing regime.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Chadian public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No electricity is supplied to third-party consumers.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installation is documented as a private self-consumption facility.
  • The nominal electrical capacity is documented precisely.
  • The prior declaration required for the self-consumption installation is completed before commissioning where applicable.
  • If the installation reaches or exceeds 1 MW, the applicable production licensing requirements are verified with ARSE and the competent Ministry before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, structural and safety requirements are independently verified.

Chad — self-consumption capacity threshold:

1 MW (1,000 kW)

Under the Chadian electricity framework, electricity self-consumption installations below 1 MW are exempt from a production licence but remain subject to prior declaration to the regulator.

The 2019 Chadian electricity-sector reform specifies that self-consumption installations of less than 1 MW are exempt from a production licence.

Such installations nevertheless remain subject to a prior declaration to the regulatory authority.

Accordingly, for a gravity-powered electricity generation facility intended exclusively for internal self-consumption and without connection to the public electricity network, 1 MW (1,000 kW) is the relevant upper regulatory threshold to use for the website classification.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

Chadian electricity legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The applicable capacity regime is therefore determined according to the legal classification of the electricity-generation installation and its intended use, rather than by the word "gravity" itself.

The proposed facility is designed as an isolated self-consumption installation. It therefore falls to be assessed under the applicable self-consumption framework.

For the purpose of the website, the relevant maximum capacity before the production licensing regime must be assessed is 1 MW (1,000 kW) .

At 1 MW or above , the applicable electricity-production licensing requirements must be assessed with ARSE and the competent Ministry before construction and operation.

The 1 MW (1,000 kW) figure is a legal and regulatory capacity threshold used to determine the applicable self-consumption and electricity-production regime.

It should not be interpreted as a technology-specific mechanical or electrical performance limitation for gravity-powered electricity generation.
