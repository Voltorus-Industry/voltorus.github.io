Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Kenya and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Kenya regulates electricity generation under the Energy Act 2019 and the electricity licensing regulations administered by the Energy and Petroleum Regulatory Authority (EPRA).

The current licensing framework contains an explicit exemption for persons generating electricity for their own use where the capacity does not exceed 1 MW.

Administrative Requirements

Kenya's 2024 electricity licensing regulations expressly exclude persons generating electricity for their own use where the capacity does not exceed 1 MW.

Own-use Generation Exemption

The Energy (Electric Power Undertaking Licensing) Regulations, 2024 provide that the licensing regulations do not apply to a person generating electrical energy for their own use where the capacity does not exceed 1 MW.

This is the principal numerical threshold relevant to the proposed private self-consumption configuration.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Installed generation capacity should not exceed 1 MW where relying on the own-use licensing exemption.
  • The installation should have no physical connection to the public electricity network.
  • No electricity should be imported from the grid.
  • No electricity should be exported to the grid.
  • No electricity should be sold to third parties.
  • Electricity should be used exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Electrical protection should prevent accidental grid connection.
  • The installation should be documented as captive / own-use generation.
  • The 1 MW threshold should be verified against the final installed electrical configuration.
Maximum Electrical Power
Kenya maximum capacity for the own-use licensing exemption:

1 MW

The Energy (Electric Power Undertaking Licensing) Regulations, 2024 expressly exclude electricity generated for the generator's own use where the capacity does not exceed 1 MW.

The Kenyan Ministry of Energy also identifies 1 MW as the threshold above which a generation licence is required for captive power.

Website classification for Kenya:

Maximum capacity under the own-use licensing exemption: 1 MW.

A facility above this threshold should be assessed for the applicable generation licence and other regulatory approvals.

The 1 MW value is a regulatory licensing threshold for own-use generation; it is not a statement that every gravity-powered installation can technically produce that amount of electricity.
