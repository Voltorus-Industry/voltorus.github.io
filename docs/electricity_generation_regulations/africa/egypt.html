Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Egypt and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Egypt's principal electricity-sector legislation is Electricity Law No. 87 of 2015 and its Executive Regulations issued by Decree No. 230 of 2016.

Article 13 of Electricity Law No. 87 of 2015 requires a licence for electricity production activities in principle.

However, the law expressly provides that electricity producers generating electricity for their own use may be exempted from permits and licences, subject to conditions established by the Executive Regulations.

The specific regulatory treatment therefore depends on the type of installation, its capacity, whether it is connected to the grid and whether it falls under specific renewable-energy self-consumption rules.

Administrative Requirements

Egypt applies a licensing framework to electricity production, but the Electricity Law specifically provides for possible exemptions for producers generating electricity for their own use.

Self-consumption Treatment

Article 13 of Electricity Law No. 87 of 2015 provides that electricity producers for their own use may be exempted from permits and licences.

The detailed conditions are established through the Executive Regulations and subsequent regulatory instruments.

The specific rules identified for solar self-consumption should not automatically be extended to a gravity-powered installation without confirmation from EgyptERA.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain genuinely isolated and dedicated to self-consumption, we recommend that:

  • The installation has no physical connection to the public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported.
  • No electricity is exported.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • Electricity is consumed exclusively by the producer.
  • The system operates in electrical island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental energisation of the public grid.
  • The exact capacity is documented.
  • EgyptERA requirements are confirmed before construction.

Egypt-wide maximum for an off-grid gravity self-consumption installation:

No single technology-neutral maximum kW/MW threshold has been identified in the reviewed Egyptian legislation for a completely isolated gravity-powered self-consumption installation.

Electricity Law No. 87 of 2015 provides a possible exemption for electricity producers generating electricity for their own use, but the detailed conditions are established by the Executive Regulations.

EgyptERA Circular No. 3 of 2023 specifically addresses solar photovoltaic self-consumption and includes regulatory provisions for isolated/off-grid installations. It should not be treated as a universal capacity limit for gravity technology.

Demonstrated Gravity-Technology Benchmark

The Rudong EVx gravity-energy-storage system in China has a rated peak power output of 25 MW and an energy capacity of 100 MWh.

This is the relevant demonstrated commercial-scale gravity technology benchmark, not an Egyptian licensing threshold.
