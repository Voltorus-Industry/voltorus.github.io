Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation and energy-storage facility located in Namibia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the Namibian public electricity network.

The principal framework is the Electricity Act 2007.

Section 17 establishes a general requirement for a generation licence, while section 18 establishes specific exemptions.

For qualifying own-consumption generation, the principal statutory threshold is less than 500 kVA.

Administrative Requirements

Section 17 of the Electricity Act establishes a general duty to obtain a licence for electricity generation.

Section 18(1)(a) provides an exemption where:

  • the generation plant has an installed capacity of less than 500 kVA;
  • electricity is generated exclusively for own consumption;
  • the person controlling the plant owns or occupies the premises.

Section 18(1)(b) also provides a corresponding exemption for qualifying standby generation where a supply network is available.

The exemption does not remove requirements under other laws, including health, safety and environmental requirements.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation from the public network.
  • Keep installed generating capacity below 500 kVA if relying on the statutory exemption.
  • Use the electricity exclusively for own consumption.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Comply with Electricity Control Board rules and applicable safety and environmental legislation.
Maximum Electrical Power
Namibia licence-free own-consumption maximum: Less than 500 kVA subject to the conditions of section 18.

The statutory wording is "less than 500 kVA", so 500 kVA itself should not be described as being inside the exemption.
