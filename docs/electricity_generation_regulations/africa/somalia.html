Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Somalia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in Somalia is governed by the National Electricity Act 2023 , which established the legal framework for the electricity sector and provided for the establishment of the National Electricity Authority (NEA) as the electricity-sector regulatory authority.

The NEA is responsible for electricity-sector regulation, including licensing and permitting for electricity generation, transmission and distribution.

Available Somali electricity-sector regulatory documentation identifies a principal generation licensing threshold of 500 kW (0.5 MW) for generation facilities, with an exemption for isolated generation below that threshold where the facility is not connected to a transmission or distribution system.

A completely isolated installation must nevertheless be assessed according to its nominal generating capacity, electrical configuration, technology, location and applicable construction, environmental, land-use and safety requirements.

Administrative Requirements

The Somali electricity framework establishes a licensing system administered by the National Electricity Authority.

Available official electricity-sector regulatory documentation identifies a generation licence requirement for generation facilities with a capacity over 500 kW and for generation facilities connected to a transmission or distribution network.

The same regulatory framework identifies an exemption for an isolated generation plant below 500 kW where the installation is not connected to a transmission or distribution system.

Isolated Self-Consumption Generation

For the proposed project, the installation is intended to operate as a private isolated electricity-generation facility.

The facility would have no physical connection to a transmission or distribution network.

Electricity would be generated solely for internal consumption by the owner or operator.

The principal capacity threshold identified in the available Somali regulatory framework is 500 kW (0.5 MW) .

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to a Somali transmission or distribution network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public electricity network.
  • No electricity is exported to the public electricity network.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public electricity network.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No electricity is supplied to unrelated third parties.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public network.
  • The installation is documented as a private self-consumption facility.
  • The nominal electrical capacity is documented precisely.
  • The applicable Somali electricity-generation authorization requirements are verified before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, structural and safety requirements are independently verified.
Maximum Electrical Power
Somalia — principal capacity threshold:

500 kW (0.5 MW)

The available Somali electricity-sector regulatory framework identifies 500 kW as the principal threshold relevant to generation licensing for isolated generation facilities.

Generation facilities exceeding 500 kW should therefore be treated as falling within the generation licensing framework unless a specific statutory or regulatory exemption applies.

For an isolated gravity-powered generation project, the decisive parameters are the installed nominal electrical capacity, absence or presence of a network connection, the intended use of the electricity and the legal classification of the installation.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

Somali electricity legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The applicable capacity framework is therefore technology-neutral and depends on the legal classification of the electricity-generation facility.

For the proposed project, the principal capacity threshold to use for the Somali electricity-generation regulatory assessment is 500 kW (0.5 MW) .

Website classification for Somalia:

500 kW

Use 500 kW as the principal Somali regulatory capacity threshold for the gravity-powered electricity- generation facility.

A facility exceeding this threshold should be specifically assessed with the National Electricity Authority before construction or operation.

The 500 kW figure is a regulatory capacity threshold and should not be interpreted as a technology-specific mechanical or electrical performance limit for gravity generation.
