Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Togo and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in Togo is principally regulated by Law No. 2000-012 of 18 July 2000 relating to the electricity sector and Law No. 2018-010 of 8 August 2018 promoting electricity production from renewable energy sources.

The electricity sector is regulated by the Autorité de Réglementation du Secteur de l'Électricité (ARSE) .

Togo expressly recognizes electricity production for the producer's own needs as auto-production / auto-consumption .

For renewable-energy installations used exclusively for the producer's own needs and not intended to supply electricity to third parties, installations with a power of 100 kWc or more require installation and operating authorizations from ARSE.

Renewable-energy installations above 32 kWc and below 100 kWc fall within the declaration regime.

A gravity-powered installation should therefore be assessed according to the applicable renewable-energy classification, nominal electrical capacity, electrical configuration and intended self-consumption use.

Administrative Requirements

Togo expressly regulates electricity generation for the producer's own needs.

For renewable-energy electricity-generation installations used exclusively for self-consumption, the statutory framework establishes specific declaration and authorization thresholds.

Installations above 32 kWc and below 100 kWc fall within the declaration regime.

Installations with a power of 100 kWc or more require installation and operating authorizations from ARSE.

Renewable Self-Consumption Generation

The proposed installation would operate as a private isolated renewable-energy electricity-generation facility.

The electricity would be used exclusively for the producer's own needs.

The facility would not be intended to supply electricity to third parties.

The principal authorization threshold for a renewable-energy self-consumption installation is 100 kWc .

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Install electrical protection preventing accidental network connection.
  • Keep the nominal capacity below 100 kWc if relying on a regime below the authorization threshold.
  • Complete the applicable declaration procedure for installations within the declaration range.
  • Obtain ARSE installation and operating authorizations where the capacity is 100 kWc or more .
  • Verify construction, environmental, planning, structural and safety requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
Togo — maximum power below the renewable-energy authorization threshold:

Less than 100 kWc

Renewable-energy installations at or above 100 kWc require installation and operating authorization from ARSE when operated for the producer's own needs.

Renewable-energy installations above 32 kWc and below 100 kWc are subject to the declaration regime.

Therefore, for a gravity-powered renewable self-consumption installation intended to remain below the authorization threshold, the maximum regulatory range is: less than 100 kWc .

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

Togo does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

Where the technology is classified within the renewable-energy framework, the renewable self-consumption thresholds apply.

For the proposed project, use less than 100 kWc as the maximum capacity below the renewable- energy authorization threshold.
