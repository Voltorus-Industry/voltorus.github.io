Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Eswatini and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in Eswatini is principally regulated by the Electricity Act, 2007 (Act No. 3 of 2007), together with the applicable electricity licensing regulations, by-laws and regulatory instruments administered by the Eswatini Energy Regulatory Authority (ESERA) .

The Electricity Act establishes licensing requirements for electricity generation and also provides a specific exemption mechanism for persons generating electricity for their own use.

Section 22 of the Electricity Act provides that a person who generates electricity for own use, where approved and authorised by the Authority, may be exempted from the obligation to apply for and hold a generation licence.

ESERA's current guidance specifically states that generators producing electricity exclusively for their own use with a generation capacity of 100 kW or above must apply for an exemption from the obligation to hold a generation licence.

Consequently, a purely private self-consumption installation with a rated generating capacity below 100 kW falls below the ESERA exemption threshold for own-use generation.

A completely isolated installation must nevertheless be assessed according to its rated generating capacity, electrical configuration, technology, location and applicable construction, environmental, safety and planning requirements.

Own-Use Generation Exemption

Section 22 of the Electricity Act provides that a person who generates electricity for own use, where approved and authorised by the Authority, may be exempted from the obligation to apply for and hold a licence.

ESERA's current guidance applies this exemption mechanism specifically to self-generation.

The exemption does not eliminate the need to comply with applicable technical, construction, electrical-safety, environmental and other legal requirements.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Eswatini public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No electricity is supplied to third-party consumers.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installation is documented as a private self-generation facility.
  • The rated generating capacity is documented precisely.
  • If the rated generating capacity is 100 kW or above, an exemption application is submitted to ESERA before operation.
  • If the project is intended to operate without an ESERA exemption application, the rated generating capacity is maintained below 100 kW.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, structural and safety requirements are independently verified.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

The Electricity Act does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The applicable regulatory treatment is therefore determined according to the installation's rated generating capacity, operating configuration and intended use.

The Electricity Act defines rated generating capacity as the designed maximum continuous output of the generating plant.

The proposed facility is intended to operate as an independent electrical island and to use all generated electricity internally.
