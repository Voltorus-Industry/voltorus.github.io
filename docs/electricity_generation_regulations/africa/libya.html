Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation and energy-storage facility located in Libya and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate as an isolated electrical system without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity network.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate requirements.

Libya's electricity sector remains strongly associated with the General Electricity Company of Libya (GECOL). Law No. 17 of 1984 established GECOL and assigned it responsibility for electricity production, transmission and distribution.

The Renewable Energy Authority of Libya has identified the need for a modern electricity-law framework capable of regulating renewable-energy activities and private participation in the sector.

No reliable nationwide kW exemption threshold specifically applicable to a private, completely isolated, gravity-powered self-consumption installation has been identified in the sources reviewed.

Administrative Requirements

The Libyan electricity framework does not provide, in the sources reviewed, a clear nationwide kW exemption specifically designed for an isolated private gravity-powered self-consumption facility.

The legal position must therefore be assessed with particular care before construction.

Electricity-Sector Framework

Law No. 17 of 1984 established GECOL and assigned the company responsibility for electricity production, transmission and distribution.

The Renewable Energy Authority of Libya has subsequently identified the need for legislation specifically governing renewable-energy activities and private-sector participation.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical separation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Install electrical protection preventing accidental energisation of the public network.
  • Obtain written confirmation from the competent Libyan electricity authorities before construction.
  • Verify construction, environmental and safety requirements independently.
Maximum Electrical Power
Libya-wide regulatory maximum identified:

No specific nationwide kW/MW exemption threshold for a completely off-grid private gravity-powered self-consumption installation has been reliably identified in the reviewed legislation and official sources.

Consequently, a specific kW value should not be entered as the legally permitted maximum for Libya.

Any proposed installation should be submitted to the competent electricity authorities for a project-specific determination before construction.
