Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in The Gambia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in The Gambia is regulated principally under the Electricity Act 2005, together with the regulatory framework administered by the Public Utilities Regulatory Authority (PURA).

The Electricity Act 2005 generally requires a licence for electricity generation, transmission, dispatch or distribution.

However, the Act expressly provides that a licence is not required for electricity generation solely for consumption at the site of the generating facility.

Consequently, a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation must be assessed under the specific site-generation exemption, while remaining subject to applicable construction, environmental, electrical safety and other legal requirements.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Act 2005 establishes a general licensing requirement for electricity generation.

However, section 5(2)(a) expressly provides that a licence is not required for electricity generation solely for consumption at the site of the generating facility.

On-site Self-consumption Exemption

The Electricity Act 2005 provides an exemption from the electricity-generation licensing requirement where electricity is generated solely for consumption at the site of the generating facility.

The exemption is therefore particularly relevant to a genuinely isolated self-consumption facility.

The legislation reviewed does not provide a numerical kW/MW ceiling in section 5(2)(a) for this specific site-consumption exemption.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The installation has no physical connection to the public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • Electricity is consumed exclusively at the site of the generating facility.
  • No electricity is supplied to external customers.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to the public grid.
  • The installation is documented as an on-site self-consumption facility.
  • The Electricity Act 2005 exemption is verified with the competent authority before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning and electrical safety requirements are independently verified.
Maximum Electrical Power
Verified numerical maximum for the specific self-consumption exemption:

No numerical kW/MW maximum was identified in section 5(2)(a) of the Electricity Act 2005 for electricity generation solely for consumption at the generating site.

The Electricity Act 2005 establishes a general generation-licensing requirement but expressly excludes electricity generation solely for consumption at the generating site from that requirement.

The legal text reviewed does not attach a specific installed-capacity figure to this exemption.

Therefore, it would be incorrect to insert an arbitrary value such as 100 kW, 500 kW or 1 MW as the legal maximum for this specific exemption.

Website classification for The Gambia:

No numerical capacity ceiling identified in the specific statutory site-consumption exemption.

The exemption must nevertheless be confirmed against the complete regulatory framework and the actual configuration of the proposed facility.

The maximum electrical capacity for the proposed gravity-powered facility should therefore be determined after written confirmation from the competent Gambian electricity authority rather than by assuming a numerical threshold that is not stated in the reviewed legislation.
