Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Sudan and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in Sudan is subject to the national electricity legislative and regulatory framework administered by the competent electricity authorities.

Sudan's electricity system includes interconnected generation as well as isolated generation systems, particularly in areas where network infrastructure is unavailable.

The available legal sources reviewed do not establish a sufficiently clear capacity-based exemption for a completely isolated private self-consumption generation facility that can safely be presented as a national statutory maximum.

The legal treatment must therefore be confirmed with the competent Sudanese electricity authority according to the generating capacity, technology, location, electrical configuration and intended use.

Administrative Requirements

Electricity-generation activities in Sudan are subject to the national electricity regulatory framework.

The legal sources reviewed do not provide a sufficiently clear capacity threshold that would justify classifying a completely isolated private generation plant as exempt solely because of its nominal generating capacity.

Isolated Self-Consumption Generation

The proposed facility would operate as an isolated private electricity-generation plant.

No physical connection to the public electricity network is intended.

Electricity would be used exclusively by the owner or operator.

The competent Sudanese electricity authority should confirm the applicable authorization requirements before construction, including any capacity-specific requirements.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Install electrical protection preventing accidental network connection.
  • Document the nominal electrical capacity.
  • Obtain written confirmation from the competent Sudanese electricity authority.
  • Verify construction, environmental, planning, structural and safety requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
Sudan — verified regulatory position:

No nationally defined capacity threshold verified for a completely isolated private self-consumption facility.

A specific MW value should not be presented as a statutory maximum without confirmation from the competent Sudanese authority.

Sudan has substantial installed electricity-generation capacity, including large hydroelectric and thermal facilities, but these figures represent national generation infrastructure and are not a statutory maximum for a private gravity-powered installation.

Small-hydropower classifications used in technical studies are also technology classifications and should not be confused with a legal authorization threshold.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

Sudanese electricity regulation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The proposed installation should therefore be assessed under the general electricity- generation regulatory framework.

The permissible maximum project capacity should be confirmed by the competent authority before construction.
