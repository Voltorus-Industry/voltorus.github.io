Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Tanzania and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in Tanzania is principally regulated by the Electricity Act, Cap. 131 and regulations administered by the Energy and Water Utilities Regulatory Authority (EWURA) .

The Electricity Act requires a licence for electricity generation unless the activity has been exempted by EWURA or falls within a statutory exemption.

EWURA currently states that electricity activities above 1 MW require a licence, while electricity activities below 1 MW are subject to the applicable registration framework.

Renewable-energy projects with an installed capacity at a single site below 1 MW may therefore fall within the exempted small-scale regime, subject to the applicable rules and registration requirements.

A completely isolated installation must nevertheless be assessed according to its nominal generating capacity, operating configuration, technology, location and intended use.

Administrative Requirements

Section 9 of the Electricity Act requires persons intending to undertake electricity generation to apply for a licence unless the person or activity has been exempted.

EWURA currently states that it issues licences for electricity activities above 1 MW and registration for electricity activities below 1 MW .

Renewable-energy generation projects below 1 MW may therefore qualify for the small-scale registration/exemption framework, subject to the applicable EWURA rules.

Isolated Self-Consumption Generation

The proposed installation would operate as a private isolated electricity-generation facility.

The facility would have no physical connection to the public electricity network.

Electricity would be generated solely for internal consumption.

For the purpose of the regulatory threshold, 1 MW is the relevant capacity limit separating small-scale generation from the licensed generation regime.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Install electrical protection preventing accidental network connection.
  • Keep the nominal generating capacity below 1 MW when relying on the small-scale threshold.
  • Complete any EWURA registration required for the applicable category.
  • Verify construction, environmental, planning, structural and safety requirements.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

Tanzanian electricity legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The applicable capacity regime is technology-neutral and depends on the legal classification of the electricity-generation activity.

For the proposed gravity-powered self-consumption facility, use less than 1 MW as the maximum capacity within the small-scale licence-exempt/registration threshold.
