Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Uganda and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Ugandan electricity generation is principally regulated by the Electricity Act, 1999 (Chapter 145), as amended.

Section 51 establishes the principal generation licensing threshold.

A person may not construct, own or operate a generating station with a capacity of or exceeding 0.5 MW (500 kW) without a generation licence, subject to the applicable statutory framework and any capacity subsequently determined by the Authority.

Accordingly, 500 kW is the relevant Ugandan threshold for the proposed gravity-powered electricity-generation installation.

Administrative Requirements

Section 51 of the Electricity Act establishes the generation licensing requirement.

No person may construct, own or operate a generating station with a capacity of or exceeding 0.5 MW without a generation licence.

A generating station below 0.5 MW therefore falls below the principal statutory generation-licence threshold.

Application to an Isolated Gravity Installation

The Electricity Act defines generation by reference to the production of electricity and does not establish a separate generation category specifically for gravity technology.

The fact that the installation is completely off-grid does not by itself establish a different generation-capacity threshold.

For the proposed installation, 500 kW is therefore the relevant maximum capacity below the principal generation-licence threshold.

  • No physical connection to the Ugandan public electricity network.
  • No electricity import.
  • No electricity export.
  • No electricity sale to third parties.
  • No electricity purchase from the public grid.
  • Electricity used exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Independent island-mode operation.
  • Electrical protection preventing accidental energisation of the public grid.
  • Precise documentation of the nominal generating capacity.
  • Regulatory verification before construction.
