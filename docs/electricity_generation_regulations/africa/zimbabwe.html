Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Zimbabwe and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Zimbabwean electricity generation is principally regulated by the Electricity Act [Chapter 13:19] , together with the Energy Regulatory Authority framework and electricity licensing regulations.

Section 40 of the Electricity Act establishes a licensing threshold of 100 kW for electricity undertakings.

The Zimbabwe Energy Regulatory Authority (ZERA) states that it regulates electricity undertakings generating, transmitting, distributing or retailing electricity for commercial purposes in excess of 100 kW.

Accordingly, 100 kW is the relevant Zimbabwean regulatory threshold for the proposed gravity-powered electricity-generation facility.

Administrative Requirements

Section 40 of the Electricity Act provides that no person may operate an electricity undertaking that generates, transmits, distributes or supplies electricity in excess of 100 kW without the appropriate licence.

ZERA's current Electricity Licensing Guidelines confirm that the licensing framework applies to electricity undertakings involved in generation, transmission, distribution and supply in excess of 100 kW.

Zimbabwe also maintains specific regulatory treatment for small generators used solely by a household or business.

Application to an Isolated Gravity Installation

Zimbabwean legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically for gravity-powered electricity generation.

The relevant threshold is determined by the electrical generating capacity and the legal status and use of the generating installation.

For a purely self-consumption installation, 100 kW is therefore the relevant capacity threshold for the licensing assessment.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • No physical connection to the Zimbabwean public electricity network.
  • No electricity import.
  • No electricity export.
  • No electricity sale to third parties.
  • No electricity purchase from the public grid.
  • Electricity used exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Independent island-mode operation.
  • Electrical protection preventing accidental energisation of the public grid.
  • Precise documentation of the nominal generating capacity.
  • Verification with ZERA before construction and operation.

This information is provided for general public information purposes only.
