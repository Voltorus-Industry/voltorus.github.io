Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Cabo Verde and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the public electricity network, with no electricity import, export or sale to third parties.

Cabo Verde regulates electricity generation through its electricity-sector legislation and licensing framework, including specific provisions for independent producers and self-producers.

Decreto-Lei No. 30/2006 established procedures for licensing independent producers and self-producers, while Decreto-Lei No. 1/2011 established a framework for independent and self-production from renewable energy sources.

A purely isolated installation must nevertheless be assessed with the competent energy authorities because the absence of a grid connection does not by itself establish a universal capacity exemption.

Administrative Requirements

Cabo Verde maintains a licensing framework for electricity production, including independent production and self-production.

  • Decreto-Lei No. 30/2006 establishes procedures for licensing independent producers and self-producers.
  • Decreto-Lei No. 1/2011 regulates the promotion, licensing and operation of independent production and self-production from renewable energy sources.
  • The general electricity regime requires the applicable production activity to be licensed by the competent energy authority.
  • Construction, environmental and technical requirements may apply independently.

No reliable nationwide capacity exemption specific to a gravity-powered, completely isolated installation was identified in the sources reviewed.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the grid.
  • Do not export or sell electricity.
  • Keep generation and consumption on the same private site.
  • Obtain a written classification from the competent energy authority before construction.
  • Document the installed electrical capacity and technology.
  • Verify environmental, construction and safety requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
Exact nationwide exemption threshold: Not identified in the authoritative sources reviewed for a completely off-grid gravity-powered self-consumption facility.

Cabo Verde has a licensing framework for self-production and independent production.

The available legislation confirms that self-production is a regulated activity, but the sources reviewed do not establish a single gravity-specific maximum electrical power below which the installation is automatically exempt.

The website should therefore not insert an invented kW figure. The project capacity should be submitted to the competent authority for confirmation before construction.

Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Cameroon and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The facility is intended to operate completely independently from the public electricity network.

Cameroon expressly regulates self-generation installations according to their installed capacity.

The official MINEE user guide identifies three relevant regimes for self-generation: freedom, declaration and authorisation.

This makes Cameroon one of the countries in this group for which a precise capacity classification can be established from the available regulatory documentation.

Administrative Requirements

The official MINEE documentation establishes the following self-generation thresholds.

  • Up to and including 100 kW: self-generation installations other than hydroelectric plants are not subject to legal or administrative formalities, provided they comply with approved standards and safety requirements.
  • More than 100 kW and less than 1 MW: prior declaration to the Electricity Sector Regulatory Agency is required.
  • More than 1 MW: the installation falls under the authorisation regime.

The documentation specifically excludes hydroelectric installations from the ≤100 kW administrative-freedom provision. A gravity-powered system using no hydroelectric resource should therefore be technically classified accordingly.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Keep the installation physically isolated from the public network.
  • Do not import electricity.
  • Do not export or sell electricity.
  • For a non-hydroelectric installation, keep the capacity at or below 100 kW if the objective is to remain within the documented freedom regime.
  • If capacity exceeds 100 kW and remains below 1 MW, complete the required declaration.
  • If capacity exceeds 1 MW, obtain the applicable authorisation before commissioning.
  • Verify construction, environmental and safety requirements independently.
Maximum Electrical Power
Maximum capacity identified for the administrative freedom regime:

100 kW

For self-generation installations other than hydroelectric plants, the MINEE guide states that installations with a capacity of 100 kW or less are not subject to legal or administrative formalities, provided they comply with approved standards and safety norms.

Above 100 kW and below 1 MW, a declaration is required.

Above 1 MW, an authorisation is required.

Accordingly, 100 kW is the precise capacity threshold to use on this website for a non-hydroelectric gravity-powered self-consumption installation seeking to remain within the documented freedom regime.
