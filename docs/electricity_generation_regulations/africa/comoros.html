Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in the Union of the Comoros and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The Comorian electricity framework has recently been supplemented by Decree No. 26-070/PR of 4 June 2026, concerning renewable-energy self-production and the organisation of electricity mini-grids.

The new framework addresses individual and collective renewable-energy self-production, renewable-energy self-production communities and mini-grids.

It also establishes a framework for possible injection of surplus electricity into the national network.

A completely isolated installation that neither imports nor exports electricity must nevertheless be assessed according to the detailed implementing provisions.

Administrative Requirements

Decree No. 26-070/PR of 4 June 2026 establishes a framework for:

  • individual renewable-energy self-production;
  • collective renewable-energy self-production;
  • renewable-energy self-production and energy management communities;
  • electricity mini-grids;
  • injection of surplus renewable electricity into the national network.

Because this decree is recent, the precise capacity thresholds and implementing procedures applicable to a specific gravity-powered installation should be verified against the implementing regulatory texts.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Keep the installation completely isolated from the national electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the network.
  • Do not inject surplus electricity.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively on the owner's site.
  • Obtain written confirmation of the applicable self-production regime.
  • Verify implementing regulations adopted under Decree No. 26-070/PR.

Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity-sector activities in the DRC are governed principally by the legislation relating to the electricity sector, including the 2014 electricity law and subsequent amendments and implementing provisions.

The DRC expressly recognizes the concept of an auto-producer, meaning a person or entity producing electricity for its own consumption.

The legal regime applicable to private self-production depends in particular on the installed capacity and whether the installation is established outside the public domain.

A completely isolated self-consumption installation must therefore be assessed according to the applicable capacity regime while also complying with technical, environmental, construction and safety requirements.

Administrative Requirements

The DRC does not treat every private electricity installation in the same manner. The applicable administrative regime depends principally on the installed capacity and the legal characteristics of the installation.

Capacity Regimes for Private Self-production

For an auto-producer operating outside the public domain, the legislation establishes several capacity categories.

  • Below 50 kW: private electricity-generating installations fall under the freedom regime, subject to the applicable technical and safety requirements.
  • 51–99 kW: an auto-producer must submit a written declaration to the local administration responsible for electricity.
  • 100–999.99 kW: an auto-production installation outside the public domain is subject to an authorization regime.
  • 1 MW and above: independent electricity production is generally subject to the licensing regime, depending on the precise legal classification and purpose of the facility.
Other Potential Administrative Requirements
  • Electricity-sector authorization or declaration where applicable.
  • Technical certification and compliance requirements.
  • Construction and building requirements.
  • Land-use and planning requirements.
  • Environmental requirements.
  • Electrical safety requirements.
  • Fire-safety requirements.
  • Structural engineering requirements.
  • Additional requirements associated with the particular technology and site.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The installation has no physical connection to the public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for the producer's own requirements.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to the public grid.
  • The installed capacity is clearly documented.
  • The applicable DRC capacity regime is verified before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning and technical requirements are independently verified.
Maximum Electrical Power
DRC self-production threshold without electricity-sector authorization:

Below 50 kW for a private electricity-generating installation established outside the public domain, under the freedom regime identified in the electricity-sector legislation.

Installations from 51 kW to 99 kW fall under a declaration regime, while installations from 100 kW to 999.99 kW fall under an authorization regime.

Consequently, for a purely off-grid self-consumption installation intended to remain within the freedom regime, the relevant maximum capacity is less than 50 kW.

Demonstrated Gravity-Technology Benchmark

The largest commercial-scale gravity-energy storage installation identified in operation is the Rudong EVx system in China.

Its rated power is 25 MW with an energy capacity of 100 MWh.

This 25 MW figure is a demonstrated technology benchmark and is not a DRC electricity-law exemption threshold.
