Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation and energy-storage facility located in Liberia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Liberia's electricity sector is regulated principally under the Electricity Law of Liberia (2015), with the Liberia Electricity Regulatory Commission (LERC) responsible for electricity-sector regulation, licensing and authorisation.

The Liberian regulatory framework expressly addresses self-supply electricity facilities and establishes capacity-based registration, authorization and permit requirements.

A genuinely isolated self-consumption installation must therefore be assessed according to the applicable self-supply rules, its installed generating capacity, electrical configuration and any additional construction, environmental and safety requirements.

Administrative Requirements

Liberia expressly regulates self-supply electricity facilities through capacity-based registration, authorization and permit requirements.

The LERC Electricity Licensing Handbook identifies the following capacity bands for electricity activities.

Self-Supply Capacity Requirements
  • Self-supply facilities from 10 kW to 100 kW require registration / authorization.
  • Self-supply facilities above 100 kW require a self-supply permit.
  • A self-supply facility above 2 MW is subject to a procedure similar to the issuance of a generation licence.

These thresholds concern self-supply regulation and should not be confused with the maximum physical power that the gravity technology can technically produce.

Other Potential Administrative Requirements
  • LERC registration, authorization or permit, where applicable.
  • Construction and building requirements.
  • Land-use requirements.
  • Electrical safety requirements.
  • Fire-safety requirements.
  • Environmental requirements.
  • Structural engineering requirements.
  • Occupational health and safety requirements.
  • Additional requirements depending on the mechanical and electrical technology used.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The installation has no physical connection to the Liberian public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • The installation operates in isolated island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to the public network.
  • The installed electrical capacity is documented precisely.
  • LERC requirements are verified before construction.
  • Construction, environmental and safety approvals are verified independently.
Maximum Electrical Power
Liberia self-supply regulatory threshold:

Self-supply facilities above 100 kW require a self-supply permit.

Facilities above 2 MW are subject to a procedure similar to the issuance of a generation licence.

The Liberia Electricity Licensing Handbook also identifies the 10 kW to 100 kW range as requiring registration / authorization for self-supply.

Therefore, the highest capacity band that remains below the self-supply permit threshold is 100 kW.

This does not mean that a gravity-powered installation is technically limited to 100 kW. It is a regulatory threshold for self-supply permitting.

Technology Reference

Gravity energy-storage technology has already been demonstrated commercially at significantly higher power levels. The Energy Vault EV1 installation in China has a reported discharge power of approximately 25 MW.

The 25 MW figure is a technology demonstration / commercial-project capacity and is not a Liberian regulatory authorization threshold.
