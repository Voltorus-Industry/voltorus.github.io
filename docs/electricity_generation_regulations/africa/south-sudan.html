Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in South Sudan and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

The electricity sector of South Sudan is regulated by the Ministry responsible for electricity and by the South Sudan Electricity Regulation Authority (SSERA) within the developing electricity regulatory framework.

South Sudan's electricity sector remains relatively underdeveloped, with limited transmission and distribution infrastructure and substantial use of isolated electricity-generation systems.

Available electricity-sector studies identify SSERA as the electricity-sector regulator responsible for licensing, tariffs and technical standards.

The available regulatory sources reviewed do not establish a sufficiently clear and currently verifiable national capacity threshold specifically exempting a completely isolated private self-consumption generation facility from electricity-generation authorization.

The project must therefore be assessed according to its nominal generating capacity, electrical configuration, technology, location, ownership, intended use and the authorization requirements applicable at the time of construction.

Administrative Requirements

South Sudan has been developing its electricity regulatory framework and institutional structure.

Available regulatory-sector information identifies SSERA as the authority responsible for electricity regulation and licensing.

The available sources reviewed do not provide a sufficiently clear national capacity threshold that can safely be used as a blanket exemption for a private, completely isolated electricity-generation facility.

Isolated Self-Consumption Generation

The proposed installation would operate as a private isolated electricity-generation facility.

The facility would have no physical connection to a public transmission or distribution network.

Electricity would be generated solely for internal consumption.

Because no sufficiently clear national capacity exemption was verified, the applicable authorization requirements should be confirmed directly with SSERA before construction.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from any public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from a public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to a public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Install electrical protection preventing accidental grid connection.
  • Document the nominal electrical generating capacity precisely.
  • Obtain written confirmation from SSERA before construction.
  • Verify construction, environmental, planning, structural and safety requirements separately.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

South Sudanese electricity regulation does not identify a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The project should therefore be assessed under the general electricity-generation regulatory framework.

The maximum project capacity should be determined following written confirmation from SSERA rather than by assigning an unsupported statutory MW limit.
