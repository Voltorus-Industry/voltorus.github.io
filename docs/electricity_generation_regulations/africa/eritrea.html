Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Eritrea and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate requirements.

Eritrea's principal electricity-sector legislation includes Electricity Proclamation No. 141/2004. The Proclamation regulates electricity operations, including generation, transmission, distribution and sale.

The Proclamation establishes a permit framework for electricity operations. No specific kW/MW exemption for a private isolated self-consumption generation installation was identified in the reviewed legislation.

Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Eswatini and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is completely isolated from the public electricity network and does not import or export electricity.

Eswatini's electricity sector is regulated principally under the Electricity Act, 2007 and by the Eswatini Energy Regulatory Authority (ESERA).

The legislation expressly regulates electricity generation and off-grid and mini-grid electricity supplies.

Importantly for an own-use project, ESERA states that electricity generators for own use are exempt from holding a generation licence, but a generator with a facility capacity of 100 kW or above must apply for an exemption.

Administrative Requirements

ESERA confirms that electricity generation is a regulated power-system activity.

However, generators producing electricity for their own use are exempted from the obligation to hold a generation licence.

ESERA further states that where the generation capacity is 100 kW or above, the own-use generator must apply for an exemption.

Own-Use Generation

The relevant regulatory distinction is therefore not simply whether the system is off-grid. The own-use status and installed generation capacity must be considered.

A facility below 100 kW should nevertheless be checked against the current ESERA licensing rules, technical requirements and other applicable legislation before construction.

Other Potential Requirements

  • Construction approval.
  • Electrical safety requirements.
  • Environmental requirements.

  • Structural engineering requirements.
  • Fire and occupational safety requirements.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Keep the facility physically isolated from the public electricity network.
  • Use electricity exclusively for own consumption.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Apply to ESERA for the applicable exemption when the facility is 100 kW or above.
  • Maintain electrical protection preventing accidental grid energisation.
Maximum Electrical Power
Eswatini own-use threshold: 100 kW is the threshold at which an own-use generator must apply for an exemption from the generation-licence obligation.

This should not be interpreted as a maximum permitted generation capacity. It is an exemption-application threshold.

Facilities of 100 kW or more can therefore be operated for own use subject to obtaining the applicable exemption and satisfying the regulatory requirements.
