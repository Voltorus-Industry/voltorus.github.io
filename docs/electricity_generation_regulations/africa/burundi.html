Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Burundi and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively by the owner for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements.

Burundi's electricity sector is governed principally by Law No. 1/13 of 23 April 2015 on the reorganisation of the electricity sector.

The legislation opened electricity generation to private operators, subject to the applicable administrative regime.

A completely isolated self-consumption installation must therefore be assessed according to its installed capacity, technology and actual use of the electricity, together with applicable environmental, construction, land-use and safety requirements.

Administrative Requirements

Burundi distinguishes electricity-generation installations according to their capacity.

  • Generation systems below 500 kW are subject to declaration.
  • Generation systems above 500 kW require authorisation.
  • Hydroelectric projects above 1 MW are subject to a concession regime.
  • Projects located on State-owned land can also require a public-private partnership arrangement.

These thresholds concern electricity-generation regulation generally and should not automatically be interpreted as a special exemption for every gravity-powered installation.

The project should therefore be classified according to its actual technology and installed electrical capacity before construction.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical separation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export or sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use the electricity exclusively on the same private site.
  • Document the installed electrical capacity.
  • Determine whether the project exceeds the 500 kW authorisation threshold.
  • Determine whether the installation constitutes a hydroelectric project exceeding 1 MW.
  • Verify environmental, construction, land-use and safety requirements before construction.
Maximum Electrical Power
Confirmed regulatory threshold:

500 kW is the relevant threshold identified for the distinction between declaration and authorisation for electricity-generation systems.

Under the electricity-sector framework, generation systems below 500 kW are subject to declaration, whereas systems above 500 kW require authorisation.

For hydroelectric projects, installations above 1 MW are subject to a concession regime.

Therefore, 500 kW should be used as the principal regulatory capacity threshold for a non-hydroelectric gravity-powered self-generation project, rather than presenting it as a maximum technically exploitable power.

Public Information Notice

This information is provided for general public information purposes only. The 500 kW and 1 MW figures identify regulatory thresholds found in the applicable electricity framework; they do not constitute a statement that a gravity-powered installation can technically produce that amount of electricity. The actual permissible project capacity must be verified with the competent Burundian authorities according to the technology, site, capacity and operating configuration.
