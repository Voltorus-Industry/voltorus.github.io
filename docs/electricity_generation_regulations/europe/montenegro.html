Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Montenegro and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Montenegro regulates electricity generation through its Energy Law and the regulatory framework supervised by the Energy and Water Regulatory Agency of Montenegro (REGAGEN).

The current Energy Law expressly identifies electricity production for own needs among activities that may be performed without an electricity licence.

The law also provides that electricity production by an energy entity in one or more energy facilities does not require a licence where the total installed capacity does not exceed 1 MW.

Administrative Requirements

Montenegro's Energy Law expressly provides that electricity production for own needs may be performed without an electricity-generation licence.

In addition, electricity production by an energy entity in one or more energy facilities with total installed capacity not exceeding 1 MW is identified among activities that may be performed without a licence.

  • Own-needs electricity production is expressly listed as a non-licensed activity.
  • Total installed capacity threshold identified: 1 MW.
  • Construction and planning requirements may apply.
  • Environmental requirements may apply.
  • Electrical safety requirements remain applicable.
  • Fire-safety requirements may apply.
  • Water-law requirements may apply where relevant.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain total installed generation capacity at or below 1 MW for the applicable non-licensed category.
  • Operate exclusively for own electricity needs.
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Prevent accidental energisation of the public electricity network.
  • Confirm the applicable construction and environmental permits before construction.
