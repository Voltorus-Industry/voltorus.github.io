Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in France and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

French electricity generation is principally governed by the Code de l'énergie .

Under Article L.311-1 of the Code de l'énergie, operation of a new electricity-production installation is generally subject to obtaining an administrative authorisation, subject to the exemption mechanism provided by Article L.311-6.

Article L.311-6 provides that electricity-production installations whose installed capacity is below or equal to a threshold depending on the type of energy used may be deemed authorised.

Article R.311-2 specifies numerical thresholds for particular energy technologies.

These include, for example, 50 MW for solar, wind and several specified renewable-energy technologies.

However, a gravity-powered electricity-generation installation using a mechanical/gravitational energy conversion principle is not expressly included among the technology categories listed in Article R.311-2.

Consequently, the 50 MW threshold applicable to certain listed renewable technologies cannot automatically be applied to a gravity-powered electricity-generation installation.

For the proposed technology, the applicable electricity-production authorisation must therefore be assessed under the general French electricity production framework and according to the actual technical classification of the installation.

Administrative Requirements

A completely off-grid configuration does not by itself remove the installation from the French electricity-production regulatory framework.

The electricity-production authorisation regime must be distinguished from construction, environmental, planning, water, electrical-safety and other regulatory requirements.

Electricity Production Authorisation

Article L.311-1 of the Code de l'énergie provides that operation of a new electricity production installation is subject to an administrative authorisation, subject to Article L.311-6.

Article L.311-6 provides a deemed-authorisation mechanism for installations at or below thresholds determined according to the type of energy used.

Article R.311-2 establishes those thresholds for specified technologies.

A gravity-powered electricity-generation technology is not expressly assigned a specific numerical threshold in the current list of Article R.311-2.

No Application of the 50 MW Renewable Threshold

Article R.311-2 contains a 50 MW threshold for several specifically identified technologies, including solar and wind installations.

That threshold is technology-specific.

It cannot simply be transferred to a gravity-powered electricity-generation installation merely because the project is described as low-carbon or renewable.

The actual primary-energy classification of the gravity system must therefore be confirmed with the competent French administration before construction.

Off-Grid Self-Consumption

The proposed installation would not be connected to the public electricity network.

No electricity would be imported from the public electricity network.

No electricity would be exported to the public electricity network.

No electricity would be sold to third parties.

Electricity would be consumed exclusively within the private installation controlled by the producer.

This operating configuration reduces the project's interaction with electricity-network rules but does not automatically eliminate electricity-production, construction, environmental or safety requirements.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The installation has no physical connection to the French public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installed electrical capacity is documented by a qualified electrical engineer.
  • The precise primary-energy classification of the gravity technology is formally established before construction.
  • The applicability of Article L.311-6 and Article R.311-2 is confirmed for the exact technology.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, fire-safety and electrical requirements are independently verified.

Article L.311-1 establishes the general rule that operation of a new electricity-production installation requires administrative authorisation, subject to the provisions of Article L.311-6.

Article L.311-6 refers to capacity thresholds depending on the type of energy used.

Article R.311-2 then identifies the technologies and corresponding thresholds.

The current Article R.311-2 list does not identify gravity-powered electricity generation as a separate technology category with its own numerical threshold.

Important Technology Classification

The following thresholds contained in Article R.311-2 should not automatically be applied to the proposed gravity technology:

  • Solar — 50 MW.
  • Wind — 50 MW.
  • Certain non-fossil combustion installations — 50 MW.
  • Biogas — 50 MW.
  • Aquifer and underground-rock energy — 50 MW.
  • Certain waste-energy installations — 50 MW.
  • Offshore wave/hydrothermal/hydrokinetic installations in the public maritime domain — 50 MW.

These are technology-specific statutory categories and do not establish a generic 50 MW threshold for every low-carbon electricity technology.
