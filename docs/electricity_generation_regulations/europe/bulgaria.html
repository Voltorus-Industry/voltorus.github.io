Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Bulgaria and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Bulgarian electricity production is principally regulated by the Bulgarian Energy Act (Energy Act) and, where the installation qualifies as a renewable energy installation, by the Energy from Renewable Sources Act.

The Energy Act establishes electricity production as an activity subject to licensing, while expressly providing exemptions for certain electricity production facilities.

The current Bulgarian Energy Act provides that an electricity production licence is not required for a person owning a power plant with a total installed electrical capacity of up to and including 20 MW .

The Energy Act also separately provides that a licence is not required for electricity generation for self-consumption only , including electricity generation by citizen energy communities and active customers.

A completely isolated self-consumption installation must therefore be assessed under both provisions, together with the applicable construction, spatial planning, environmental, electrical safety and technology-specific requirements.

Administrative Requirements

We should not treat a completely off-grid installation as automatically exempt from all Bulgarian administrative requirements.

The electricity-production licensing provisions, construction legislation, spatial-planning rules, environmental requirements and electrical safety requirements must be assessed separately.

Electricity Production Licence

Article 39 of the Bulgarian Energy Act lists electricity production among the activities subject to licensing.

However, Article 39(4)(1) expressly provides that an electricity production licence is not required for a person owning a power plant with a total installed electrical capacity of up to and including 20 MW .

This threshold was increased from 5 MW to 20 MW by the amendment published in State Gazette No. 11 of 2 February 2023.

Consequently, the current Bulgarian electricity-production licensing threshold is 20 MW , not 5 MW.

Self-Consumption Exemption

The Bulgarian Energy Act contains a separate exemption for electricity generation for self-consumption only .

Article 39(4)(5) provides that a licence is not required for electricity generation for self-consumption only, including electricity generation by citizen energy communities and active customers.

This provision is particularly relevant to the proposed operating configuration because the installation is intended to be physically isolated from the public electricity network.

No electricity would be imported, exported, sold or supplied to third parties.

The self-consumption exemption must nevertheless be read together with the construction, environmental, spatial-planning and technical requirements applicable to the project.

Renewable Energy Self-Consumption Rules

Where a generation installation qualifies as an electricity-production installation from a renewable energy source, additional provisions of the Energy from Renewable Sources Act may apply.

Article 25a of the Energy from Renewable Sources Act provides a special procedure for certain renewable-energy installations built on roofs, facades and associated properties in urbanised areas where the electricity is intended solely for own consumption.

Under that provision, the total installed capacity may be up to twice the supplied capacity of the customer but may not exceed 5 MW .

This 5 MW threshold is specific to the Article 25a renewable-energy self-consumption regime and must not be confused with the general electricity-production licensing threshold under Article 39 of the Energy Act.

Because the proposed technology is a gravity-powered system rather than automatically one of the renewable sources defined by the Bulgarian renewable-energy legislation, the applicability of Article 25a must be confirmed from the technical characteristics and legal classification of the actual installation.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Bulgarian public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No electricity is supplied to third parties.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installed electrical capacity is clearly documented and verified by a qualified electrical engineer.
  • The project is assessed under Article 39 of the Bulgarian Energy Act before construction.
  • The separate self-consumption exemption under Article 39(4)(5) is documented where applicable.
  • The 20 MW installed-capacity threshold is checked against the applicable production-licensing provisions.
  • Where the installation qualifies as renewable energy, the applicability of the Energy from Renewable Sources Act is separately verified.
  • Construction, environmental, spatial-planning, water, fire-safety and technical requirements are independently verified before construction.

Total installed electrical capacity

The current Bulgarian Energy Act provides that an electricity-production licence is not required for a person owning a power plant with a total installed electrical capacity of up to and including 20 MW .

This is the current threshold following the amendment published in State Gazette No. 11 of 2 February 2023, which increased the previous threshold from 5 MW to 20 MW.

The Bulgarian Agency for Sustainable Energy Development confirms in its renewable-energy procedures handbook that a production licence is required only for electricity-production facilities with a total installed electrical capacity above 20 MW .

Installation up to 20 MW

A power plant with a total installed electrical capacity of up to and including 20 MW does not require the electricity-production licence referred to in Article 39 of the Energy Act.

This threshold applies to the general electricity-production licensing exemption.

Other permits and administrative requirements may nevertheless apply to construction and operation of the facility.

Installation above 20 MW

Where a power plant has a total installed electrical capacity above 20 MW , the general exemption in Article 39(4)(1) no longer applies.

Electricity production then falls within the ordinary production-licensing framework of the Bulgarian Energy Act, unless another statutory exemption applies.

The separate statutory exemption for electricity production for self-consumption only must also be assessed under Article 39(4)(5).

Special Renewable Self-Consumption Threshold

A separate 5 MW threshold exists under Article 25a of the Energy from Renewable Sources Act for certain renewable-energy self-consumption installations located on or associated with buildings in urbanised areas.

Under this specific regime, the total installed capacity may be up to twice the supplied capacity but may not exceed 5 MW .

This 5 MW threshold is not the general electricity-production licensing threshold and should only be used where the project satisfies the specific conditions of Article 25a.
