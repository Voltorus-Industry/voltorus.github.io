Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Luxembourg and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate as a completely isolated electrical system, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity network.

Luxembourg electricity-sector activities are governed principally by the amended Law of 1 August 2007 on the organisation of the electricity market.

Luxembourg legislation distinguishes between electricity production and electricity supply to final customers.

The ILR identifies a ministerial authorisation requirement for the construction of new production capacity based on non-renewable energy sources or combined heat and power above 10 MW.

However, the reviewed legislation does not provide a general numerical exemption specifically for a completely isolated gravity-powered electricity generation installation.

Administrative Requirements

Luxembourg's electricity authorisation framework differentiates between electricity production, electricity supply and the characteristics of the generation technology.

  • Electricity-production authorisation requirements must be assessed according to the actual technology.
  • The 10 MW threshold identified by ILR concerns new production capacity based on non-renewable sources or combined heat and power.
  • The 10 MW threshold must not automatically be applied to a gravity-powered installation.
  • Construction and building requirements may apply.
  • Environmental requirements may apply.
  • Electrical safety requirements remain applicable.
  • Planning and municipal requirements may apply.
  • Water-law requirements may apply where the technology uses water.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Prevent accidental connection to the public electricity network.
  • Obtain confirmation of the applicable authorisation regime before construction.
Maximum Electrical Power
Luxembourg — gravity-powered technology:

No specific numerical maximum has been identified in the reviewed Luxembourg legislation for a completely isolated gravity-powered electricity generation installation operated exclusively for self-consumption.

The ILR identifies a threshold of more than 10 MW for the ministerial authorisation concerning the construction of new production capacity based on non-renewable sources or combined heat and power.

This threshold is technology-specific and therefore cannot be treated as the maximum capacity applicable to a gravity-powered installation.

The competent Luxembourg authority should therefore classify the proposed gravity technology before a numerical exemption threshold is entered into the comparative database.
