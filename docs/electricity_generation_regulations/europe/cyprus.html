Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Cyprus and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity generation in Cyprus is regulated by the Laws Regulating the Electricity Market and the regulatory framework administered by the Cyprus Energy Regulatory Authority (CERA).

The Cypriot electricity licensing framework distinguishes between electricity generation for commercial purposes, electricity generation for own use, generation using renewable energy sources and generation from autonomous power plants that are not connected to the transmission or distribution system.

CERA's current General Licence framework expressly includes electricity generation from power plants that are not connected to the transmission or distribution system.

CERA also provides a specific licence-exemption regime for electricity generation for own use from systems with an electrical capacity greater than 30 kW and up to 1 MW .

For renewable-energy electricity generation, CERA provides a separate exemption framework covering renewable-energy power plants from more than 50 kW up to and including 8 MW , subject to the conditions of that regime.

A completely isolated gravity-powered installation must therefore be classified according to its actual technology and energy source. The 1 MW own-use threshold is the appropriate principal capacity figure for a generic non-RES self-consumption classification, while the 8 MW threshold applies specifically to the statutory RES exemption.

Administrative Requirements

We should not treat an off-grid installation as automatically exempt from all Cypriot administrative requirements.

Cyprus has a specific regulatory framework for autonomous electricity-generation installations, electricity generation for own use and renewable energy generation.

Autonomous / Off-Grid Generation

Article 27 of the Laws Regulating the Electricity Market provides for a General Licence covering generation of electricity from power plants that are not connected to the transmission or distribution system.

CERA's current General Licence information also expressly identifies Autonomous generator as a General Licence category.

This is directly relevant to the proposed configuration because the facility is intended to operate as a completely independent electrical island.

The autonomous status must nevertheless be established through the applicable CERA regulatory procedure and does not remove construction, environmental, safety or other statutory requirements.

Electricity Generation for Own Use

CERA provides a specific exemption from the obligation to hold a licence for generation of electricity for own use from systems with a capacity greater than 30 kW and up to 1 MW .

CERA's current licensing information expressly identifies this category as an exemption from the obligation to hold a licence.

This regime is particularly relevant where electricity is generated exclusively for the producer's own requirements.

The 1 MW threshold is therefore the principal capacity figure to use for a generic non-RES own-use installation.

Renewable Energy Generation

CERA provides a separate exemption regime for electricity generation stations using renewable energy sources.

The current regime covers electricity generation stations using any RES technology with an installed capacity from 50 kW up to and including 8 MW .

CERA's current licensing information states that construction and operation licences apply to RES generation stations exceeding 8 MW .

However, the proposed gravity-powered system should not automatically be classified as RES merely because gravity is used as the mechanical operating principle.

The 8 MW threshold should therefore only be applied if the actual installation qualifies as a renewable-energy power plant under Cypriot legislation.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Cypriot transmission or distribution network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No electricity is supplied to third parties.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates as an autonomous electrical island.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installed electrical capacity is documented and verified by a qualified electrical engineer.
  • The 1 MW own-use threshold is checked where the installation is classified as a non-RES self-consumption generator.
  • If the installation is claimed to use a renewable energy source, the RES classification is verified separately.
  • The applicability of the CERA autonomous-generator General Licence is verified before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, fire-safety and technical requirements are independently verified.

Principal Figure for Generic Own-Use Generation

For a generic electricity-generation facility operated exclusively for the producer's own use, the principal capacity threshold identified by CERA is 1 MW .

The specific exemption applies to own-use systems with capacity greater than 30 kW and up to 1 MW.

This is the figure that should be used in the comparative country table for a generic gravity-powered self-consumption installation, unless the project is legally classified under the separate RES framework.

Renewable-Energy Classification

If the gravity-powered installation qualifies under Cypriot law as a renewable-energy electricity-generation station, a different capacity regime applies.

CERA provides an exemption for RES generation stations with installed capacity from 50 kW up to and including 8 MW .

RES generation stations exceeding 8 MW fall within the construction and operation licensing regime.

The 8 MW threshold must therefore only be used when the gravity-generation technology actually qualifies as RES under the applicable Cypriot legislation.

Autonomous Off-Grid Generation

Article 27(1) provides for a General Licence covering generation of electricity from power plants that are not connected to the transmission or distribution system.

CERA's current General Licence framework also expressly lists Autonomous generator as a General Licence category.

Consequently, the completely off-grid configuration is legally significant and should be assessed independently from grid-connected generation.

The applicable CERA licence category must be confirmed from the final technology, energy-source classification and installed electrical capacity before construction.

Under the current Cypriot electricity regulatory framework, CERA provides a specific exemption from the obligation to hold a licence for electricity generation for own use from systems with capacity greater than 30 kW and up to 1 MW. CERA also provides a separate exemption regime for renewable-energy power plants with installed capacity greater than 50 kW and up to 8 MW. In addition, electricity generation from power plants that are not connected to the transmission or distribution system is included in the General Licence framework for autonomous generation. For a generic gravity-powered electricity-generation installation, the 1 MW threshold should be used as the principal comparative value for the own-use non-RES classification. A gravity-powered mechanical system should not automatically be classified as renewable energy merely because gravity is the operating mechanism. Construction, planning, environmental, electrical safety, fire-safety, structural and other administrative requirements may apply independently of the electricity-production licence or exemption.
