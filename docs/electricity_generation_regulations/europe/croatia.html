Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Croatia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Croatian electricity production is principally regulated by the Electricity Market Act (Zakon o tržištu električne energije), together with the Energy Act and other legislation applicable to energy infrastructure.

The Electricity Market Act regulates electricity production, transmission, distribution, supply, market activities and the rights and obligations of electricity producers and consumers.

Croatian legislation expressly allows electricity production without an electricity-production licence in certain circumstances, including production using generating facilities whose combined installed capacity is up to and including 1 MW .

The same provision separately allows electricity production exclusively for the producer's own needs without an electricity-production licence.

A completely isolated self-consumption installation must therefore be assessed primarily under the electricity-production exemption, together with applicable construction, spatial-planning, environmental, electrical-safety, fire-safety and technology-specific requirements.

Administrative Requirements

We should not treat a completely off-grid installation as automatically exempt from all Croatian administrative requirements.

The electricity-production licensing provisions must be considered separately from construction, spatial-planning, environmental, technical and safety requirements.

Electricity Production Licence

Under Article 10 of the Croatian Electricity Market Act, electricity production is normally an electricity-market activity requiring the appropriate authorisation.

Article 10(2), however, provides an exemption allowing legal or natural persons to carry out electricity production without an electricity-market activity licence where they ensure professional management and operation of the energy facilities in accordance with the applicable technical requirements.

One of the express exemptions covers electricity production using generating facilities whose total installed capacities are up to and including 1 MW .

This establishes 1 MW as the principal installed-capacity threshold relevant to the general licence exemption.

Self-Consumption Exemption

Article 10(2) contains a separate exemption for electricity production exclusively for the producer's own needs .

This is particularly relevant to the proposed configuration because the installation is intended to be completely isolated from the public electricity network.

Under the proposed configuration:

  • No electricity would be imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity would be exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity would be sold to third parties.
  • Electricity would be used exclusively for the producer's own requirements.

The own-needs exemption therefore provides a particularly relevant legal basis for a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation.

Renewable Energy Considerations

Croatia has a separate legislative framework for electricity produced from renewable energy sources and high-efficiency cogeneration.

The Croatian renewable-energy legislation contains specific rules concerning self-consumption, renewable-energy consumers and electricity-production installations.

However, a gravity-powered generation system should not automatically be classified as a renewable-energy installation merely because gravity is used as the mechanical principle for electricity generation.

The legal classification depends on the actual source of the energy used by the installation.

If the installation uses a renewable energy source recognized by Croatian legislation, the renewable-energy self-consumption provisions may additionally apply.

For a generic gravity-powered facility, the Electricity Market Act should therefore be used as the principal electricity-production licensing reference unless the actual technology qualifies separately under the renewable-energy legislation.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Croatian public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No electricity is supplied to third parties.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in electricity balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The total installed electrical capacity is documented and verified by a qualified electrical engineer.
  • The 1 MW installed-capacity threshold is checked against the applicable electricity-production licensing provisions.
  • The separate exemption for electricity production exclusively for own needs is documented and assessed.
  • If the technology is claimed to use renewable energy, the renewable-energy classification is verified separately.
  • Construction, environmental, spatial-planning, water, fire-safety and technical requirements are independently verified before construction.

Total installed electrical capacity

Article 10(2) of the Croatian Electricity Market Act permits electricity production without an electricity-market activity licence where the production facilities have a combined installed capacity of up to and including 1 MW , subject to the statutory technical-management requirements.

The same Article 10(2) separately permits electricity production exclusively for the producer's own needs without the electricity-production licence.

Installation up to 1 MW

A production installation or combination of production facilities with a total installed capacity of up to and including 1 MW falls within the statutory electricity production licence exemption.

The producer must nevertheless ensure professional management and operation of the energy facilities in accordance with applicable technical requirements.

Construction, spatial-planning, environmental, electrical and safety requirements remain separately applicable.

Completely Isolated Self-Consumption

The Croatian legislation contains a separate exemption for electricity production exclusively for the producer's own needs .

This exemption is particularly relevant where the installation is completely isolated from the public electricity network and does not import or export electricity.

The legal analysis should therefore distinguish between:

  • the general 1 MW installed-capacity exemption; and
  • the separate exemption for production exclusively for own needs.

The 1 MW figure should consequently be treated as the principal capacity threshold for the general licensing analysis rather than as a technology-specific physical limit on the proposed gravity-generation system.

Renewable Self-Consumption — Separate Regime

Croatia's renewable-energy legislation contains additional rules for electricity produced from renewable sources for self-consumption.

The current legislation also provides a specific 6 MW limit for certain renewable-energy self-consumption production installations located at a remote location behind other metering points.

This 6 MW threshold applies only to that specific statutory renewable-energy self-consumption configuration.

It should not be substituted for the general 1 MW electricity-production licence threshold when assessing a generic, completely isolated gravity-powered electricity-generation facility.
