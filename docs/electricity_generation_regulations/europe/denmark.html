Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Denmark and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Danish electricity production is principally regulated by the Danish Electricity Supply Act (Elforsyningsloven) together with regulations issued under that Act and other legislation governing energy, construction, environment and safety.

The Danish Energy Agency (Energistyrelsen) is responsible for the relevant national electricity-production authorisation framework.

The current Danish Electricity Supply Act contains an explicit installed-capacity threshold for electricity-production concessions.

Under Section 10 of the current Electricity Supply Act, electricity production from installations with a capacity of more than 25 MW may only be operated by companies that have obtained the required concession from the Minister for Climate, Energy and Utilities.

The Danish Energy Agency confirms that electricity production from installations exceeding 25 MW requires authorisation.

Consequently, 25 MW is the principal Danish installed-capacity threshold for the electricity-production concession framework. The completely isolated self-consumption configuration must nevertheless be assessed together with the specific rules applicable to the installation and its technology.

Administrative Requirements

We should not treat an off-grid installation as automatically exempt from all Danish administrative requirements.

Electricity-production concession requirements must be considered separately from construction, environmental, planning, safety and technology-specific requirements.

Electricity Production Concession

Section 10 of the Danish Electricity Supply Act provides that electricity production from installations with a capacity above 25 MW may only be carried out by companies that have obtained the required concession from the Minister for Climate, Energy and Utilities.

The concession is granted for at least 20 years .

The applicant must demonstrate the necessary technical and financial capacity to perform the concession-based electricity-production activity.

The current Danish regulatory framework therefore establishes 25 MW as the principal installed-capacity threshold for the electricity-production concession requirement.

Completely Off-Grid Generation

The proposed installation is intended to have no physical connection to the Danish transmission or distribution network.

Electricity would be generated and consumed entirely within the private electrical installation controlled by the producer.

No electricity would be injected into the Danish public electricity network.

The Danish Electricity Supply Act's electricity-production rules must nevertheless be considered when determining whether the facility constitutes an electricity-production installation subject to the statutory authorisation framework.

The final legal assessment should therefore confirm the status of the autonomous facility with the Danish Energy Agency before construction.

Self-Consumption

The facility is intended exclusively to supply electricity to the producer's own household, industrial or corporate electricity demand.

No electricity would be sold or supplied to third parties.

No electricity-market participation would be undertaken.

The facility would therefore operate as a private autonomous electricity system rather than as a public electricity-generation installation supplying the Danish electricity network.

The self-consumption configuration should be documented technically and contractually so that the installation cannot accidentally export electricity to the public network.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Danish transmission or distribution network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No electricity is supplied to third parties.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installed electrical capacity is documented and verified by a qualified electrical engineer.
  • The 25 MW concession threshold is checked against the current Electricity Supply Act.
  • The autonomous nature of the installation is documented before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, fire-safety and technical requirements are independently verified.

Installed electrical capacity

Section 10 of the current Danish Electricity Supply Act provides that electricity production from installations with a capacity above 25 MW requires a concession from the Minister for Climate, Energy and Utilities.

The Danish Energy Agency confirms that electricity production from installations exceeding 25 MW requires authorisation.

Accordingly, 25 MW is the principal Danish installed-capacity threshold for the production-concession framework.

Installation up to 25 MW

Electricity production from an installation with a capacity of up to and including 25 MW does not fall within the Section 10 electricity-production concession requirement applicable to installations above 25 MW.

This does not mean that the project is exempt from every other Danish permit, approval or technical requirement.

Construction, environmental, planning, electrical-safety and other requirements must still be assessed.

Installation above 25 MW

Electricity production from an installation exceeding 25 MW requires the electricity-production authorisation specified by the Danish Electricity Supply Act.

The applicant must demonstrate sufficient technical and financial capacity.

The Danish Energy Agency states that installations above 25 MW located on land require authorisation before electricity production can be carried out.

The concession is normally granted for a minimum period of 20 years under Section 10.
