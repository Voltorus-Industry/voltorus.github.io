Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in the Russian Federation and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Russian electricity legislation regulates electricity generation, transmission, distribution, supply and the operation of electricity-generation facilities.

The principal federal framework is established by Federal Law No. 35-FZ "On the Electric Power Industry".

A completely isolated installation must therefore be assessed according to the characteristics of the generating facility, its operating configuration, electrical parameters, construction requirements and applicable safety and energy-supervision rules.

Administrative Requirements

Off-grid operation does not by itself establish a general exemption from Russian electricity, safety, construction or industrial requirements.

Federal legislation regulates electricity-generation facilities and their operation. Government Decree No. 85 establishes rules concerning permission for commissioning certain electricity-generation facilities.

Federal Electricity Framework

Federal Law No. 35-FZ establishes the legal framework governing the Russian electricity sector.

Electricity-generation facilities can be subject to technical, operational and supervisory requirements depending on their characteristics and operating configuration.

A completely isolated installation should therefore be assessed independently from a conventional grid-connected generating plant, but should not be presumed exempt solely because it does not exchange electricity with the grid.

Electricity-Generation Facility Thresholds

Russian legislation contains several different capacity thresholds for electricity-generation facilities.

In particular, a generating facility with a capacity of 500 kW or more falls within specific regulatory categories used for electricity-generation facilities and reporting.

However, the 500 kW figure is not a general nationwide exemption from all permits for an isolated self-consumption installation.

The applicable authorization must therefore be determined from the actual electrical characteristics and regulatory category of the proposed facility.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The installation has no physical connection to the Russian public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No electricity trading or market participation is undertaken.
  • The installation operates as an electrically isolated island.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental energisation of the public grid.
  • The generating capacity and electrical parameters are documented precisely.
  • The applicable Russian electricity and commissioning requirements are verified before construction.
Maximum Electrical Power
Regulatory threshold identified:

500 kW is a documented Russian regulatory threshold for specific categories of electricity-generation facilities.

This figure must not be interpreted as a universal exemption from all Russian authorization requirements. The actual authorization requirement depends on the characteristics and operating configuration of the installation.

Russian legislation does not provide a single simple capacity exemption specifically created for a gravity-powered, completely isolated, self-consumption-only generating facility.

The 500 kW threshold is therefore the principal numerical regulatory threshold identified for classification purposes, but the proposed installation must be checked against the applicable commissioning and energy-supervision rules.
