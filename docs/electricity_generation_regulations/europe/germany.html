Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Germany and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

German electricity installations are principally governed by the Energiewirtschaftsgesetz (EnWG) , together with electrical-safety, construction, environmental and other applicable legislation.

The German framework does not establish a general nationwide MW licensing threshold specifically for an isolated gravity-powered electricity-generation installation.

The applicable requirements depend on the actual installation, its technology, location, environmental effects, construction characteristics and whether it is connected to the public electricity system.

The Bundesnetzagentur operates the Marktstammdatenregister (MaStR) , which is the central register for electricity and gas market master data.

The German framework also contains specific thresholds for particular regulatory purposes. These thresholds must not be confused with a general generation authorisation limit.

In particular, the 1 MW threshold found in the Electricity Tax implementing legislation applies to specific tax treatment for electricity generated from specified renewable technologies. It is not a general 1 MW construction or electricity-generation licence threshold for a gravity-powered installation.

Administrative Requirements

A completely isolated installation must not be assumed to be exempt from German administrative requirements solely because it does not interact with the public electricity grid.

Electricity-market rules must be considered separately from construction, environmental, planning, electrical-safety, fire-safety and occupational requirements.

Electricity Generation and Self-Consumption

German law recognises installations operated for the producer's own electricity requirements.

Section 37 of the EnWG expressly addresses operators of energy-generation installations used to cover their own requirements.

The proposed installation would be completely isolated and would not rely on public-grid supply.

The absence of a public-grid connection significantly changes the applicability of network-connection and electricity-supply requirements, but does not eliminate construction, safety or environmental requirements.

Technical Safety

Section 49 of the EnWG requires energy installations to be constructed and operated so that technical safety is ensured.

The generally recognised rules of technology must be observed.

For electricity installations, compliance with the recognised technical rules of the Verband der Elektrotechnik Elektronik Informationstechnik e.V. is relevant to the statutory presumption of compliance.

The gravity generator therefore requires an appropriate electrical and mechanical safety design regardless of whether it is connected to the public network.

Environmental Authorisation

The Bundes-Immissionsschutzgesetz (BImSchG) provides an authorisation regime for installations that are capable of causing harmful environmental effects or otherwise significantly affecting the public or neighbouring areas.

Whether a particular gravity-powered installation falls within a BImSchG authorisation category depends on its actual design and operation.

A gravity-powered installation should therefore be assessed against the applicable environmental and construction regimes rather than against an assumed electricity-production MW threshold.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the German public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No balancing-market or aggregation activity is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The electrical installation complies with the applicable technical safety rules.
  • The mechanical structure is designed and certified according to the applicable German engineering and safety requirements.
  • The precise environmental classification of the installation is established before construction.
  • Applicable federal-state and municipal construction requirements are verified.

Important 1 MW Distinction

German electricity-tax legislation contains a 1 MW threshold in §10 of the Stromsteuer-Durchführungsverordnung.

That provision concerns electricity generated from specified sources including wind, solar, geothermal and hydropower and relates to general permission for tax-privileged electricity uses.

It does not establish a general 1 MW electricity-generation licence threshold for a gravity-powered installation.

The 1 MW figure must therefore not be entered into the project's German generation-capacity field as though it were a general licensing ceiling for gravity technology.

100 kW EnWG Distinction

Section 13a of the EnWG contains obligations concerning operators of electricity-generation or storage installations with a rated capacity from 100 kW and certain remotely controllable installations.

This provision concerns adjustment of active or reactive power at the request of transmission-system operators.

It is therefore a system-operation provision and does not constitute a 100 kW construction or generation-authorisation limit for an isolated private installation.
