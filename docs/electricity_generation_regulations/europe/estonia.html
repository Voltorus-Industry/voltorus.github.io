Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Estonia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Estonian electricity generation is principally regulated by the Electricity Market Act (Elektrituruseadus) .

The Electricity Market Act regulates the generation, storage, transmission, sale, export, import and transit of electricity and the technical and economic management of the electricity system.

The Estonian Competition Authority (Konkurentsiamet) is responsible for the relevant electricity-sector activity-licensing framework.

The current Estonian framework contains a specific capacity exemption from the authorisation requirement for electricity generation.

The current regulatory information published by the Estonian Competition Authority states that the authorisation obligation does not apply when electricity is generated by a generating installation with a net capacity of up to 1 MW .

The Electricity Market Act also contains a separate definition under which generation by a generating installation with a total net capacity not exceeding 100 kW that is not connected to the electricity system is excluded from the statutory definition of "generation".

For the comparative electricity-production classification, the relevant current Estonian authorisation threshold is therefore 1 MW , while the completely isolated configuration has an additional statutory distinction at 100 kW .

Administrative Requirements

We should not treat a completely off-grid installation as automatically exempt from all Estonian administrative requirements.

The electricity-sector authorisation framework must be considered separately from construction, environmental, planning, electrical-safety and technology-specific requirements.

Electricity Generation Authorisation

The Electricity Market Act establishes an authorisation framework for electricity generation.

The current Estonian Competition Authority guidance states that the authorisation obligation does not apply when electricity is generated by a generating installation having a net capacity of up to 1 MW .

The relevant capacity is the net capacity of the generating installation.

Accordingly, 1 MW is the principal Estonian capacity figure to use for the electricity-generation authorisation classification.

Completely Off-Grid Generation

The Electricity Market Act contains a specific definition of generation that excludes electricity generated by a generating installation with a total net capacity not exceeding 100 kW when the installation is not connected to the electricity system.

This provision is particularly relevant to the proposed autonomous configuration.

The proposed installation is intended to have no physical connection to the Estonian electricity system.

The 100 kW statutory off-grid distinction and the 1 MW authorisation threshold should therefore both be recorded when assessing the legal status of the project.

Self-Consumption

The proposed facility would produce electricity exclusively for its own internal electricity requirements.

No electricity would be sold to third parties.

No electricity would be exported to the public electricity system.

No electricity would be imported from the public electricity system.

The installation would therefore operate as an autonomous private electricity system.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The installation has no physical connection to the Estonian electricity system.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No electricity aggregation or balancing-market participation is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to the public electricity system.
  • The net electrical capacity is documented by a qualified electrical engineer.
  • The 1 MW authorisation threshold is verified against the current Electricity Market Act.
  • Where the installation is at or below 100 kW and completely disconnected from the electricity system, the specific statutory off-grid definition is documented.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, fire-safety and electrical requirements are independently verified.

The Electricity Market Act additionally contains a specific statutory distinction for generating installations with total net capacity not exceeding 100 kW that are not connected to the electricity system.
