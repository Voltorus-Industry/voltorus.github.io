Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Poland and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Polish electricity generation is regulated principally by the Energy Law (Prawo energetyczne), together with legislation governing renewable energy sources, construction and environmental protection.

Article 32 of the Energy Law establishes a concession requirement for electricity-generation business activity, while providing specific exclusions.

For electricity sources with an aggregate installed electrical capacity not exceeding 50 MW that are not classified as renewable-energy installations or cogeneration units, the generation activity is excluded from the concession requirement.

Administrative Requirements

Article 32 of the Polish Energy Law requires a concession for electricity-generation business activity subject to the statutory exceptions.

One important exception applies to electricity sources with total installed electrical capacity not exceeding 50 MW when the sources are not renewable-energy installations and are not cogeneration units.

A gravity-powered system cannot automatically be classified as renewable electricity merely because it is low-carbon. The actual primary energy source and technical design must be assessed.

  • 50 MW threshold for the specified non-OZE/non-cogeneration category.
  • OZE classification may result in a different regulatory regime.
  • Construction requirements may apply.
  • Environmental requirements may apply.
  • Planning requirements may apply.
  • Electrical safety requirements apply.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Keep the aggregate installed electrical capacity at or below 50 MW if the installation qualifies for the non-OZE/non-cogeneration exception.
  • Confirm the legal classification of the gravity-powered technology before relying on the 50 MW threshold.
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use generated electricity exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Verify construction and environmental permits independently from the electricity concession.
Maximum Electrical Power
Poland — applicable non-OZE/non-cogeneration threshold:

50 MW aggregate installed electrical capacity

Article 32(1)(1)(b) of the Energy Law excludes from the generation-concession requirement electricity generation in sources having a total installed electrical capacity not exceeding 50 MW where those sources are not classified as renewable energy installations and are not cogeneration units.

The 50 MW figure is therefore the appropriate statutory capacity threshold for a gravity-powered installation only if the proposed technology falls within that non-OZE/non-cogeneration category.

If the technology is legally classified as an OZE installation, the OZE-specific rules must instead be applied.

For the comparative database, the Polish value should therefore be recorded as: 50 MW — non-OZE/non-cogeneration category.
