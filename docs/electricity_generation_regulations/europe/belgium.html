Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Belgium and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Belgian electricity legislation distinguishes electricity production from electricity transmission, distribution and supply and establishes a specific federal framework for electricity production.

Belgian electricity regulation is divided between federal and regional authorities. The federal framework applies in particular to electricity production, while the Regions are responsible for important matters concerning environmental, planning, construction and local energy regulation.

The federal Law of 29 April 1999 on the organisation of the electricity market establishes the principal framework applicable to electricity production.

The Belgian framework expressly defines an "autoproducer" as a natural or legal person producing electricity mainly for its own use. Therefore, the fact that an installation is intended exclusively for internal self-consumption does not by itself remove the installation from the electricity-production regulatory framework.

A completely isolated self-consumption installation must therefore be assessed as an electricity production installation while separately verifying the applicable regional construction, environmental, planning, safety and site-specific requirements.

Administrative Requirements

We should not treat a completely off-grid installation as automatically exempt from Belgian electricity-production requirements.

Belgian law expressly includes an "autoproducer" within the definition of a producer. Consequently, electricity generated primarily for the producer's own use remains relevant to the electricity-production regulatory framework.

Federal Electricity Production Framework

Under Article 4 of the Law of 29 April 1999 on the organisation of the electricity market, the establishment of new electricity production installations is in principle subject to prior individual authorisation.

The King may, under the conditions established by the applicable regulations, exempt low-power electricity production installations from the individual authorisation requirement and subject them instead to a prior declaration procedure.

The current CREG guidance identifies the applicable exemption threshold as a net developable capacity of 25 MWe .

New electricity production installations with a net developable capacity less than or equal to 25 MWe are exempt from the individual federal production authorisation.

However, installations within this threshold remain subject to a prior declaration to the CREG and the federal Minister for Energy or the minister's delegated authority.

25 MWe Federal Threshold

The relevant federal threshold for a new electricity production installation is:

Maximum power covered by the federal authorisation exemption:

25 MWe
Net developable capacity

An installation with a net developable capacity of up to and including 25 MWe does not require the individual federal electricity-production authorisation referred to in Article 4 of the Law of 29 April 1999.

Instead, the installation must be subject to the applicable prior declaration procedure to the CREG and the federal Minister for Energy or the delegated authority.

An installation exceeding 25 MWe falls outside this federal authorisation exemption and requires an individual production authorisation.

Regional Requirements

The federal 25 MWe threshold does not replace regional permitting requirements.

Belgium is divided into three Regions for important aspects of environmental, planning, construction and local energy regulation:

  • Flemish Region
  • Walloon Region
  • Brussels-Capital Region

Depending on the location and characteristics of the installation, additional permits, authorisations, notifications or environmental approvals may therefore be required.

The regional requirements must be checked independently from the federal 25 MWe electricity-production threshold.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Belgian public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in electricity balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installation is documented as a private self-consumption facility.
  • The net developable electrical capacity is determined and documented in accordance with the applicable Belgian regulatory methodology.
  • The applicable federal declaration procedure is completed where the installation has a net developable capacity of up to 25 MWe.
  • An individual federal production authorisation is obtained where the installation exceeds 25 MWe.
  • The applicable regional environmental, construction and planning requirements are verified before construction.
  • The project's technology-specific safety, structural, environmental and operational requirements are independently verified.

Net developable capacity

The current Belgian federal electricity-production framework establishes a specific threshold of 25 megawatts electrical (MWe) of net developable capacity.

New electricity production installations with a net developable capacity less than or equal to 25 MWe are exempt from the individual federal electricity-production authorisation.

This exemption does not mean that the installation can be constructed without administrative formalities. A prior declaration must be submitted to the CREG and to the federal Minister for Energy or the competent delegated authority.

Installation up to 25 MWe

Where the net developable capacity is ≤ 25 MWe , the installation benefits from the federal exemption from the individual production authorisation.

The applicable prior declaration procedure remains required.

The 25 MWe threshold is therefore the key federal capacity threshold to use for the classification of the proposed installation.

Installation above 25 MWe

Where the net developable capacity exceeds 25 MWe , the installation is outside the federal exemption and requires an individual electricity-production authorisation.

The authorisation is issued by the federal Minister responsible for Energy following the procedure involving the CREG.

The relevant federal capacity threshold for a new electricity-production installation is 25 MWe of net developable capacity.

Installations with a net developable capacity of ≤ 25 MWe are exempt from the individual federal production authorisation but remain subject to the applicable prior declaration procedure.

For a gravity-powered electricity-generation project, the 25 MWe threshold should therefore be used as the principal federal electricity-production threshold. The project's exact permitting position must then be assessed according to the Region, technology, installation design, site, environmental characteristics and other applicable legislation.
