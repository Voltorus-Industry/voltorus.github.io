Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Greece and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate as a completely isolated electrical system, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity network.

Greek electricity production is principally governed by Law 4001/2011, together with the subsequent electricity licensing framework introduced by Law 4685/2020 and Law 4819/2021.

Electricity production is generally subject to the applicable licensing regime unless the installation falls within a statutory exemption.

Greece also has a specific regulatory framework for renewable-energy self-consumption.

The available legislation does not establish a single numerical electricity-generation threshold specifically applicable to a completely isolated gravity-powered electricity-generation installation.

Administrative Requirements

The absence of a public-grid connection does not automatically eliminate the requirement to assess electricity-production licensing.

  • Applicable electricity-production licensing requirements.
  • Renewable-energy rules where the gravity system legally qualifies as a renewable-energy source.
  • Building and planning requirements.
  • Environmental requirements.
  • Electrical safety requirements.
  • Fire and structural safety requirements.
  • Water-law requirements where water is used.

No specific national MW threshold has been identified in the applicable electricity-production legislation that can be directly assigned to a completely isolated gravity-powered generation facility.

Numerical thresholds applicable to particular renewable technologies must not automatically be transferred to gravity-powered technology.

The project's maximum legally exploitable capacity must therefore be established from the legal classification of the actual gravity technology and its applicable licensing category.

Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Hungary and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the public electricity network.

Hungarian electricity generation is governed principally by Act LXXXVI of 2007 on Electricity (VET) and its implementing regulations.

Hungarian law distinguishes between large power plants and small power plants according to installed nominal capacity.

Electricity generation in a small power plant with nominal capacity of at least 0.5 MW requires the applicable combined small-power-plant licence.

Administrative Requirements

Under Section 4 of Act LXXXVI of 2007, electricity generation requires the applicable generation licence according to the installed nominal capacity.

  • Below 0.5 MW: outside the specific small-power- plant licensing threshold of Section 4(1).
  • From 0.5 MW: combined small-power-plant licence regime applies.
  • From 50 MW: producer operating licence regime applies.
  • Construction and planning requirements may apply independently.
  • Environmental and safety requirements may apply depending on the installation.
Maximum Electrical Power
Hungary — maximum capacity below the small-power-plant licence threshold:

0.5 MW

Section 4(1) of Act LXXXVI of 2007 provides that electricity generation in a small power plant with nominal capacity of at least 0.5 MW requires the applicable combined licence.

Accordingly, for the purpose of a conservative comparative database, the maximum capacity below this licensing threshold is 499.999 kW .
