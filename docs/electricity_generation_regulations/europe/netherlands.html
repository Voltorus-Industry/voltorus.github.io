Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in the Netherlands and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

The Dutch electricity framework is governed principally by the Electricity Act framework as replaced and supplemented by the Energiewet and by the Omgevingswet environmental and planning framework.

The current Energiewet identifies national-level project decision thresholds for electricity-production installations.

For installations producing renewable electricity other than wind or solar electricity, the relevant national threshold is 50 MW.

Administrative Requirements

The Dutch legal framework distinguishes between electricity-sector regulation, environmental regulation and spatial-planning procedures.

The Energiewet establishes a national project threshold of 50 MW for the construction or expansion of a production installation for renewable electricity generation other than wind and solar.

The threshold concerns the statutory project-decision framework and should not automatically be interpreted as a general generation-licence exemption.

  • National threshold identified: 50 MW.
  • The threshold applies to renewable electricity production other than wind and solar.
  • The legal classification of gravity-powered electricity must be established.
  • Environmental and planning requirements may apply.
  • Construction requirements may apply.
  • Electrical safety requirements remain applicable.
  • Water-law requirements may apply where the installation uses water resources.
Measures to Reduce Administrative Risk
  • Determine whether the gravity-powered technology qualifies legally as renewable electricity generation.
  • Keep the project below 50 MW if the objective is to remain below the identified national project threshold.
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use generated electricity exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Obtain confirmation from the competent Dutch authority concerning the applicable environmental and planning procedure.
Maximum Electrical Power
Netherlands — identified national threshold:

50 MW

Applicable to a production installation for renewable electricity generation other than wind or solar, under the national project-decision framework.

Article 5 of the Energiewet identifies a national project category for the construction or expansion of a production installation, including its connection to a system, with a capacity of at least 50 MW where the installation produces renewable electricity other than wind or solar electricity.

Gravity-powered electricity is not automatically classified as renewable electricity merely because the technology is low-carbon.

The 50 MW threshold should therefore be used for the comparative database only where the proposed gravity-powered technology is legally classified within the renewable-electricity category covered by the Energiewet.

The figure to retain for the Netherlands under that classification is 50 MW.
