Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Portugal and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Portuguese electricity production and self-consumption are regulated principally by Decreto-Lei n.º 15/2022, as amended.

The Directorate-General for Energy and Geology (DGEG) distinguishes between renewable electricity production for self-consumption at different installed-capacity levels.

For renewable self-consumption installations, production above 30 kW and up to 1 MW is subject to prior registration and an operating certificate, while production above 1 MW requires a production and operating licence.

Administrative Requirements

DGEG distinguishes between prior registration and licensing according to installed capacity for renewable self-consumption.

Renewable self-consumption production with installed power greater than 30 kW and up to 1 MW requires prior registration and an exploitation certificate.

Renewable self-consumption production above 1 MW requires a production and exploitation licence.

  • ≤30 kW: simplified category under the applicable self-consumption framework.
  • >30 kW and ≤1 MW: prior registration and exploitation certificate.
  • >1 MW: production and exploitation licence.
  • Construction requirements may apply.
  • Environmental requirements may apply.
  • Planning requirements may apply.
  • Electrical safety requirements apply.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Keep installed power at or below 1 MW if the project is intended to remain below the production and exploitation licence threshold for renewable self-consumption.
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use generated electricity exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Confirm that the gravity-powered source qualifies legally as a renewable energy source before using the renewable self-consumption thresholds.

Above 1 MW, a production and exploitation licence is required.

DGEG states that renewable electricity production for self-consumption with installed power greater than 30 kW and up to 1 MW is subject to prior registration and an exploitation certificate.

DGEG further states that renewable self-consumption production with installed power above 1 MW requires a production and exploitation licence.

The 1 MW figure is therefore the relevant capacity threshold for the simplified renewable self-consumption regime.

This classification applies only if the proposed gravity-powered technology is legally treated as a renewable energy source under Portuguese law.
