Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Latvia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the public electricity network.

Latvian electricity production is principally governed by the Electricity Market Law and associated regulations.

Latvian legislation distinguishes microgeneration from larger electricity-generation installations.

The identified microgenerator threshold is 11.1 kW for the relevant grid-connected microgeneration regime.

Administrative Requirements

The Latvian microgeneration framework is principally designed around installations operating in parallel with the distribution system.

The official Latvian description identifies microgeneration equipment for household self- consumption operating at no more than 400 V and 16 A.

This corresponds to approximately 11.1 kW for a three-phase 400 V system.

However, this threshold should not be confused with a general capacity ceiling for a completely isolated industrial or corporate gravity-powered installation.

Maximum Electrical Power
Latvia — identified microgeneration threshold:

11.1 kW

The identified Latvian microgeneration regime uses an operating voltage not exceeding 400 V and total operating current not exceeding 16 A.

The resulting three-phase apparent-power value is approximately 11.1 kVA, commonly expressed in the regulatory material as an 11.1 kW microgeneration threshold.

This 11.1 kW threshold applies to the identified microgeneration/grid-parallel framework and should not be represented as a universal maximum for every isolated gravity-powered electricity facility.
