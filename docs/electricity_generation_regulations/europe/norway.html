Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Norway and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Norway's electricity infrastructure is regulated principally by the Energy Act (Energiloven) and the regulations adopted under that Act.

The Norwegian concession regime for electrical installations is principally based on the electrical voltage of the installation rather than a general MW threshold applicable to all generation technologies.

The current Energy Regulations provide that electrical installations above 1,000 V AC or 1,500 V DC are subject to the concession regime under the Energy Act, subject to specific technology-based provisions and exceptions.

Administrative Requirements

The Energy Act establishes the general concession framework for electrical installations.

The Energy Regulations specify that installations above 1,000 V AC or 1,500 V DC are concessionary installations.

The 1 MW threshold applicable to certain wind installations and the 10 MW threshold applicable to certain solar installations are technology-specific and are not applicable to a gravity-powered facility.

  • Electrical voltage must be assessed.
  • Construction and planning requirements may apply.
  • Environmental requirements may apply.
  • Electrical safety requirements apply.
  • Fire-safety requirements may apply.
  • Water and hydrological requirements may apply depending on the technology.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Design the facility as a completely isolated electrical island.
  • Prevent any physical connection to the public electricity network.
  • Assess whether any part of the electrical installation exceeds 1,000 V AC or 1,500 V DC.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Verify the concession classification with NVE before construction.

The Norwegian Energy Act establishes concession requirements for electrical installations.

The implementing Energy Regulations define concessionary electrical installations principally according to voltage, with the general threshold of more than 1,000 V AC or 1,500 V DC.

The 1 MW threshold for land-based wind installations and the 10 MW threshold for land-based solar installations are specific to those technologies.

They must not be used as a maximum for a gravity-powered installation. For the comparative database, the Norwegian criterion should therefore be recorded as: voltage-based concession regime; no gravity-specific MW threshold identified.

Public Information Notice

This information is provided for general public information purposes only. Norway does not provide a general MW threshold specifically applicable to gravity-powered generation in the legislation reviewed. The principal general concession criterion for electrical installations is voltage. The exact concession status of the proposed installation should therefore be confirmed with NVE based on its voltage, configuration and technology.
