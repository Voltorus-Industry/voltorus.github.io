Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in the Czech Republic and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Electricity production in the Czech Republic is principally regulated by Act No. 458/2000 Coll., the Energy Act (Energetický zákon).

The Czech Energy Regulatory Office (Energetický regulační úřad — ERÚ) is responsible for electricity-sector licensing and regulatory administration.

The current Czech Energy Act contains specific installed-capacity rules governing when an electricity-production licence is required.

Under the legislation applicable from 1 July 2026 , a licence is required for electricity production in a generating facility with an installed capacity above 100 kW when the facility is connected to the transmission or distribution system, subject to the additional cases specified by the Energy Act.

The same legislation provides that a licence is not granted for electricity production in a generating facility with installed capacity of up to and including 100 kW , except in the statutory situations where licensing remains mandatory.

The Czech framework therefore uses 100 kW as the principal current installed-capacity threshold for the general licence exemption.

A completely isolated self-consumption installation must nevertheless be assessed separately because the Czech licensing provisions distinguish between grid-connected generation, self-consumption and the electricity actually delivered or shared through an electricity market connection.

Administrative Requirements

We should not treat a completely off-grid installation as automatically exempt from all Czech administrative requirements.

The electricity licensing provisions must be considered separately from construction, planning, environmental, electrical-safety and other technical requirements.

Electricity Production Licence

Under the Czech Energy Act, persons conducting business in the energy sectors generally require a licence granted by the Energy Regulatory Office.

The current legislation provides a specific rule for electricity production in generating facilities connected to the transmission or distribution system.

A licence is required for electricity production in a generating facility with installed capacity above 100 kW where the generating facility is connected to the transmission or distribution system, subject to the other mandatory licensing situations defined by law.

The legislation also provides that a licence is not granted for electricity production in generating facilities with installed capacity up to and including 100 kW , except for the statutory exceptions.

Accordingly, 100 kW is the current principal capacity threshold to use in the Czech comparative classification.

Completely Off-Grid Generation

The proposed facility has no physical connection to the Czech transmission or distribution system.

This distinction is important because the Czech Energy Act expressly connects several electricity-production licensing requirements to connection with the transmission or distribution system.

The proposed configuration is therefore an autonomous electrical island rather than a grid-connected generating facility.

No electricity would be delivered into the public electricity network.

The final classification should nevertheless be confirmed with ERÚ where the facility is operated as a commercial energy activity, particularly if the installed capacity exceeds the applicable exemption threshold.

Self-Consumption

The proposed installation is intended to produce electricity exclusively for the producer's own requirements.

No electricity will be sold to third parties and no electricity will be supplied through the public electricity system.

The current Czech Energy Act specifically distinguishes small generating facilities according to their installed capacity and their connection and electricity-delivery configuration.

The 100 kW threshold should therefore be applied together with the actual technical configuration rather than in isolation.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, We recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Czech transmission or distribution system.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No electricity is supplied to third parties.
  • No electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No electricity aggregation or balancing-market participation is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to the public grid.
  • The installed electrical capacity is documented and verified by a qualified electrical engineer.
  • The 100 kW threshold is verified against the current Energy Act.
  • The project's commercial status is verified, particularly where the facility is operated as an energy-sector business.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, fire-safety and technical requirements are independently verified.

Installed electrical capacity

Under the Czech Energy Act applicable from 1 July 2026 , the principal installed-capacity threshold for the electricity-production licence exemption is 100 kW .

The current legislation provides that electricity production in a generating facility with installed capacity up to and including 100 kW is within the licence exemption, subject to the statutory exceptions.

For generating facilities connected to the transmission or distribution system, a licence is specifically required where the installed capacity exceeds 100 kW , subject to the additional cases established by the Energy Act.

Installation up to 100 kW

An electricity-generation facility with installed capacity of up to and including 100 kW falls within the current statutory electricity-production licence exemption, subject to the conditions and exceptions contained in the Energy Act.

This is the principal figure that should be used for the Czech Republic in the comparative database.

Installation above 100 kW

Above 100 kW , the electricity-production licence framework must be assessed.

For a grid-connected facility, the Energy Act expressly requires a licence for electricity production above 100 kW, subject to the statutory exceptions.

For a completely isolated installation, the absence of a transmission or distribution connection must also be considered in the legal classification.
