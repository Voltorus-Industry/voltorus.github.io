Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in Malta and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public grid.

Electricity generation in Malta is regulated by the Regulator for Energy and Water Services (REWS) under the Electricity Supply Regulations and related electricity legislation.

Maltese regulations provide a specific exemption from the authorisation to construct a generation station and from the requirement to hold a generation licence for certain small generation installations.

The exemption expressly covers a producer with total peak generation capacity rated up to and including 16 Amps per phase, single-phase or multi-phase, at 230/400 V AC, where the electricity is produced solely from renewable sources of energy or by a cogeneration plant.

The exemption is subject to notification to the Regulator before construction.

Administrative Requirements

Maltese regulations provide an exemption from the requirement to obtain an authorisation to construct the generation station and from the requirement to hold a generation licence for the specified small generation category.

The exemption applies where the producer has total peak generation capacity rated up to and including 16 Amps per phase, at 230/400 V AC, and the electricity is produced solely from renewable sources of energy or by a cogeneration plant.

The producer must nevertheless notify the Regulator before construction and provide the information requested by the Regulator.

  • Maximum current: 16 A per phase.
  • Supply voltage: 230/400 V AC.
  • Notification to REWS before construction.
  • Electrical safety requirements remain applicable.
  • Construction requirements may apply.
  • Planning requirements may apply.
  • Environmental requirements may apply.
  • Fire-safety requirements may apply.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Keep the installation within the statutory 16 A-per-phase threshold.
  • Ensure that the technology legally qualifies as renewable-energy generation or cogeneration for purposes of the exemption.
  • Maintain complete isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Notify REWS before construction as required.
  • Install appropriate electrical protection against accidental energisation of the public network.
Maximum Electrical Power
Malta — statutory exemption threshold:

16 A per phase

At 400 V three-phase, this corresponds to approximately:

11.1 kVA

The Maltese regulation expresses the exemption in electrical current rather than directly in kW.

For a three-phase 400 V system:

S = √3 × 400 V × 16 A

S ≈ 11.1 kVA

The legal threshold itself remains 16 A per phase , not a fixed kW value.

Furthermore, the exemption applies only where the generation is solely from renewable sources of energy or from a cogeneration plant.

Therefore, a gravity-powered system should only use the 16 A-per-phase exemption after confirming that the Maltese regulator considers the specific technology to fall within the renewable-energy or cogeneration category.
