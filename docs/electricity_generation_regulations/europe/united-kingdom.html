Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity generation facility located in the United Kingdom and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the public electricity network.

No electricity would be imported from or exported to the public electricity network.

Electricity would be used exclusively by the producer for internal household, industrial or corporate requirements.

Electricity generation in Great Britain is regulated under the Electricity Act 1989, subject to statutory licence exemptions.

The most relevant exemption for a private self-consumption installation is the Class A generation exemption, subject to its detailed conditions.

Administrative Requirements

Electricity generation is generally a licensable activity under the Electricity Act 1989 unless the generating activity falls within an applicable statutory or individual exemption.

Class A — Small Generator Exemption

The Class A exemption applies to certain small generating stations.

The exemption provides a threshold of 50 MW of electrical power from a generating station where the declared net capacity of the generating station is below 100 MW , subject to the conditions of the exemption.

Power supplied to a single consumer or qualifying group of consumers on the same site can be treated differently under the exemption conditions.

This makes the Class A exemption particularly relevant to on-site private generation and self-consumption projects.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical separation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use the electricity exclusively on the producer's site.
  • Ensure the installation satisfies the conditions of the applicable Class A exemption.
  • Keep the declared net capacity below the applicable Class A limit.
  • Obtain planning, environmental, construction and safety approvals where applicable.
Maximum Electrical Power
United Kingdom — Class A generation exemption:

50 MW output threshold

Under the Class A generation exemption, a generating station with a declared net capacity below 100 MW can fall within the exemption where it does not provide more than 50 MW of electrical power from the generating station, subject to the detailed statutory conditions.

Electricity supplied to qualifying consumers on the same site is treated specifically by the exemption rules and can be disregarded for the relevant threshold calculation.

Class A generation exemption threshold, subject to the statutory conditions and the requirement that the generating station's declared net capacity is below 100 MW.
