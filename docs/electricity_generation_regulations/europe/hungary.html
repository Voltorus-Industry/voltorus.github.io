Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Hungary and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the producer.

Hungarian electricity generation is principally regulated by Act LXXXVI of 2007 on Electricity (VET — Villamos energiáról szóló törvény) and its implementing legislation.

The Hungarian electricity framework establishes specific authorization requirements according to the nominal generating capacity and operating configuration of the electricity-generation facility.

Hungarian law expressly recognizes an "önellátó termelő egység" (self-supplying generating unit), which is subject to specific authorization provisions.

The legislation provides a principal capacity threshold of 0.5 MW (500 kW) for electricity-generation authorization requirements applicable to self-supplying generating units.

A completely isolated installation must therefore be assessed according to its nominal generating capacity, electrical configuration, technology, location and applicable construction, environmental, safety and planning requirements.

Administrative Requirements

Hungarian electricity legislation establishes authorization requirements for electricity-generation installations according to their nominal generating capacity.

Under the current Electricity Act, electricity generation in a generating plant with a nominal capacity of 50 MW or more requires a producer's operating licence or, where applicable, a self-supplying producer licence.

A small power plant with a nominal capacity of 0.5 MW or more requires the applicable small power plant combined licence or, where the installation qualifies as a self-supplying generating unit, a self-supplying producer licence .

The current legislation specifically states that the construction and operation of a self-supplying generating unit at or above the applicable capacity threshold is subject to authorization.

Self-Supplying Generating Unit

Hungarian law defines an "önellátó termelő egység" as a self-supplying generating unit.

The current statutory definition identifies such a unit by reference to a connection capacity exceeding 0.5 MW and a configuration involving connection to the public network without injection capacity, with the generated electricity being shared or sold to users connected at the same connection point.

For a genuinely isolated installation with no physical connection whatsoever to the public electricity network, the exact legal classification should therefore be confirmed with the Hungarian Energy and Public Utility Regulatory Authority.

The 500 kW threshold nevertheless remains the principal electricity-generation authorization threshold identified in the current Hungarian Electricity Act.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to the Hungarian public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from the public grid.
  • No electricity is exported to the public grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from the public grid.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in electricity balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of the public grid.
  • The installation is documented as a private self-consumption facility.
  • The nominal electrical capacity is documented precisely.
  • The applicable Hungarian electricity-generation authorization requirements are verified before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, planning, water, structural and safety requirements are independently verified.
Maximum Electrical Power
Hungary — principal capacity threshold:

500 kW (0.5 MW)

Under the Hungarian Electricity Act, a generating installation with a nominal capacity of 0.5 MW or more falls within the electricity-generation authorization regime.

The current Hungarian Electricity Act provides that electricity may be generated in a small power plant with a nominal capacity of 0.5 MW or more only with the applicable authorization.

The same legislation specifically provides an authorization framework for self-supplying generating units .

Accordingly, for a gravity-powered electricity generation project intended for self-consumption, 500 kW is the relevant capacity threshold to use when determining whether the Hungarian electricity generation authorization regime is triggered.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

Hungarian electricity legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The applicable capacity threshold is therefore determined according to the legal classification of the electricity-generation installation rather than by the word "gravity" itself.

For the proposed project, the relevant threshold is 500 kW (0.5 MW) for the electricity-generation authorization regime.

Website classification for Hungary:

500 kW

Use 500 kW as the Hungarian regulatory capacity threshold for the gravity-powered electricity-generation facility.

A facility at or above this nominal capacity is subject to the applicable Hungarian electricity generation authorization regime.

The 500 kW figure is a legal capacity threshold for the authorization regime and should not be interpreted as a technology-specific mechanical or electrical performance limit for gravity generation.
