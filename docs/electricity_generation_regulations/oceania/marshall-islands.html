Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in the Republic of the Marshall Islands and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from any public electricity network.

No electricity would be imported from or exported to an external public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, commercial, research or corporate requirements controlled by the operator.

The Marshall Islands does not appear to have a comprehensive national electricity-generation licensing statute comparable to the electricity licensing acts found in several other Pacific jurisdictions.

The former Alternative Energy Fund Act of 1989 established a revolving fund for the development, marketing and operation of alternative energy systems.

However, the Alternative Energy Fund Act was repealed by the Alternative Energy Fund (Repeal) Act 2008 (P.L. 2008-13). It therefore should not be presented as a current generation licence.

Administrative Requirements

The legal framework identified for electricity in the Marshall Islands differs from jurisdictions having a comprehensive electricity-generation licensing act.

An energy-sector assessment published for the Republic of the Marshall Islands states that the applicable framework did not include an Electric Power Act regulating MEC or KAJUR.

Alternative Energy Fund Act

The Alternative Energy Fund Act of 1989 created a revolving fund for development, marketing and operation of alternative energy systems.

This legislation was repealed by the Alternative Energy Fund (Repeal) Act 2008. It therefore cannot be relied upon as a current statutory authorization or licence for electricity generation.

Maximum Electrical Power
Marshall Islands — statutory capacity threshold:

No current national MW generation threshold identified.

No current national electricity-generation statute establishing a general MW threshold for private off-grid self-consumption has been identified in the legal sources reviewed.

The former Alternative Energy Fund Act 1989 did not establish a general electricity-generation capacity licensing threshold and was repealed in 2008.

Consequently, a specific MW figure should not be presented as a statutory maximum for an isolated self-consumption facility in the Marshall Islands without identifying a more specific current law applicable to the proposed location and activity.

Public Information Notice

The Republic of the Marshall Islands does not appear, from the legislation reviewed, to have a comprehensive national electricity-generation licensing statute comparable to the Electricity Acts of several other Pacific countries. The Alternative Energy Fund Act 1989 concerned financing and operation of alternative energy systems but was repealed in 2008. No current national statutory MW capacity limit specifically governing isolated private electricity generation has been identified. Project-specific environmental, construction, business, safety, land-use and utility requirements must therefore be verified before construction or operation.
