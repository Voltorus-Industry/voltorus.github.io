Overview

We are considering a private electricity-generation facility located in New Zealand and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is designed to operate as an isolated electrical system with no intended physical connection to the public electricity network.

Electricity would not be imported from or exported to the public grid and would not be sold to third parties.

The principal electricity legislation includes the Electricity Act 1992 and the Electricity Industry Act 2010.

The Electricity Industry Act 2010 establishes the regulatory framework for the electricity industry and identifies generators as industry participants.

The Electricity Act 1992 contains important electrical safety and electricity-work requirements.

No general statutory electricity-generation MW threshold equivalent to a universal generation licence threshold was identified in the provisions reviewed for an isolated private self-consumption installation.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Industry Act 2010 regulates participation in the electricity industry.

The Electricity Industry Participation Code 2010 contains rules governing the electricity industry.

The Electricity Act 1992 establishes electrical safety obligations, electrical codes of practice and requirements concerning electrical work.

  • Determine whether the installation falls within the statutory definition of an electricity generator or electricity supply system.
  • Verify applicable Electricity Authority requirements.
  • Establish the required electrical safety management system where section 61A applies.
  • Ensure electrical work is performed by appropriately authorised persons.
  • Verify building and planning requirements.
  • Verify Resource Management Act requirements where applicable to the site and project.
  • Verify fire and occupational safety requirements.
Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Keep the installation physically isolated from the national grid.
  • Do not export electricity to a network.
  • Do not purchase electricity from a network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively within the controlled private installation.
  • Install electrical protection preventing unintended grid energisation.
  • Prepare the applicable safety management system.
  • Document the generation capacity and electrical architecture.
  • Verify all planning, building, environmental and electrical safety requirements before construction.
Maximum Electrical Power
New Zealand — general statutory generation capacity threshold:

No universal MW generation-licence threshold identified in the provisions reviewed

The New Zealand electricity legislation reviewed does not establish a simple universal rule under which a private generator automatically requires a generation licence solely because its electrical capacity exceeds a particular MW value.

Regulatory treatment depends on the nature of the electricity activity, network connection, electricity market participation, electrical safety obligations, land-use requirements and other applicable legislation.

The project capacity should therefore be stated as project-specific and assessed under the applicable electricity, electrical safety, planning and environmental framework.

Public Information Notice

New Zealand regulates electricity generation through the Electricity Industry Act 2010, Electricity Act 1992, the Electricity Industry Participation Code and related electrical safety requirements. The legislation reviewed does not establish a universal MW threshold that alone determines whether an isolated private self-consumption generator may operate. The precise obligations depend on the configuration of the installation, whether it connects to or supplies a network, the applicable electricity-safety regime, planning requirements and other legislation.
