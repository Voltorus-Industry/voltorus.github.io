Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Fiji and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from any public electricity network and is not intended to import electricity from or export electricity to the public grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, commercial, research or corporate requirements controlled by the operator.

Fiji regulates the electricity industry principally under the Electricity Act 2017. The Act establishes a regulatory framework for generation, transmission, distribution and supply of electricity.

Part 2 of the Electricity Act 2017 contains specific provisions concerning licensing and self-supply.

Section 16 specifically addresses self-supply. A person intending to self-supply must provide details to the public electricity supplier, subject to the statutory mechanism contained in that section.

Administrative Requirements

Section 7 of the Electricity Act 2017 establishes a prohibition on unlicensed electricity supply, subject to the exemptions provided by the Act.

Section 9 establishes the process for electricity licences, while section 10 permits conditions to be attached to licences.

Self-supply — Section 16

Section 16(1) provides that a person intending to self-supply, unless that self-supply existed before commencement of the Act, must provide details of the self-supply to the public electricity supplier.

If the public electricity supplier determines that it can provide the generation and supply at the price and terms legitimately demonstrated as possible by the person, the public electricity supplier may elect to supply on those terms.

If the public electricity supplier elects to supply under section 16(1), the person must not self-supply under section 16(2).

Installation and Safety Requirements

The Electricity Regulations 2019 contain licensing and registration provisions for electrical installations.

The regulations also contain specific technical provisions concerning generation, switchgear, protection, earthing, electrical installations and safety.

Accordingly, off-grid operation does not mean that electrical safety and installation requirements disappear.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical separation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not participate in electricity trading.
  • Document the installation as a private self-supply facility.
  • Provide the required self-supply information under section 16 of the Electricity Act 2017.
  • Verify whether the proposed activity requires an electricity licence or falls within an applicable exemption.
  • Comply with the Electricity Regulations 2019.
  • Implement electrical protection and isolation preventing accidental grid energisation.
  • Verify planning, environmental, building and occupational safety requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
Fiji — statutory capacity threshold for self-supply:

No general MW threshold specifically identified in section 16 of the Electricity Act 2017.

The Electricity Act 2017 expressly regulates self-supply but section 16 does not establish a general maximum MW capacity for self-supply.

The legal analysis therefore depends on the nature of the activity, its licensing status, the applicable regulatory requirements and the technical installation requirements.

The absence of a stated MW threshold does not mean that a large generating installation is automatically exempt from licensing, safety or other regulatory requirements.

Public Information Notice

Fiji regulates electricity generation and supply principally under the Electricity Act 2017. The Act expressly addresses self-supply in section 16 and establishes a statutory mechanism requiring a person intending to self-supply to provide details to the public electricity supplier. Electricity installation, generation, protection and safety requirements are also addressed by the Electricity Regulations 2019. No general statutory MW maximum for self-supply has been identified in section 16. The precise requirements therefore depend on the installation, its licensing status, technical characteristics and applicable regulatory procedures.
