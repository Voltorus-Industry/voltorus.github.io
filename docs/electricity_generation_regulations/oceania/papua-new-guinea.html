Overview

We are considering a private electricity-generation facility located in Papua New Guinea and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The proposed installation would operate independently from the public electricity network, with no intended electricity import or export.

The electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, commercial or corporate purposes.

The principal modern regulatory framework is the National Energy Authority Act 2021 together with the Electricity Supply Industry Law.

The electricity supply industry, including generation, transmission, distribution, supply and sale of electricity, is regulated.

Electricity undertakers must obtain the applicable licence under the Electricity Supply Industry Law.

The National Energy Authority framework also provides specific rules for small-scale generation projects, including a requirement that licences for projects with installed capacity up to 10 MW be reserved for Papua New Guinea citizen companies.

Administrative Requirements

The National Energy Authority performs energy regulatory functions and is responsible for the regulatory framework applying to the electricity supply industry.

Generation is expressly included among the regulated electricity operations.

  • Determine the applicable electricity-generation licence.
  • Determine whether the project qualifies as a small-scale generation project.
  • Verify company ownership and citizenship requirements.
  • Submit the required licence application.
  • Comply with technical and safety requirements.
  • Verify construction and land requirements.
  • Verify environmental approvals.
  • Verify occupational health and safety requirements.
Measures to Reduce Regulatory Risk
  • Maintain electrical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Obtain written confirmation from the National Energy Authority concerning the licensing status of the proposed isolated installation.
  • If a generation licence is required, verify all ownership and citizenship requirements before structuring the project company.
  • Document the installed electrical capacity.
  • Complete all environmental, construction and electrical-safety approvals.
Maximum Electrical Power
Papua New Guinea — licensing:

Electricity generation is a regulated activity.

The National Energy Authority framework provides a specific rule concerning generation projects with installed capacity up to 10 MW.

The 10 MW value should not be interpreted as a maximum permissible generating capacity.

It is relevant to the statutory licensing framework, including the reservation of licences for small-scale generation projects up to 10 MW for Papua New Guinea citizen companies.

Larger projects remain subject to the applicable licensing and regulatory framework.

A completely isolated self-consumption project should therefore be submitted to the National Energy Authority for a formal determination of the applicable licensing requirements before construction.

Public Information Notice

Papua New Guinea has a regulated electricity supply industry covering electricity generation and related activities. The National Energy Authority Act 2021 establishes the National Energy Authority and provides the modern regulatory framework, including licensing requirements. The framework contains specific rules concerning small-scale generation projects up to 10 MW and Papua New Guinea citizen companies. The absence of a public-grid connection does not by itself establish that a generation project is exempt from the electricity regulatory framework. The exact licensing position should therefore be confirmed with the National Energy Authority before construction.
