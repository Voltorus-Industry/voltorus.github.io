Overview

Electricity generation in Solomon Islands is principally regulated by the Electricity Act [Cap. 128] and the regulations made under that Act.

The Electricity Act establishes the Solomon Islands Electricity Authority and provides a statutory framework for the generation, transmission, distribution and use of electricity.

The Act expressly provides for licensing procedures concerning electricity generation. The Electricity Regulations identify a supplier as the Solomon Islands Electricity Authority or the holder of a licence to generate electricity.

The proposed installation is assumed to be a completely isolated electrical system with no physical connection to a public electricity network, no electricity import, no electricity export and no sale of electricity to third parties.

The fact that electricity is intended exclusively for internal self-consumption does not by itself establish a statutory exemption from the Electricity Act. The licensing position must therefore be confirmed with the competent electricity authority before construction or operation.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Act establishes the Solomon Islands Electricity Authority and gives it functions relating to electricity generation, transmission, distribution and supply.

A generation licence is the principal statutory authorization mechanism for electricity generation.

The licensing framework may specify matters including the area of supply, declared voltage, maximum charges, purpose of generation, expiry date and other conditions considered necessary by the Authority.

Generation Licence

The Electricity Act provides for licences authorising electricity generation.

The licensing framework is not limited solely to electricity sold to a public network. Consequently, an operator proposing an independent generation facility should obtain confirmation from the competent authority as to whether a generation licence is required for the specific self-consumption project.

The absence of a public-grid connection should therefore not automatically be treated as an exemption from the Electricity Act.

Other Potential Requirements
  • Electricity-generation authorization or licence where required by the Electricity Act.
  • Compliance with applicable electricity regulations.
  • Electrical installation and safety requirements.
  • Competent and appropriately qualified electrical personnel.
  • Land and site authorization.
  • Environmental requirements applicable to the proposed installation.
  • Construction and planning requirements.
  • Fire and occupational safety requirements.
  • Any additional conditions imposed by the competent authority.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Keep the installation physically isolated from any public electricity network.
  • Install electrical protection preventing accidental grid connection.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Document the installation as a private self-consumption facility.
  • Clearly document the installed electrical capacity.
  • Obtain written confirmation from the competent electricity authority concerning the licensing status before construction.
  • Verify land, environmental, construction, electrical-safety and fire-safety requirements.

No specific gravity-generation MW threshold identified in the Electricity Act.

The Electricity Act establishes a licensing framework for electricity generation but the provisions reviewed do not establish a specific MW threshold applicable specifically to gravity-powered generation.

The regulatory position therefore depends principally upon the nature of the generation activity and the applicable licence and conditions.

The project capacity should be stated according to the engineering design and submitted to the competent authority for determination of the applicable authorization requirements.
