Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Kiribati and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the public electricity network, with no import or export of electricity.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, commercial, scientific, research or corporate requirements under the control of the operator.

Electricity regulation in Kiribati is principally associated with the Public Utilities Ordinance (Cap. 83), together with subsequent amendments and electricity regulations.

The Public Utilities Ordinance established the Public Utilities Board and provides the statutory framework for electricity and other public utility functions.

The Public Utilities (Amendment) Act 1997 is particularly relevant to private generation because it amended the Ordinance to permit persons other than the Public Utilities Board to generate and distribute electricity to their premises in specified circumstances when the Board is unable to supply those premises.

Administrative Requirements

The Public Utilities Ordinance establishes the statutory Public Utilities Board and provides the framework for electricity supply.

Private generation must therefore be assessed against the Ordinance, its amendments and applicable electricity regulations.

Private Generation — 1997 Amendment

The Public Utilities (Amendment) Act 1997 specifically amended the Public Utilities Ordinance to allow generation of power by persons other than the Public Utilities Board in certain circumstances.

The amendment made it lawful within an electricity supply area for persons to generate and distribute electricity to their premises during periods when the Board was unable to supply power to those premises, without written permission from the Board.

This provision is not equivalent to a general national exemption for every private self-consumption installation. The precise statutory conditions must therefore be checked against the current consolidated legislation.

Technical and Safety Requirements

Electricity Regulations form part of the regulatory framework and must be considered for electrical installations and equipment.

Electrical safety, installation standards, wiring, protection and any applicable construction requirements must be verified before operation.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Operate as a genuinely isolated electrical island.
  • Maintain no physical connection to an external public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use electricity exclusively within the operator's premises and controlled facilities.
  • Verify the applicability of the Public Utilities Ordinance and its amendments.
  • Verify the applicable Electricity Regulations.
  • Obtain any required permission, approval, construction authorization or utility consent.
  • Implement electrical protection preventing accidental connection to a public network.
  • Verify environmental and land-use requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
Kiribati — statutory capacity threshold:

No general MW threshold for private self-consumption identified.

The available legislation identifies circumstances in which private generation may occur, but the provisions reviewed do not establish a general electricity-generation MW threshold that can be presented as the maximum capacity for an isolated self-consumption facility.

The 1997 amendment concerns the circumstances in which private generation and distribution to the generator's own premises is permitted, rather than establishing a technology-specific MW ceiling.

Public Information Notice

Electricity generation in Kiribati is principally governed by the Public Utilities Ordinance (Cap. 83), its amendments and applicable Electricity Regulations. The Public Utilities (Amendment) Act 1997 specifically addressed private generation in circumstances where the Public Utilities Board was unable to supply the relevant premises. No general MW capacity threshold for an isolated private self-consumption generating facility has been identified in the legislation reviewed. The exact legal treatment must therefore be verified against the current consolidated legislation, the location of the installation and the applicable technical and administrative requirements.
