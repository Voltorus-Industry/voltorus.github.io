Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Australia and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from any public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, research, commercial or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the operator.

Australia does not have one single national electricity-generation licensing regime applicable to every installation. Electricity regulation is divided between Commonwealth legislation, the National Electricity Law and National Electricity Rules, and State and Territory legislation.

The National Electricity Rules principally regulate electricity systems and generating systems connected to transmission or distribution systems within their applicable jurisdictional framework.

Under National Electricity Rules clause 2.2.1, a person must not own, control or operate a generating system connected to a transmission or distribution system unless registered as a Generator, subject to applicable exemptions.

A genuinely isolated off-grid installation is therefore not automatically subject to the same NEM generator registration regime merely because it generates electricity. The precise requirements must instead be checked against the legislation of the Australian State or Territory in which the installation is located.

Administrative Requirements

Australia has a federal constitutional structure. Electricity generation is regulated through a combination of national and jurisdictional legislation.

The National Electricity Rules apply principally to generating systems connected to transmission or distribution systems within the relevant National Electricity Market framework.

Clause 2.2.1 of the National Electricity Rules provides that a person must not own, control or operate a generating system connected to a transmission or distribution system unless registered as a Generator, subject to the applicable exemption provisions.

Off-grid Self-consumption

A facility that is genuinely isolated from public electricity networks does not fall into the same category as a generator connected to the transmission or distribution network.

However, absence of a national NEM registration requirement does not constitute a general exemption from State or Territory laws.

Depending on the location, additional requirements may concern electrical safety, planning approval, environmental assessment, construction, occupational health and safety, dangerous goods, fire protection and technical standards.

Example of State-specific Regulation

Western Australia's Electricity Industry Act 2004 expressly provides a licensing framework for generation, transmission, distribution and retail electricity activities.

However, section 7(5) provides an important self-supply exception where generating works are used solely for supplying electricity for consumption by the owner, controller or operator or a related body corporate.

The Western Australian Economic Regulation Authority also states that self-supply falls outside the electricity licensing requirement where the works are used solely for consumption by the relevant owner, controller or operator or related body corporate.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a genuinely isolated self-consumption facility, the following configuration is recommended:

  • No physical connection to a public electricity transmission or distribution network.
  • No electricity import from a public network.
  • No electricity export to a public network.
  • No sale of electricity to third parties.
  • No electricity trading.
  • No electricity-market participation.
  • Electricity used exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Independent electrical island operation.
  • Protection against accidental energisation of an external electricity network.
  • Verification of the electricity legislation applicable in the specific State or Territory.
  • Verification of planning and environmental requirements before construction.
  • Compliance with applicable electrical safety and technical standards.
Maximum Electrical Power
Australia — national statutory capacity threshold:

No single Australia-wide off-grid self-consumption MW threshold identified.

Electricity generation requirements vary according to the applicable Commonwealth, State or Territory legislation and whether the installation is connected to an electricity network.

The National Electricity Rules do not establish a single national MW threshold that can be presented as the maximum permitted capacity for a genuinely isolated private generating facility throughout Australia.

For example, Western Australia provides an express self-supply exception from electricity licensing. The applicable legal treatment therefore depends materially on the location and configuration of the installation.

Accordingly, the maximum permissible capacity should not be stated as a single national MW figure without first identifying the State or Territory.

Public Information Notice

Australia does not have one single national electricity generation licensing rule applicable to every isolated generating facility. The National Electricity Rules regulate relevant network-connected generating systems, while State and Territory legislation regulates many electricity activities outside that framework. A genuinely isolated self-consumption facility may fall outside particular network registration or licensing requirements, but the precise legal treatment must be established from the legislation applicable at the project location. Electrical safety, planning, environmental, construction and other requirements may remain applicable.
