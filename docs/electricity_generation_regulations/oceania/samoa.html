Overview

We are considering a private electricity-generation facility located in Samoa and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The facility would operate independently from the electricity network and would not import electricity from or export electricity to the public network.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, commercial or corporate requirements.

The principal electricity legislation is the Electricity Act 2010 (Act No. 23 of 2010).

The Act establishes the Electricity Regulator and provides a licensing framework for electricity services.

Importantly, the Act defines a "generation licence" as a licence to generate electricity for sale.

The Office of the Regulator describes the generation licence regime in the context of Independent Power Producers generating electricity and selling it to the national grid.

Consequently, a genuinely isolated private generation installation used exclusively for internal self-consumption is materially different from a licensed IPP selling electricity to the national electricity network. Nevertheless, the exact legal treatment should be confirmed with the Regulator before construction.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Act 2010 establishes the Electricity Regulator and provides the licensing framework for electricity services.

Section 12 identifies the types of licences and section 13 establishes the application and grant procedure.

Section 16 establishes an offence for operating without a required licence.

  • Determine whether the installation constitutes electricity generation for sale.
  • Confirm that the installation is genuinely isolated from the national electricity network.
  • Obtain written confirmation from the Electricity Regulator concerning licensing status.
  • Verify electrical safety and installation requirements.
  • Verify building and land-use requirements.
  • Verify environmental requirements.
  • Verify occupational health and safety requirements.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation from the national electricity network.
  • Do not export electricity to the national network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not operate as an Independent Power Producer selling electricity to the electricity network.
  • Use all generated electricity exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Install protection against accidental grid connection.
  • Document the isolated electrical architecture.
  • Document the nominal and installed generation capacity.
  • Obtain written regulatory confirmation before construction.

The Electricity Act 2010 defines a generation licence as a licence to generate electricity for sale.

The licensing provisions are therefore directly relevant to commercial generation and electricity services falling within the statutory licensing framework.

The Office of the Regulator specifically describes the generation licence in connection with Independent Power Producers generating electricity for sale to the national grid.

For a completely isolated installation used exclusively for internal consumption, no general statutory MW ceiling was identified. The project should nevertheless be submitted to the Electricity Regulator for a formal determination before construction.

Public Information Notice

Samoa regulates electricity services under the Electricity Act 2010. The Act expressly defines a generation licence as a licence to generate electricity for sale. The Office of the Regulator also describes generation licensing in connection with Independent Power Producers selling electricity to the national grid. A completely isolated private installation used solely for internal self-consumption is therefore legally distinguishable from a grid-connected IPP. Nevertheless, the final licensing position should be confirmed by the Electricity Regulator, together with all applicable electrical, construction, environmental and safety requirements.
