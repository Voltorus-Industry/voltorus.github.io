Overview

Electricity generation in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is principally governed by the Electricity Supply Act 1973.

The statutory framework grants Saint Vincent Electricity Services Limited (VINLEC) the principal electricity supply rights in the country.

The legal framework also regulates generation by persons other than VINLEC and requires the applicable permission or licence.

Importantly, available legal analysis of the Electricity Supply Act states that electricity generation for the owner's own use, commonly described as autogeneration, also requires VINLEC approval.

Consequently, a completely off-grid installation cannot be classified automatically as exempt merely because electricity is not sold or supplied to the public.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Supply Act provides the statutory framework for electricity generation, transmission and distribution.

VINLEC holds the principal electricity supply rights under the statutory framework.

Generation by another person or entity is therefore not automatically authorised merely because the electricity is consumed by the generator itself.

Self-consumption / Autogeneration

Available legal analysis of the 1973 Electricity Supply Act states that power generation for the owner's own use also requires VINLEC approval.

This is particularly important for an isolated industrial or corporate generation facility.

The absence of a public-grid connection therefore does not by itself establish an exemption from the electricity legislation.

  • Obtain confirmation from VINLEC concerning the proposed self-generation installation.
  • Determine whether a licence or formal approval is required.
  • Verify electrical inspection and safety requirements.
  • Verify planning and construction requirements.
  • Verify environmental requirements applicable to the proposed site and technology.
  • Maintain physical isolation from the public network if the project is intended to remain off-grid.
Maximum Electrical Power
General statutory MW threshold:

No general numerical MW threshold identified for owner self-generation under the Electricity Supply Act 1973.

The statutory framework does not provide a general technology-specific MW threshold under which a private self-generation installation is automatically exempt.

The relevant legal issue is therefore the applicable permission or licence for generation and the approval requirements imposed by the electricity framework.

Website classification:

Private off-grid generation should be treated as subject to prior regulatory confirmation rather than automatically exempt.

Public Information Notice

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines regulates electricity generation under the Electricity Supply Act 1973. The electricity sector has historically operated under a strong VINLEC-centred licensing framework. Available legal sources indicate that autogeneration for the owner's own use also requires the applicable VINLEC approval. A private off-grid gravity-powered generating facility should therefore obtain written regulatory confirmation before construction or operation.
