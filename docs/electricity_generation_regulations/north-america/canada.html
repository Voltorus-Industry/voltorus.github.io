Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Canada and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from any public electricity network.

No electricity would be imported from or exported to a public electricity grid.

Canada does not have a single nationwide electricity generation licensing regime applicable to all installations.

Section 92A of the Constitution Act, 1867 gives provinces legislative authority concerning the development, conservation and management of sites and facilities within the province for the generation and production of electrical energy.

Consequently, the precise authorization requirements depend on the province or territory where the project is located.

There is therefore no single Canadian statutory MW threshold that can accurately be presented as the national maximum generating capacity for an isolated self-consumption installation.

Administrative Requirements

Canadian electricity regulation is principally decentralized at provincial and territorial level.

A project developer must therefore identify the applicable provincial electricity statute and regulator before determining whether a generation licence is required.

  • Identify the province or territory.
  • Identify the applicable electricity legislation.
  • Determine whether the project constitutes a regulated electricity generation activity.
  • Determine whether the installation is entirely for internal consumption.
  • Verify building and electrical-code requirements.
  • Verify environmental assessment requirements.
  • Verify land-use and planning requirements.
  • Verify fire and occupational safety requirements.
Example — Ontario

Ontario's Energy Board Act requires a licence for specified electricity activities, including generation for sale through the IESO-administered markets or directly to another person.

This demonstrates why the regulatory analysis for a purely self-consumption facility differs from the analysis of a commercial electricity generator.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Specify the exact province or territory before starting the regulatory approval process.
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation from the public grid.
  • Do not sell or supply electricity to third parties.
  • Obtain written confirmation from the provincial electricity regulator where appropriate.
  • Verify electrical-code compliance.
  • Verify environmental and planning requirements.
  • Document installed generation capacity.

Public Information Notice

This information is provided for general public information purposes only. Canada does not have one nationwide electricity-generation licensing regime governing all private generating facilities. Section 92A of the Constitution Act, 1867 assigns provinces legislative authority concerning facilities for electrical-energy generation. The applicable licensing, environmental, planning, construction and electrical-safety requirements must therefore be determined according to the province or territory in which the project is located. No single national MW ceiling has been identified.
