Overview

Haiti regulates electricity generation through its electricity-sector legal framework, including the Décret régissant le secteur de l'énergie électrique published on 3 February 2016.

The decree regulates electricity production, transmission, distribution and commercialization.

Electricity generation is treated as a regulated activity. Article 5 establishes that activities in the electricity sector are subject to a licence, an operating right or a public-service concession.

Article 6 provides that electricity production, transmission, distribution and commercialization may be carried out only by persons or entities holding the required licence or operating right.

Article 10 is particularly relevant to an electricity generation project. It provides that any enterprise intending to produce electricity by any means must first obtain a production licence from the regulatory authority.

The decree does not establish a general MW threshold under which an off-grid electricity-generation facility is automatically exempt from the production-licence requirement. A genuinely isolated self-consumption project must therefore be analysed under the applicable licensing framework rather than assuming that self-consumption alone creates an exemption.

Administrative Requirements

Article 10 of the electricity-sector decree provides that an enterprise intending to produce electricity by any means must obtain a production licence beforehand.

Article 11 provides that production licences may be granted following an individual application or following a call for tenders by the regulatory authority.

Article 12 establishes the criteria for granting a production licence.

Production Licence Criteria
  • Nature of the energy source.
  • Choice of site, land occupation and use of public property.
  • Energy efficiency and appropriateness.
  • Installed capacity and nominal capacity of the site.
  • Financial capacity of the applicant to implement the project without a State guarantee.
Additional Requirements
  • Production licence where required by Article 10.
  • Compliance with environmental-protection requirements.
  • Compliance with urban-planning requirements.
  • Personnel and public-safety requirements.
  • Civil-liability requirements.
  • Land-use requirements.
  • Construction, operation and maintenance requirements specified in the production agreement.
  • Any additional requirements imposed by the competent regulatory authority.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical separation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Document the installation as an internal self-consumption facility.
  • Precisely document installed and nominal capacity.
  • Obtain a written determination from the competent Haitian authority concerning the applicable production licence.
  • Verify environmental, land-use, construction and safety requirements before construction.
  • Obtain approval before increasing installed generation capacity.
Maximum Electrical Power
Haiti — statutory capacity threshold:

No general MW exemption identified in the production-licensing provisions

Article 10 requires an enterprise intending to produce electricity by any means to obtain a production licence beforehand.

Article 12 expressly considers the installed capacity and nominal capacity of the site when assessing a production licence.

The law therefore does not justify presenting a particular MW value as a generally permitted maximum for an off-grid generation facility.

The permissible capacity is therefore project-specific and subject to the applicable licensing and regulatory process .

Public Information Notice

Haitian electricity legislation regulates electricity generation as a licensed activity. Article 10 of the electricity-sector decree requires an enterprise intending to produce electricity by any means to obtain a production licence beforehand. The decree does not establish a general MW threshold that automatically exempts an off-grid self-consumption generation facility from this requirement. Project capacity, site, energy source, financial capability, environmental protection, land use and safety are relevant to the regulatory assessment.
