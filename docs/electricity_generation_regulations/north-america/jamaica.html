Overview

Electricity generation in Jamaica is principally governed by the Electricity Act, 2015.

The Act establishes a specific legal category for a self-generator, meaning a person who generates electricity for that person's own use.

Section 17 establishes duties for generation licensees but expressly provides that those duties do not apply to self-generators.

Section 18 establishes specific requirements applicable to self-generators.

A self-generator must develop and maintain its generation system in accordance with the Generation Code.

Where electricity is supplied or sold beyond the self-generation arrangement, additional licensing and contractual requirements apply.

A genuinely isolated facility producing electricity exclusively for its own consumption is therefore treated differently from an electricity generator producing electricity for the public electricity system.

Administrative Requirements
Self-generation

Section 17(2) provides that the general duty imposed on generation licensees does not apply to self-generators.

Section 18(1) requires a self-generator to develop and maintain its electricity-generation system in accordance with the Generation Code.

The self-generation regime is therefore distinct from the licensing regime applicable to generators producing electricity for sale or supply.

Sale or Supply of Excess Electricity

Section 18 establishes additional conditions where excess electricity is to be sold or otherwise supplied.

Such arrangements require compliance with the applicable contractual and licensing framework.

An installation intended to remain purely self-consumption should therefore not export or sell electricity unless the applicable regulatory requirements have been satisfied.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Operate exclusively as a self-generator.
  • Maintain no electrical connection to the public grid if the project is intended to be genuinely isolated.
  • Do not sell or supply excess electricity.
  • Maintain the generation system in accordance with the Generation Code.
  • Install appropriate electrical protection.
  • Document the installation as an exclusive self-consumption facility.
  • Verify all electrical installation and safety requirements before commissioning.
Maximum Electrical Power
Jamaica — self-generation capacity:

No general MW threshold identified for the self-generator category in Section 18

The Electricity Act 2015 does not establish a general MW ceiling in Section 18 for a self-generator that exclusively consumes its own electricity.

The distinction is therefore principally based on the legal nature of the activity and whether electricity is supplied beyond the self-generation arrangement.

The project's electrical capacity should nevertheless be documented precisely and the technical requirements applicable to the installation verified before operation.

Public Information Notice

Jamaica's Electricity Act 2015 expressly recognizes self-generators. Section 17 excludes self-generators from certain duties imposed on generation licensees, while Section 18 establishes specific obligations for self-generators. A genuinely isolated facility used exclusively for its owner's electricity consumption is therefore legally distinct from a generator supplying electricity to the public system. Sale or supply of excess electricity creates additional regulatory requirements.
