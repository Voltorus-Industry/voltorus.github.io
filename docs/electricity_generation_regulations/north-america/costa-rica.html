Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Costa Rica and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the Sistema Eléctrico Nacional and without electricity import or export.

Costa Rica regulates electricity generation through several legal instruments, including Law No. 7200 and Law No. 7593.

Law No. 7200 concerns autonomous or parallel electricity generation by limited-capacity plants capable of being integrated into the national electricity system.

Article 2 of Law No. 7200 defines limited-capacity plants as hydroelectric and non-conventional plants not exceeding 20,000 kW.

This 20 MW threshold is principally relevant to the generation regime established by Law No. 7200 and should not automatically be treated as a general maximum for a genuinely isolated self-consumption facility.

Administrative Requirements

ARESEP regulates the electricity service from generation through transmission and distribution.

Law No. 7593 establishes that public-service providers generally require the corresponding concession or permit from the competent public authority.

  • Determine whether the installation constitutes regulated public electricity generation.
  • Determine whether Law No. 7200 applies.
  • Determine whether the facility will ever be integrated into the national electricity system.
  • Verify any generation concession requirement.
  • Verify environmental requirements.
  • Verify land-use and construction requirements.
  • Verify electrical safety requirements.

Maximum Electrical Power
Costa Rica — Law No. 7200:

20,000 kW / 20 MW

Article 2 defines limited-capacity plants as hydroelectric and non-conventional plants not exceeding 20,000 kW.

Under the Chapter I regime of Law No. 7200, private generation is subject to the statutory limited-capacity framework and the associated concession requirements.

The Chapter II regime establishes a separate framework for plants using exclusively non-conventional sources, with a statutory ceiling of 50,000 kW in the relevant generation regime.

These thresholds concern the legal framework of private generation under Law No. 7200, particularly generation associated with the national electricity system.

They should not automatically be represented as a universal maximum for a facility that is genuinely isolated and used solely for private consumption.

The applicability of these limits to a purely isolated self-consumption facility must be legally determined according to the project's actual configuration.
