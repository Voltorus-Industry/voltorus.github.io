Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Grenada and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to operate independently from the national electricity network.

No electricity would be imported from or exported to the public electricity network.

Electricity would be consumed exclusively by the operator for its own requirements.

Electricity generation is principally regulated by the Electricity Supply Act No. 19 of 2016 .

The Act establishes a licensing regime while creating specific exemptions for self-generators.

Section 13(2) provides that a self-generator does not require a licence in specified circumstances, including generation from renewable resources for its own consumption and generation exclusively for its own consumption where the premises are located in an area not supplied by a network licensee.

Administrative Requirements

Section 13(1) establishes the general rule that electricity supply requires a licence.

Section 13(2) establishes exceptions for certain self-generators.

Self-generation Exemptions
  • Self-generation from renewable resources for the generator's own consumption.
  • Generation exclusively for own consumption where the premises are located in an area not supplied by a network licensee.
Grid-connected Self-generation

Where a self-generator connects its generation facility to the electrical system of a network licensee, section 25 requires a permit.

The permit is granted by the Minister on the recommendation of the Commission.

The permit may be valid for a period not exceeding 15 years.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the network licensee's electrical system.
  • Use the generated electricity exclusively for internal consumption.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Do not export electricity to the national network.
  • Document the facility as a self-generator.
  • Verify compliance with technical and safety regulations.
  • Obtain the applicable permit if any connection to the network is subsequently proposed.
Maximum Electrical Power
Grenada — statutory capacity threshold:

No general MW threshold is specified in section 13(2) for the self-generator exemptions.

The Electricity Supply Act does not establish a general MW ceiling for the self-generation exemptions contained in section 13(2).

The legal distinction is primarily based on the purpose of the generation, whether it is for own consumption, whether renewable resources are involved and whether the facility is connected to a network licensee.

A grid-connected self-generator falls under the permit regime of section 25.

Public Information Notice

Grenada's Electricity Supply Act No. 19 of 2016 establishes a general electricity licensing framework but provides specific exemptions for self-generators. Section 13(2) includes renewable self-generation for own consumption and exclusive self-generation in areas not supplied by a network licensee. A permit is required under section 25 where a self-generator intends to connect its generation facility to a network licensee's electrical system. The Act does not establish a general MW ceiling for the self-generator exemptions in section 13(2).
