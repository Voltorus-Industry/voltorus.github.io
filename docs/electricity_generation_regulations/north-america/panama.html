Overview

Electricity generation in Panama is governed principally by Law 6 of 3 February 1997, which establishes the regulatory and institutional framework for the public electricity service.

The law regulates generation, transmission, distribution and commercialization activities associated with the public electricity service.

Article 60 establishes a licensing regime for the construction and operation of generation plants that are not subject to the concession regime.

The Autoridad Nacional de los Servicios Públicos (ASEP) is responsible for granting the relevant generation licences under the law.

Panama also has a specific regulatory procedure for autoconsumo con fuentes nuevas, renovables y limpias.

A project that is genuinely isolated from the public electricity network should therefore be classified carefully according to whether it falls under the public-generation licensing regime or another self-consumption regime.

Administrative Requirements
Generation Licence

Article 60 of Law 6 establishes the licensing regime for construction and operation of generation plants that are not subject to concession.

The licence is granted by the electricity regulatory authority through a reasoned resolution.

Environmental-protection, industrial-safety, hygiene and establishment-operation requirements must be satisfied.

Self-consumption

Panama has a specific procedure for self-consumption using new, renewable and clean energy sources.

The regulatory framework contains technical thresholds including installations up to 500 kW and higher-capacity installations.

Current ASEP rules should be checked before commissioning because the self-consumption framework and its technical limits are subject to regulatory resolutions.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Clearly classify the installation as self-consumption.
  • Keep the installation physically isolated from the public network if off-grid operation is intended.
  • Do not inject electricity into the public network.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Precisely document installed capacity.
  • Determine whether the project falls within the ASEP self-consumption procedure.
  • Verify whether a generation licence under Article 60 is required.
  • Obtain environmental and construction approvals before construction where applicable.
Maximum Electrical Power
Panama — generation licensing:

No single general MW maximum for all generation facilities

Article 60 establishes a licensing regime for applicable generation plants rather than imposing a single maximum generation capacity.

The self-consumption framework contains technical capacity thresholds. The regulatory documentation specifically identifies installations up to 500 kW and higher-capacity categories.

Current ASEP procedures must therefore be consulted for the exact treatment of the proposed capacity.

The website classification should consequently be: project-specific capacity subject to the generation licence and/or self-consumption regulatory framework.

Panama regulates electricity generation principally through Law 6 of 1997. Article 60 establishes a licensing regime for the construction and operation of generation plants that are not subject to concession. Panama also maintains a specific self-consumption framework for new, renewable and clean energy sources. A genuinely isolated self-consumption installation should therefore be classified according to its capacity, technology, connection status and intended use, and the applicable ASEP requirements should be verified before construction or operation.
