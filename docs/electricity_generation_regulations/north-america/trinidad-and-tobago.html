Overview

Electricity generation and electricity installations in Trinidad and Tobago are principally governed by the Trinidad and Tobago Electricity Commission Act, Chapter 54:70, together with other applicable legislation and regulations.

The Act establishes the Trinidad and Tobago Electricity Commission (T&TEC) and provides the statutory framework for the supply and use of electrical energy.

The Act also contains a specific licensing framework concerning electrical installations.

Section 66 provides that, except as otherwise prescribed by law, a person may not use, work or operate an installation, or supply energy from an installation to another person, except under and in accordance with an applicable licence.

The legal position for a completely isolated self-consumption generator must therefore be examined according to the type of generator, installation, connection and use.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Commission Act provides the principal statutory framework for electricity supply and use.

Section 66 establishes a licence requirement for the operation or use of electrical installations, subject to exceptions and provisions elsewhere in the law.

The Act contains a specific provision concerning approved generators in Part VIII.

Generator and Installation Licensing

Section 65A provides that Part VIII does not apply to an approved generator of electricity.

Section 66 nevertheless establishes the general licensing rule for use, operation or supply from an electrical installation, except where the law provides otherwise.

T&TEC also publishes information concerning standby generator licences under the Electricity Commission Act.

The exact classification of a large permanent off-grid generating facility therefore requires project-specific confirmation from the competent authority.

  • Determine whether the installation is an "approved generator".
  • Determine whether section 66 requires an installation licence.
  • Confirm applicable requirements with T&TEC and the competent government authority.
  • Verify electrical safety and inspection requirements.
  • Verify planning and environmental requirements.
  • Maintain electrical isolation from the public electricity network where applicable.
Maximum Electrical Power
General statutory MW threshold:

No general MW threshold identified in the Electricity Commission Act for permanent private off-grid self-consumption generation.

The legislation uses concepts such as licensing, installation requirements and approved generators rather than establishing a single general MW ceiling applicable to every type of private generation.

Accordingly, the project capacity should be assessed together with the generator classification and the applicable licensing provisions.

Public Information Notice

Trinidad and Tobago regulates electricity generation, supply and electrical installations principally under the Trinidad and Tobago Electricity Commission Act, Chapter 54:70. Section 66 establishes a licensing framework for the use and operation of electrical installations, while section 65A contains a specific provision concerning approved generators. A permanent off-grid gravity-powered generating facility intended exclusively for internal consumption should therefore be submitted to the competent authority for a project-specific determination of its licensing and approval requirements.
