Overview

Electricity generation in Saint Lucia is principally regulated by the Electricity Supply Act, Cap. 9.02, as amended.

The Act grants Saint Lucia Electricity Services Limited (LUCELEC) an exclusive licence framework covering electricity generation, transmission, distribution and sale, subject to the statutory amendments concerning renewable electricity.

Section 25 establishes the exclusive right concerning electricity generation from fossil fuels during the continuance of the licence.

The Act separately provides that the National Utilities Regulatory Commission is responsible for licensing and regulating electricity generation from renewable energy resources.

An isolated self-consumption installation therefore cannot simply be assumed to be outside the electricity regulatory framework. Its treatment depends on the technology and the applicable licensing provisions.

Administrative Requirements

Section 3 of the Electricity Supply Act grants LUCELEC a sole and exclusive licence to generate, transmit, distribute and sell electricity in Saint Lucia.

Section 4, as amended, assigns responsibility for the licensing and regulation of electricity generation from renewable energy resources to the Commission.

The legal framework therefore distinguishes between fossil-fuel generation and renewable-energy generation.

Renewable Electricity

Section 4 provides that, subject to the Act, the Commission is responsible for licensing and regulating the generation of electricity from renewable energy resources.

If the proposed gravity technology qualifies legally as generation from a renewable energy resource, the renewable-generation licensing framework should therefore be examined with the National Utilities Regulatory Commission.

  • Determine the legal classification of the proposed gravity technology.
  • Determine whether it falls within the renewable electricity provisions.
  • Consult the National Utilities Regulatory Commission before construction.
  • Confirm whether a generation licence or other authorization is required.
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation if the project is intended to remain off-grid.
  • Obtain applicable planning, construction, environmental and electrical safety approvals.
Maximum Electrical Power
General statutory MW threshold:

No general MW threshold identified in the Electricity Supply Act for gravity-powered self-consumption.

The Electricity Supply Act does not establish a technology-specific numerical MW ceiling for a gravity-powered self-consumption installation.

Regulatory treatment is instead determined by the applicable licensing provisions, particularly the provisions concerning renewable electricity generation.

The absence of a numerical threshold does not mean that a large private generating plant is automatically exempt from licensing.

Website classification:

Generation should be treated as subject to project-specific regulatory determination by the competent authority.

Public Information Notice

Saint Lucia's electricity sector is governed principally by the Electricity Supply Act, Cap. 9.02. The Act maintains an exclusive electricity licence framework while assigning the National Utilities Regulatory Commission responsibility for licensing and regulating generation from renewable energy resources. A private off-grid gravity-powered installation should therefore be submitted for a project-specific determination, particularly concerning whether the technology qualifies as renewable generation and whether a generation licence is required.
