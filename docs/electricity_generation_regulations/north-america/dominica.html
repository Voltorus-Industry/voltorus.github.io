Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Dominica and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from the national electricity grid, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to the public electricity network.

Electricity would be used exclusively for the operator's own household, industrial, scientific, commercial or corporate requirements.

Electricity generation in Dominica is principally regulated by the Electricity Supply Act No. 10 of 2006.

The Act establishes a licensing framework for electricity generation, transmission, distribution and supply and assigns regulatory functions to the Independent Regulatory Commission (IRC).

The legislation specifically distinguishes between generation connected to the national grid, off-grid generation and domestic self-generation.

The principal capacity threshold relevant to an isolated generating facility is 20 kW. Under section 31(3), a generation licence is required for generation sets of 20 kW or greater that are not connected to the national grid. Section 31(4) provides an exemption for generation facilities used for domestic self-generation which are not connected to the national grid.

Administrative Requirements

Section 31 of the Electricity Supply Act establishes the principal generation licensing requirements.

Off-grid Generation

Section 31(3) requires a generation licence for generation sets having a capacity of 20 kW or greater that are not connected to the national grid.

Therefore, the absence of a grid connection does not by itself eliminate the generation licence requirement once the 20 kW threshold is reached.

Domestic Self-generation

Section 31(4) provides that, subject to the provisions of section 31(3), a generation licence is not required for generation facilities used for domestic self-generation when those facilities are not connected to the national grid.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the national electricity grid.
  • Do not import electricity from the national grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the national grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Clearly document the installation as an internal self-consumption facility.
  • Precisely document the nominal electrical generating capacity.
  • If the facility is 20 kW or greater, obtain the applicable generation licence unless a statutory exemption applies.
  • Ensure electrical protection prevents accidental energisation of the national grid.
  • Verify all environmental, structural and safety requirements before construction.
Maximum Electrical Power
Dominica — statutory threshold:

20 kW

Under section 31(3) of the Electricity Supply Act, a generation licence is required for generation sets of 20 kW or greater that are not connected to the national grid.

The 20 kW figure is therefore the principal statutory threshold for an isolated generation facility.

It should not be interpreted as a maximum permitted generation capacity. It is a licensing threshold.

A facility exceeding 20 kW may therefore be developed, subject to obtaining the applicable licence and meeting all other legal and technical requirements.
