Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in The Bahamas and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is intended to remain physically isolated from the public electricity network.

No electricity would be imported from or exported to the public electricity grid and no electricity would be sold to third parties.

The principal current legal framework is the Electricity Act, 2024.

The Electricity Act, 2024 places electricity-sector licensing under the Utilities Regulation and Competition Authority (URCA).

URCA's generation-licence procedure expressly states that entities with self-generating capacity exceeding 1 MW fall within the generation licensing requirement.

The 1 MW figure should therefore be presented as a licensing threshold for self-generation under the current URCA framework, rather than as a general technological maximum for electricity generation.

Administrative Requirements

The Electricity Act, 2024 establishes URCA as the principal electricity-sector regulator.

Section 22 of the current framework requires persons entering regulated electricity-sector activities to obtain the applicable authorization.

  • Determine whether the proposed self-generation capacity exceeds the 1 MW licensing threshold.
  • Apply to URCA for a Generation Licence where required.
  • Provide technical and corporate information required by URCA.
  • Comply with licence conditions where a licence is issued.
  • Verify planning, construction and environmental requirements.
  • Verify electrical safety and engineering requirements.
Off-grid Self-consumption

The project should be classified explicitly as self-generation for internal consumption.

The physical absence of grid import, grid export and electricity sales should be documented in the engineering and regulatory submission.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain permanent electrical isolation from the public grid.
  • No electricity export.
  • No electricity sales to third parties.
  • No participation in electricity markets.
  • Precisely document the installed generation capacity.
  • Obtain written confirmation from URCA concerning the applicable licensing category.
  • Verify all construction, environmental and electrical safety requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
The Bahamas — self-generation licensing threshold:

1 MW

URCA states that entities whose self-generating capacity exceeds 1 MW are subject to the Generation Licence requirement under the Electricity Act, 2024.

This 1 MW figure is a regulatory licensing threshold. It should not be described as a universal maximum permitted generation capacity.

A project above 1 MW can therefore potentially be developed subject to the applicable Generation Licence and its conditions.
