Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Guatemala and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The installation is designed to operate independently from the public electricity network.

No electricity would be imported from or exported to the public grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for the operator's own household, industrial, commercial, scientific or corporate requirements.

Electricity generation is principally regulated by the Ley General de Electricidad, Decreto No. 93-96 , and its implementing regulations.

The law expressly establishes that electricity generation is free and does not require prior government authorization, subject to the Constitution and other applicable laws.

Article 8 further provides that the installation of generating plants is free. An authorization from the Ministry is required where public-domain property is used and the generating plant exceeds 5 MW.

Administrative Requirements

Article 1 of the General Electricity Law establishes the principle that electricity generation is free.

Article 8 provides that generating plants may be installed freely without authorization from a governmental entity, subject to environmental, public-safety and property protections.

Public-Domain Property

Where a generating plant requires the use of State public-domain property, authorization from the Ministry is required when the plant's capacity exceeds 5 MW.

The authorization allows the holder to use public-domain property for the authorized works.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Operate the facility exclusively for internal self-consumption.
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Avoid electricity sales to third parties.
  • Avoid use of public-domain property where practicable.
  • If public-domain property is required, verify whether the 5 MW authorization threshold is applicable.
  • Obtain environmental approval and other applicable permits.
  • Maintain complete engineering documentation specifying installed capacity.
Maximum Electrical Power
Guatemala — statutory threshold:

5 MW

The 5 MW threshold applies specifically where public-domain property is to be used for the generating plant.

The 5 MW figure is not a general maximum generation capacity in Guatemala.

Article 8 expressly establishes freedom to install generating plants. The authorization requirement is connected to the use of State public-domain property and the capacity threshold specified by the law.

A private, off-grid generating installation on privately controlled property may therefore fall outside the electricity-law authorization regime, subject to the Constitution, environmental law, construction law and other applicable legislation.

Public Information Notice

Guatemala's General Electricity Law, Decree No. 93-96, establishes electricity generation as a free activity that does not generally require prior government authorization. Article 8 provides that generating plants may be installed freely, subject to environmental and public-safety requirements. Where State public-domain property is required and the generating plant exceeds 5 MW, authorization from the Ministry is required. The 5 MW figure is therefore not a national maximum generating capacity but a threshold connected to the use of public-domain property.
