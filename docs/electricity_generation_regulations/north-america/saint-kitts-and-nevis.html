Overview

Electricity generation in Saint Kitts and Nevis is principally regulated by the Saint Christopher Electricity Supply Act 2011, together with the applicable amendments, regulations and public-utilities legislation.

The Act regulates the generation, transmission, distribution and supply of electricity and provides mechanisms for both public and private electricity supply licences.

A project intended exclusively for private self-consumption must be distinguished from a project supplying electricity to other persons or to the public.

Section 3(3)(c) of the Saint Christopher Electricity Supply Act 2011 allows the Minister to determine that a licence is not required for electricity equipment or electricity of such a limited nature and used solely to supply the person's own premises that a licence is not considered necessary.

The Act therefore does not establish a general electricity-generation MW threshold below which every private self-generation facility is automatically exempt. The possible exemption is dependent on the circumstances and determination provided by the Act.

Administrative Requirements

Section 3 of the Saint Christopher Electricity Supply Act establishes the general control of electricity supply.

Section 5 authorises the Minister to grant licences giving a company or person the right to supply, generate, transmit or distribute electricity for public or private purposes.

A private electricity project may therefore fall within the private supplier licensing framework.

However, section 3(3)(c) expressly permits the Minister to determine that a licence is unnecessary where the electricity installation is of a limited nature and is solely intended to supply the person's own premises.

Renewable Self-generation

Section 3(2) specifically refers to private electrical plants powered only by wind and photovoltaic plants used solely to supply the person's own premises.

The legislation therefore expressly recognises private self-generation installations.

The specific statutory treatment of a gravity-powered installation must nevertheless be confirmed with the competent authority because the statutory wording does not expressly name gravity-generation technology.

  • Determine whether the project requires a private supplier licence.
  • If relying on section 3(3)(c), obtain confirmation from the competent Minister that the installation qualifies for the licence determination.
  • Ensure that the installation remains physically isolated from the public electricity network.
  • Ensure compliance with electrical safety and inspection requirements.
  • Verify planning, construction and environmental requirements separately.
  • Prevent unauthorised access to or energisation of the public electricity system.
Maximum Electrical Power
Statutory electricity-generation threshold:

No general MW threshold identified for private self-consumption.

The Saint Christopher Electricity Supply Act 2011 does not establish a general numerical MW threshold in section 3(3)(c).

Instead, the Minister may determine that a licence is unnecessary where the installation is of a sufficiently limited nature and is used solely for the person's own premises.

Consequently, the absence of a numerical threshold should not be interpreted as an automatic exemption for a large generating facility.

Website classification:

Private off-grid generation may potentially qualify for a licence exemption determination where it is exclusively for the owner's premises and is considered sufficiently limited under section 3(3)(c).

Public Information Notice

Saint Kitts and Nevis regulates electricity generation principally through the Saint Christopher Electricity Supply Act 2011. Private self-generation is recognised by the legislation. Section 3(3)(c) permits the Minister to determine that a licence is unnecessary for an installation of a limited nature used solely to supply the person's own premises. The Act does not establish a general numerical MW threshold for this determination. A proposed gravity-powered off-grid facility should therefore obtain a project-specific determination from the competent authority before construction or operation.
