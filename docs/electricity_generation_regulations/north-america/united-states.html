Overview

Electricity generation in the United States is regulated through a combination of federal, state, local and, in some circumstances, tribal law.

There is therefore no single United States federal electricity-generation licence or universal MW threshold applicable to every private electricity-generating facility.

The principal federal electricity statute is the Federal Power Act (FPA).

Federal regulation under the FPA principally concerns interstate transmission, wholesale sales of electric energy and certain categories of hydroelectric generation.

A completely isolated installation that generates electricity exclusively for the owner's internal consumption and does not sell electricity to third parties may therefore have a substantially different federal regulatory profile from a generator selling electricity in interstate wholesale markets.

State and local requirements may nevertheless apply, including construction, electrical, environmental, land-use, fire-safety and generator-specific requirements.

Administrative Requirements

The Federal Power Act, particularly 16 U.S.C. § 824, establishes federal regulation of interstate transmission and wholesale sales of electric energy.

Federal jurisdiction does not replace the authority of the states over many intrastate electricity matters.

Hydroelectric Generation

Hydroelectric facilities may be subject to FERC licensing under Part I of the Federal Power Act when they satisfy the statutory jurisdictional criteria.

FERC identifies jurisdictional circumstances including projects located on navigable waters, projects occupying federal lands or reservations, projects using surplus water or waterpower from a federal dam, and certain projects affecting interstate or foreign commerce.

Certain hydroelectric projects may qualify for exemptions from licensing, including specified small hydroelectric projects.

These provisions are technology-specific and do not create a general licensing threshold for non-hydroelectric private generation.

State and Local Regulation

States retain substantial regulatory authority over intrastate electricity activities and electrical infrastructure.

Depending on the state, a private generating facility may require permits or approvals relating to construction, electrical installations, environmental protection, land use, fire safety, emissions or other matters.

The exact requirements cannot be determined from federal law alone without identifying the state and project characteristics.

  • Identify the U.S. state in which the project will be located.
  • Determine whether the generator will ever connect to an interstate or public electricity network.
  • Confirm that no wholesale electricity sales will occur.
  • Determine whether the technology falls within a federally regulated category such as hydroelectric generation.
  • Obtain applicable state and local construction and electrical permits.
  • Verify environmental requirements.
  • Verify fire-safety and occupational-safety requirements.
Maximum Electrical Power
United States — general statutory MW threshold:

No single federal MW threshold applies to all private electricity generation.

Federal law does not establish a universal capacity threshold such as 500 kW or 1 MW that automatically determines whether a private off-grid electricity generator may operate.

Different federal regimes use different jurisdictional criteria.

For example, FERC regulates qualifying non-federal hydroelectric projects based principally on statutory jurisdictional criteria rather than a universal electricity-generation threshold.

Certain small hydroelectric projects may qualify for specific exemptions. FERC identifies a category of small hydroelectric projects of 10 MW or less that can qualify for an exemption under specified conditions.

Website classification:

For a completely off-grid, non-hydroelectric, self-consumption facility: federal electricity licensing must be assessed according to the applicable federal statute and state/local law; there is no universal federal MW threshold.

Public Information Notice

The United States does not have a single national electricity-generation licensing threshold applicable to every private generating installation. Federal regulation under the Federal Power Act focuses on specified categories including interstate transmission, wholesale electricity sales and qualifying hydroelectric projects. A completely isolated private facility used exclusively for internal consumption may therefore have limited federal electricity-market regulation, but state and local construction, electrical, environmental, land-use, fire-safety and other requirements may still apply. The precise legal position must be determined from the state, technology, capacity, location and operating configuration of the proposed project.
