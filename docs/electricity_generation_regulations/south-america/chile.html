Overview

Electricity generation in Chile is principally regulated by the General Electricity Services Law, consolidated in DFL No. 4/20018.

The current consolidated legislation regulates electricity production, transmission, distribution, concessions and State functions.

Chilean legislation expressly distinguishes the national interconnected system, medium systems and isolated systems for small consumers.

A completely isolated installation with no physical connection to the national electricity system is therefore legally distinguishable from a grid-connected distributed-generation installation.

The proposed facility is intended exclusively for internal self-consumption and will have no grid import or export.

Administrative Requirements

DFL No. 4/20018 establishes the general legal framework for electricity generation, transmission, distribution and concessions.

The concession regime is particularly relevant to specified hydraulic generation facilities, transmission infrastructure and public distribution services.

Isolated Systems

Article 10-3 defines an isolated system for small consumers as a system with installed generating capacity less than or equal to 1,500 kW, disconnected from the national electricity system and primarily intended to supply domestic or commercial activities.

Article 10-2 defines a medium system as one with installed generation capacity exceeding 1,500 kW that is disconnected from the national system and supplies regulated or free customers.

A private industrial self-consumption facility may require a more specific classification depending on its purpose, ownership and whether it supplies third parties.

Other Potential Requirements
  • Electrical safety requirements administered by SEC.
  • Declaration and commissioning requirements applicable to the electrical installation.

  • Technical standards applicable to isolated systems.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete electrical isolation from the national electricity system.
  • Do not import electricity from the public network.
  • Do not export electricity to the public network.
  • Do not supply electricity to third parties.
  • Clearly document the system as an isolated self-consumption facility.
  • Determine whether the system falls within the ≤ 1,500 kW isolated-small-consumer category.
  • Comply with SEC electrical safety and commissioning requirements.
  • Obtain applicable environmental and construction approvals.
Maximum Electrical Power
Chile — statutory isolated-system threshold:

1,500 kW

Article 10-3 defines the isolated system for small consumers as a system with installed generation capacity less than or equal to 1,500 kW.

This 1,500 kW value is a statutory classification threshold for an isolated electricity system for small consumers.

It should not automatically be interpreted as a universal maximum generating capacity for every private off-grid industrial installation.

Projects exceeding 1,500 kW may fall into the statutory medium-system framework or another applicable electricity category.

Public Information Notice

Chilean electricity legislation expressly recognizes isolated electricity systems. Under Article 10-3 of the current DFL No. 4/20018, an isolated system for small consumers has installed generation capacity less than or equal to 1,500 kW and is disconnected from the national electricity system. A project above this level may fall into the medium-system framework or another regulatory category. Electrical safety, environmental, construction and other applicable requirements remain relevant regardless of grid connection status.
