Overview

Paraguay regulates independent electricity production principally through Law No. 3009/2006, concerning Independent Production and Transportation of Electrical Energy (PTIEE), as subsequently amended.

Law No. 3009 expressly includes cogeneration and autogeneration within its scope.

The law defines an Autogenerador as an independent producer generating electricity as a secondary product whose principal purpose is the production of goods or services and which generally operates isolated from the National Interconnected System (SIN).

Article 21 establishes the legal regime for autogeneration and provides for an autogeneration licence for electricity intended to satisfy the applicant's own needs.

The Paraguayan framework therefore expressly recognizes isolated self-generation as a regulated category.

Administrative Requirements

Article 2 of Law No. 3009 establishes the regulatory framework for independent production and transportation, including cogeneration and autogeneration.

Article 3 defines the licence as the administrative act granting the right to carry out electricity generation or transportation under the law.

Article 21 specifically provides for autogeneration licences for electricity intended to satisfy the applicant's own requirements.

  • Determine the applicable PTIEE licence category.
  • Register and obtain the applicable authorization before construction or operation.
  • Demonstrate the technical and financial capability required by the applicable licensing procedure.
  • Verify environmental approvals.
  • Verify construction and electrical-safety requirements.
Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain the facility in isolated operation.
  • Use generated electricity exclusively for the operator's own requirements.
  • Avoid supply to unrelated third parties.
  • Avoid connection to the SIN unless specifically required and authorized.
  • Obtain the applicable autogeneration licence.
  • Verify environmental and construction approvals.
Maximum Electrical Power
No general gravity-specific capacity threshold identified.

Law No. 3009 establishes a specific regulatory regime for independent production and autogeneration, but does not establish a general MW ceiling specifically for gravity-powered generation.

The law does, however, contain technology-related licensing provisions. In particular, Article 5 of the PTIEE framework addresses non-conventional generation and certain hydroelectric generation categories.

The capacity and technology of the proposed project must therefore be submitted for classification under the applicable current PTIEE regime.
