Overview

Electricity generation in Argentina is principally regulated at the federal level by Law No. 15,336 and Law No. 24,065, together with their implementing regulations.

Law No. 24,065 characterizes electricity transmission and distribution as public services. Generation is regulated according to the circumstances affecting the general interest.

Article 5 of Law No. 24,065 defines a generator for the national electricity market as an operator whose production is placed totally or partially into a nationally regulated transmission or distribution system.

The proposed installation is different from this market-generation model because it is intended to operate as a completely isolated electrical system.

Electricity would be generated exclusively for internal consumption, with no physical connection to a public electricity network, no electricity import and no electricity export.

The principal legal issue is therefore whether the particular technology, source of energy, location and use of public or private property trigger federal, provincial or municipal authorization requirements.

Administrative Requirements

Law No. 15,336 applies to electricity-industry activities falling within federal jurisdiction, including generation, transformation, transmission and distribution.

Article 14 establishes concession and authorization requirements for certain activities involving generation and electricity infrastructure under national jurisdiction.

Hydroelectric Generation

Article 14 of the current text of Law No. 15,336 requires a concession for exploitation of hydroelectric energy from public watercourses when the normal power granted exceeds 500 kW.

This threshold is specifically associated with the hydroelectric use of public water resources under federal jurisdiction.

It should not be interpreted as a general 500 kW threshold applicable to every private electricity-generation technology.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk
  • Maintain complete physical isolation from the public electricity network.
  • Do not import electricity from the public grid.
  • Do not export electricity to the public grid.
  • Do not sell electricity to third parties.
  • Use the generated electricity exclusively for the operator's own requirements.
  • Prevent accidental electrical interconnection with the public network.
  • Determine whether the project is subject to provincial or municipal electricity regulation.
  • Verify whether public-domain resources are used.
  • Document the exact installed generating capacity.
  • Obtain written confirmation from the competent authority before construction where the legal classification is uncertain.
Maximum Electrical Power
Argentina — statutory capacity thresholds:

500 kW threshold identified for certain hydroelectric concessions involving public watercourses under federal jurisdiction.

The 500 kW threshold in Article 14 of Law No. 15,336 concerns hydroelectric exploitation of public watercourses under federal jurisdiction.

It is not a general electricity-generation ceiling applicable to an isolated private gravity-generation facility.

Law No. 27,424 concerns renewable distributed generation by users of the public distribution network and therefore does not constitute the principal framework for a completely off-grid facility.

The applicable maximum capacity must therefore be determined according to the technology, energy source, location, jurisdiction and use of public resources.

Public Information Notice

Argentina regulates electricity generation through federal electricity legislation together with provincial and municipal rules within their respective jurisdictions. The national framework does not impose one universal capacity threshold applicable to every isolated private generation technology. Article 14 of Law No. 15,336 establishes a 500 kW concession threshold for certain hydroelectric exploitation of public watercourses under federal jurisdiction. A completely isolated self-consumption installation must therefore be assessed according to its technology, capacity, location, energy source and applicable federal, provincial and municipal requirements.
