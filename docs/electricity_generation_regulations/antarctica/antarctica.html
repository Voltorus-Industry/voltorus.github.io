Overview

We are considering a gravity-powered low-carbon electricity-generation facility located in Antarctica and operated exclusively for internal self-consumption.

The intended installation is designed to operate independently from any public electricity network, without importing electricity from or exporting electricity to an external public electricity grid.

Electricity would be used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, research-station or corporate electricity requirements under the control of the operator.

Antarctica is not governed by a conventional national electricity regulatory framework. Activities in the Antarctic Treaty area are principally governed by the Antarctic Treaty System and, in particular, the Protocol on Environmental Protection to the Antarctic Treaty (Madrid Protocol).

The Environmental Protocol designates Antarctica as a natural reserve, devoted to peace and science and establishes comprehensive environmental protection requirements.

The Protocol requires proposed activities in the Antarctic Treaty area to be assessed in advance according to their potential environmental impacts.

The applicable environmental assessment procedure depends on whether the proposed activity is expected to have less than a minor or transitory impact, a minor or transitory impact, or more than a minor or transitory impact.

No Antarctic-wide statutory electricity-generation capacity threshold specifically applicable to gravity-powered generation has been identified. The precise authorization, environmental assessment and operational requirements therefore depend on the activity, location, technology, environmental impact and the national Antarctic programme or jurisdiction under which the activity is conducted.

Administrative Requirements

Antarctica does not have a single national electricity authority comparable to the electricity regulators established by individual sovereign states.

Activities conducted in the Antarctic Treaty area are subject to the Antarctic Treaty System and to the applicable national legislation implementing the obligations of the relevant Antarctic Treaty Party.

The Protocol on Environmental Protection to the Antarctic Treaty requires prior environmental assessment of proposed activities covered by the Protocol.

Under Article 8 of the Environmental Protocol and Annex I , activities are assessed according to their potential environmental impact.

Environmental Impact Assessment

If an activity is determined at the preliminary stage to have less than a minor or transitory impact, it may proceed in accordance with the applicable national procedure.

Where an activity is likely to have a minor or transitory impact, an Initial Environmental Evaluation (IEE) is required.

Where an activity is likely to have more than a minor or transitory impact, a Comprehensive Environmental Evaluation (CEE) is required.

The required environmental assessment must be completed before the activity is undertaken, in accordance with the applicable Antarctic Treaty Party procedures.

Measures to Reduce Licensing Risk

For a project intended to remain a genuinely isolated self-consumption installation, we recommend that:

  • The electricity-generation installation has no physical connection to an external public electricity network.
  • No electricity is imported from an external public electricity grid.
  • No electricity is exported to an external public electricity grid.
  • No electricity is sold to third parties.
  • No electricity is purchased from an external public electricity grid.
  • No participation in electricity trading is undertaken.
  • No participation in electricity balancing or aggregation markets is undertaken.
  • Electricity is used exclusively for internal household, industrial, scientific, research station or corporate self-consumption.
  • The installation operates independently in island mode.
  • Electrical protection prevents accidental connection to or energisation of an external electricity network.
  • The installation is documented as a private self-consumption facility.
  • The nominal electrical capacity is documented precisely.
  • The proposed activity is submitted to the applicable Antarctic environmental assessment procedure before construction or operation.
  • The applicable national Antarctic legislation and authorization requirements are verified before construction.
  • Construction, environmental, protected-area, waste-management, structural, electrical and safety requirements are independently verified.
Maximum Electrical Power
Antarctica — statutory capacity threshold:

No fixed Antarctic-wide MW threshold identified

The Antarctic Treaty System and the Protocol on Environmental Protection do not establish a specific electricity-generation capacity threshold dedicated to gravity-powered electricity-generation facilities.

Unlike national electricity laws that establish licensing thresholds such as 500 kW, 1 MW or 50 MW, the Antarctic Treaty System does not prescribe a specific electrical generating capacity threshold for gravity-powered generation.

The regulatory assessment is principally based on the nature, location, purpose and potential environmental impact of the proposed activity.

Consequently, there is no scientifically or legally justified Antarctic-wide figure such as 500 kW that can accurately be presented as the maximum permitted generating capacity for this technology.

Specific Application to Gravity Technology

Antarctic legislation does not establish a separate statutory capacity threshold specifically named for "gravity-powered electricity generation".

The electrical capacity of the proposed installation must therefore be determined from the engineering design and assessed within the applicable Antarctic environmental and national authorization framework.

The principal regulatory issue is not a gravity-specific MW threshold but whether the proposed activity complies with the Antarctic Treaty System, the Environmental Protocol, the applicable environmental assessment procedure and the legislation of the relevant Antarctic Treaty Party.

For the proposed project, the maximum electrical power should therefore be stated as project-specific and subject to the applicable environmental and national authorization process , rather than assigning an unsupported statutory MW limit.
